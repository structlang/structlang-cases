After the fake dog refund incident ended, I told different people about it.

**To my mom**:

"The dog was refunded."

She: "Oh, good. We're not really suitable for keeping a dog anyway—kids can get allergies."

**To a friend**:

"I refunded that fake dog. The seller refused to budge, so I contacted the regulatory department and finally got my money back."

He: "You were lucky. Usually these things don't get refunded."

**To a colleague (detailed version)**:

"I discovered the seller lacked pet business qualifications, took this to the regulatory department, the official initially stonewalled, but he said 'this whole industry is like that,' revealing a weakness. I then threatened to call the mayor's hotline, the official immediately pressured the seller, and the seller refunded that same day."

He: "Impressive, you really got to the key point."

**Same event, three tellings, three understandings.**

What happened?

## The Inevitable Loss from Experience to Narrative

Recall the complete event process:

- The anger of discovering I'd been deceived
- The frustration of being rejected by the seller the first time
- The excitement of discovering the sign loophole
- The nervousness when meeting the official
- The anxiety when the official stonewalled
- The surprise when he misspoke
- The gambling mindset when threatening the mayor's hotline
- The relief when the seller compromised

And countless details:

- The seller's changing expressions
- The official's office layout
- The weather, time, thoughts on the road
- The exact wording of each conversation
- The hesitation before each key decision

**All this combined forms an extremely complex stream of experience.**

But when I said "the dog was refunded," all of this vanished.

What remained was just a single point: `*refund dog`

This process is **compression**.

---

## Compression Is Inevitable

You might say: But you could tell all the details completely.

Really?

Try it.

Even if I used an entire chapter (Chapter 6) to narrate the entire event in detail, I could only tell:

- Main time nodes (T0-T6)
- Key dialogue content (general meaning, not word-for-word)
- Core decision moments (why I did what I did)

But I cannot tell:

- Every second of psychological activity
- Every exchange of glances
- All environmental details
- Countless "possible but didn't happen" branches

**The reason is simple: cognitive capacity is limited.**

Your brain cannot store all information. You can only store **the compressed version**.

Like taking a photograph:

- The original scene has infinite information (light, temperature, sound, smell)
- The photo only retains visual information
- And even that is compressed (limited pixels, colors)

**A photo is not a copy of reality, but a compression of reality.**

Memory is the same.

## Layers of Compression

Compression isn't one-time, but progressive layers.

### First Layer: Point Structure Is Compression

Returning to Chapter 1, we discussed point structure:

```
Intention: <refund>
Manifestation: *seller
```

What does this point "seller" compress?

- This is a person (age, gender, appearance)
- He has a shop (location, size, decor)
- He has a history (how he opened the shop, what business he's done)
- He has a family (married? children?)
- He has emotions (current mood, facial expression)

All this information, under the "refund" intention, is compressed into a single point: `*seller`

**Point structure itself is the product of compression.**

### Second Layer: Line Structure Is the First Decompression

When I judged the seller:

```
Seller = Dishonest(-0.8)
```

This expands one dimension on the point structure.

But simultaneously, this is also a new compression:

- All the seller's actions
- All the seller's words
- All the seller's details

Were all compressed into a single value: -0.8

### Third Layer: Surface Structure Is the Second Decompression

When I consider two dimensions simultaneously:

```
Seller[-0.8, +0.5](integrity × refund capability)
```

This expands another layer on the line structure.

But it's still compression: only two dimensions, other dimensions are ignored.

### Fourth Layer: Volume Structure Is the Third Decompression

```
Seller(-+-)
```

Three dimensions: Action, Quality, aTtitude.

Richer, but still compression: many dimensions aren't included (like the seller's financial situation, family pressure, industry experience).

**So the entire structural system is a layer-by-layer decompression process:**

```
Point → Line → Surface → Volume → Subject-Object
```

But each layer is still compressed.

**You can never fully decompress to the original experience.**

## Decompression Is Subjective

Suppose I only told you: "The dog was refunded."

How would you understand this sentence?

It depends on **your intention and experience**.

### If You're My Mom

She cares about family life, her intention is: `<family members' safety and comfort>`

When she hears "refunded the dog," her decompression path:

```
*refund dog[family intention]
↓ Decompression
Refund dog = No longer keeping a pet(+0.6)[family safety]
↓ Further decompression
Conclusion: Good thing, avoids kid allergies
```

She doesn't care at all how I refunded it or why it succeeded.

### If You're My Friend

He cares about social experience, his intention is: `<understanding rights protection cases>`

When he hears "contacted regulatory department and got refunded," his decompression path:

```
*refund dog[rights protection intention]
↓ Decompression
Refund dog = Rights protection success(+0.7)[lucky]
↓ Further decompression
Conclusion: Pretty lucky, usually can't get refunded
```

He noticed the process, but decompressed it as "luck," not "strategy."

### If You're My Colleague

He cares about methodology, his intention is: `<learning handling techniques>`

When he hears "got to the key point," his decompression path:

```
*refund dog[learning intention]
↓ Decompression
Refund dog = Strategy success(+0.9)[proper method]
↓ Further decompression
Conclusion: Got to the key point, worth learning
```

He understood the strategic level, but might not understand the structural level.

**Same sentence, three completely different decompressions.**

---

## Why Different Decompressions?

Because decompression isn't "restoring original information," but **reconstruction based on current intention and existing experience**.

**Intention determines decompression direction**:

- My mom's intention is family life → decompress as "pet-keeping risks"
- Friend's intention is understanding cases → decompress as "difficulty of rights protection"
- Colleague's intention is learning methods → decompress as "strategy effectiveness"

**Experience determines decompression content**:

- My mom has experience with pet allergies → decompress as "not keeping pets is safer"
- Friend has experience of failed rights protection → decompress as "getting a refund is lucky"
- Colleague has experience analyzing problems → decompress as "key points are important"

**So decompression is not objective restoration, but subjective creation.**

## Multi-Agent Compression Chains

Now the situation becomes more complex.

Suppose my friend tells his friend about my experience:

"A friend of mine got a fake dog refunded, contacted the regulatory department, and succeeded in the end."

His friend might respond: "Oh, regulatory departments are useful."

**Look what happened**:

```
Original experience (me)
  ↓ Compression
"Contacted regulatory department and refunded dog" (me to friend)
  ↓ Friend's decompression (based on his intention and experience)
"Rights protection can contact regulatory" (friend's understanding)
  ↓ Friend's re-compression
"Friend refunded fake dog through regulatory" (friend to his friend)
  ↓ His friend's decompression
"Regulatory departments are useful" (his friend's conclusion)
```

**This is a chain of compression-decompression-re-compression-re-decompression.**

Each transmission involves:

1. Previous person compresses experience into narrative
2. Next person decompresses narrative into understanding
3. Understanding ≠ original experience
4. When compressing again, more information is lost

**After passing through three hands, it may be completely unrecognizable.**

## History Is a Story from One Perspective

Now ask a question: What's the "truth" of the fake dog refund incident?

**From my perspective**:

A complete structural game. I anchored the legal origin, found evidence, caught the other side's mistakes, introduced external threats, guided the leap, and ultimately succeeded.

**From the seller's perspective**:

An unexpected trouble. Could have dragged it out, but the customer was particularly persistent, the regulatory department suddenly got serious, had to give in.

**From the official's perspective**:

A complaint that almost backfired. Fortunately handled in time, avoided accountability from superiors.

**From my mom's perspective**:

Refunded a dog, that's good.

**Which is the "truth"?**

**All of them, and none of them.**

Because:

1. **There's no uncompressed experience**
    
    - Even I myself can only remember the compressed version
    - I cannot completely reproduce every second of psychological activity
2. **Each perspective is real**
    
    - I indeed experienced the game process
    - The seller indeed felt it was unexpected
    - The official indeed felt it was trouble
    - My mom indeed only cared about the result
3. **Each perspective is partial**
    
    - I don't know the seller's true inner thoughts
    - The seller doesn't know my complete strategy
    - The official doesn't know if I would really call
    - My mom doesn't know any details

**So "historical truth" is essentially a false proposition.**

History is merely **the compression-decompression product from a certain perspective**.

## Narrative Control: Who Dominates Compression?

Since decompression is subjective, **compression can also be manipulated**.

### I Can Choose How to Tell It

Same event, I can tell it like this:

**Version 1** (emphasizing luck):

"I was lucky—the seller happened to lack qualifications, the official happened to misspeak, I happened to catch it."

**Version 2** (emphasizing strategy):

"I carefully observed and found the loophole, caught the moment he misspoke, introduced external threats, guided the other side to leap."

**Version 3** (emphasizing justice):

"I persisted in protecting my rights, exposed the seller's illegal operation, pushed regulatory intervention, and ultimately got justice."

**Version 4** (emphasizing difficulty):

"I made several trips, they kept stonewalling, I almost gave up, fortunately I persisted."

**Which is true?**

All are true. But different emphases guide listeners to different decompressions.

- Version 1 → Listener decompresses as "luck"
- Version 2 → Listener decompresses as "wisdom"
- Version 3 → Listener decompresses as "justice"
- Version 4 → Listener decompresses as "persistence"

**This is narrative control: guiding others' decompression direction through selective compression.**

### The Seller Can Also Tell His Version

If the seller tells this story:

"A customer was particularly difficult, came to the shop to make trouble every day, I didn't want trouble so I refunded."

His friend might decompress: "Sigh, customers are too hard to please these days."

**Same event, in the seller's narrative, I transformed from "rights protector" to "difficult customer".**

## The News Report Compression Trap

Suppose this event made the news.

**News headline**:

"Consumer Discovers Seller Lacks Qualifications, Regulatory Intervention Leads to Successful Refund"

**News content**:

"Citizen Mr. Han purchased a pet dog and discovered the seller lacked business qualifications, complained to the regulatory department, and successfully got a refund under regulatory intervention. The relevant official stated they will strengthen supervision of the pet market."

**Look what this report compressed away**:

- My strategy process (sign loophole, misspeak, mayor's hotline threat)
- The official's stonewalling (he initially didn't want to handle it)
- The seller's stubborn resistance (he initially refused to refund)
- The multiple game process (time windows, leaps)

**What does the news present?**

A simplified linear narrative:

```
Discover problem → Complain → Regulatory intervention → Resolution
```

Is this narrative "true"? From the result, yes.

But it lost **the complexity of the process**.

Readers might decompress after reading: "Oh, complaining solves it."

But actually, complaining doesn't necessarily solve it. It solved because I had evidence, caught mistakes, introduced threats.

**News compression creates a "simplified causal chain".**

## Why Understand Compression and Decompression?

Because:

**1. You can understand the source of misunderstanding**

Communication difficulties aren't because the other side deliberately misinterprets, but because:

- Your compressed information ≠ the other side's decompressed understanding
- Your intentions and experiences differ
- Leading to different decompression directions

**2. You can identify narrative manipulation**

News, advertising, propaganda—all guide your decompression through selective compression.

Once you realize this, you can ask:

- What did this narrative compress away?
- From another perspective, what would the story be?
- Am I accepting a compressed version, or thinking independently?

**3. You can communicate better**

Knowing the compression-decompression mechanism, you know:

- Don't assume the other side can "automatically understand" your meaning
- Choose appropriate compression methods based on the other side's intention and experience
- Actively clarify, confirm whether the other side's decompression matches your intention

**4. You can understand "there's no absolute truth"**

Not nihilism, but understanding:

- Truth is perspective-dependent
- Each perspective has its reality
- But each perspective is also partial
