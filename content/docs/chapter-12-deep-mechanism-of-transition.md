## Precise Definition of Leap

In Chapter 7, we said leap is "sudden change of structure."

Now we need to be more precise:

**Leap = Moment of subjective view change = Moment of establishing new structure and giving it higher weight**

Let's unpack this definition.

### Three Key Elements

**Element 1: Subjectivity**

Leap occurs at the **subjective cognition** level, not physical changes in the objective world.

```
Objective level:
- Official said something (physical sound)
- This was recorded (physical fact)

Subjective level (my cognition):
- My judgment of the official leaped from "bystander" to "implicated party"
- This is structural reorganization in my mind

Leap occurs at: subjective level
```

Same event, in different subjects' eyes, leap can be completely different:

- In my eyes: Official's slip → leaped to "implicated party"
- In official's eyes: Might not think he said anything wrong
- In observer's eyes: Just an ordinary statement

**Element 2: Instantaneity**

Leap is **completed instantly**, not "slowly changing from 8am to 3pm."

```
Not:
Seller's attitude gradually softens (this is a trend)

But:
Moment after official hung up, seller instantly decided to refund (this is leap)
```

This "instant" isn't infinitely small in physical time, but the **moment of cognitive decision**—the moment of "I decide to believe X" or "I no longer believe Y."

**Element 3: Weight Change**

New structure is given **higher weight**, overwhelming the old structure.

```
Official's judgment of me:

Old structure:
"This person is filing a complaint"
Weight: 0.4 (medium attention)

New structure (after hearing "mayor's hotline"):
"This person might really complain to superiors"
Weight: 0.95 (extreme attention)

New weight > Old weight → Leap completed
```

Not old and new coexisting in hesitation, but **new structure overwhelming old structure**, becoming dominant.

## Leap vs. Trend: Quantitative Change vs. Qualitative Change

In Chapter 7 we distinguished leap from trend. Now we understand their relationship more deeply.

### A Physical Analogy: Water Boiling

Imagine you're boiling a pot of water:

```
1°C → 20°C → 50°C → 80°C → 99°C
This is continuous change (temperature trend)

99°C → 100°C
Water suddenly boils (state leap)
```

- From 1°C to 99°C: Water is still water, just getting hotter (quantitative change)
- At 100°C: Water becomes steam, properties change (qualitative change)

**Key**: Quantitative change accumulates to critical point, suddenly becomes qualitative change.

### Quantitative and Qualitative Change in Fake Dog Refund

**T0-T3: Quantitative Change Accumulation**

```
My actions:
- First time finding seller (A+)
- Second time finding seller (A+)
- Finding sign evidence (A+)
- Finding official (A+)

These are all trends: My determination accumulating, evidence increasing

But structure hasn't leaped yet:
- Seller still (-+-)
- Official still (-+-)
```

**T3': Critical Point**

```
Official's slip: "This whole industry is like that"

This statement itself isn't long or complex
But it triggered the critical point:
- Exposed his knowledge
- My judgment from "he doesn't know" to "he knows but doesn't care"
```

**T4: Qualitative Change (Leap)**

```
I said "mayor's hotline"

Official: (-+-) ⇒ (+++)
Instantly from stonewalling to active

This isn't him slowly becoming active
But at that instant, his priorities completely reordered
```

### Acknowledge Continuity, But Analyze with Dichotomy

In reality, the speed of cognitive change is a **continuous spectrum**:

```
Extremely slow change ←————————————→ Instantaneous change
(Years)                                (Instant)
Quantitative change                     Qualitative change
```

But why do we use the dichotomy of "leap vs. trend"?

**Because we focus on subjective experience**:

- When you **become aware** change happened, it's already a leap
- Even if objectively there's an accumulation process
- But in subjective cognition, it's compressed into "sudden change at some moment"

**This itself is manifestation of cognitive compression!**

## Why Is Leaping Back So Hard?

After leap occurs, can it return to original state?

Suppose seller refunded, but later I discover the dog is actually purebred, I was wrong. Can I return to the judgment "seller is honest"?

**Answer: Very hard, even if returning it's not the original state.**

### Two Types of Irreversibility

**Type 1: Strong Irreversibility**

Needs new strong evidence to reverse.

```
Example of trust collapse:

I discover friend betrayed me
Trust: +0.8 ⇒ -0.8 (leap)

To restore to +0.8:
- Needs new strong evidence (not automatic recovery)
- Needs long-term rebuilding
- Needs behavior "proving trustworthiness"

Key: Won't "automatically" revert
```

In fake dog case:

```
Even if later discover dog is purebred
Seller's judgment in my mind cannot automatically return to "honest"

Because:
- "Once refused refund stubbornly" this fact already happened
- My judgment structure of him already rewritten
- Needs stronger evidence to rewrite again
```

**Type 2: Path Dependence**

Can return to similar state, but not the original state.

```
Example of couple breaking up then getting back together:

"Romance" before breakup:
- Based on naive trust of first love
- Haven't experienced betrayal

"Romance" after getting back together:
- Based on maturity and caution after experiencing breakup
- Know relationship can break
- Seemingly returning to "romance," actually different

Key: History left traces
```

**Irreversibility of cognitive leap**:

Although can change back, but:

1. Needs new leap (not automatic revert)
2. New state ≠ old state (carries experience)

## Unified Essence of Trigger Factors

Chapter 7 covered four types of trigger factors: information update, object change, external intervention, internal accumulation.

Now we reveal their **unified essence**:

**Effect of all trigger factors = Creating a new compression point in subjective cognition, or significantly changing the weight of existing compression point**

Let's revisit the four types of trigger factors.

### Trigger Factor 1: Information Update

```
Mechanism: Introduce new dimension → Old compression framework cannot contain

Fake dog case:
Old framework: Moral dimension (seller dishonest)
New information: Lacks business license (legal dimension)

Why trigger leap?
Because the compression point "dishonest" cannot contain the information "illegal"
"Dishonest" is moral judgment, medium weight
"Illegal" is legal judgment, extremely high weight

Old compression point fails → Must establish new compression point
Seller = lawbreaker (weight 1.0)

Essence: New information creates new, higher-weight compression point
```

### Trigger Factor 2: Object Change

```
Mechanism: Manifestation of compressed object changes → Compression point must be rewritten

Fake dog case:
Object: Official
Old compression: Bystander (inaction but unknowing, weight 0.4)

State change: Slip "this whole industry is like that"
New manifestation: He knew!

Why trigger leap?
Because key premise of compression point "bystander" is "unknowing"
Once "knowing" exposed, entire compression point collapses

Must rewrite: Official = knowing derelict (weight 0.9)

Essence: Object's core attributes change, compression point redefined
```

### Trigger Factor 3: External Intervention

```
Mechanism: High-weight external structure enters → Reorders all weights

Fake dog case:
External structure: Mayor's hotline
Weight: 1.0 (absolute authority)

Effect:
Official's judgment weight of me: 0.4 ⇒ 0.95
Why?
Because in his structure:
- Ordinary complaint: weight 0.4 (can be stonewalled)
- Superior accountability: weight 0.95 (must handle immediately)

Mayor's hotline = trigger for superior accountability

Essence: External authority structure forcibly changes weight distribution in cognition
```

### Trigger Factor 4: Internal Accumulation

```
Mechanism: Experience and beliefs suddenly drive decision at some moment

Fake dog case:
Why could I maintain (+++) throughout?

Internal accumulation:
- Legal origin anchoring (weight 1.0)
- Belief that "deadlock always breaks" (experience compression)

These weren't formed instantly
But suddenly took effect at key moments:
- Deciding to find official
- Deciding to threaten mayor's hotline
- Deciding not to give up

Essence: Long-accumulated compression points activated at key moment
```

## Tension: Driving Force of Leap

Leap doesn't happen out of thin air. Before leap, **tension** is always accumulating.

### What Is Tension?

**Tension = Gap between current structure and intention**

Imagine you're pulling a rubber band:

```
Stage 1: Start pulling
Rubber band gradually lengthens
Tension starts accumulating↑
Structure can still maintain

Stage 2: Continue pulling
Rubber band very taut
Tension approaching limit↑↑
Structure about to fail

Stage 3: Snap!
Rubber band breaks
Tension releases
Structure leaps
```

### Tension in Fake Dog Case

```
My intention: Get back deceived money (always clear)

Current structure (T0-T3):
Seller refuses refund (-+-)
Official stonewalls (-+-)

Tension = Intention (want money back) - Reality (can't get money back)
Tension value: From 0.2 → 0.5 → 0.7 → 0.9 (gradually rising)

T0: Just discovered deception, tension 0.2 (still acceptable)
T1: Seller refuses, tension 0.5 (starting to be anxious)
T2: Found evidence, tension 0.7 (know there's hope but not realized yet)
T3: Official stonewalls, tension 0.9 (near explosion)
T4: Mayor's hotline threat, tension releases, leap occurs
```

**Tension accumulation curve**:

```
Tension
 ↑
 │              
 │            ╱ @T3-T4 (explosion)
 │          ╱
 │        ╱ @T2-T3 (acceleration)
 │      ╱
 │    ╱ @T1-T2 (slow)
 │  ╱
 ├────────────→ Time
T0           T4
```

**Key insight**:

- Before leap, tension is always accumulating
- Greater tension, closer to critical point
- Once exceeding critical point, leap inevitably occurs

## Critical Point: How to Identify Imminent Leap?

Since leap has tension accumulation process, can we identify critical point in advance?

**Answer: Partially yes, partially no.**

### Identifiable Signals

**Signal 1: Subtle Changes in Other Party**

```
Between T3 and T4:
Seller's resistance starts showing fluctuation
- Tone from "absolutely no refund" to "this is very difficult"
- From "no discussion" to "let me think"

This is reflection of tension on other party's side
Shows he also feels structure about to fail
```

**Signal 2: Your Own Emotional Intensity**

```
If you find:
- Increasingly anxious
- Increasingly volatile emotions
- Constantly thinking about this matter

Shows your subjective tension is high
May be approaching leap critical point
```

**Signal 3: Deadlock Duration**

```
Empirical rule:
Longer deadlock time → Greater tension → Closer to leap

T0-T1: 1 day, still okay
T1-T3: Cumulative 3 days, starting to destabilize
T3-T4: If delays further, must explode
```

### Unpredictable Factors

**Factor 1: When Trigger Factor Appears**

```
I couldn't predict:
- When official would say wrong thing (slip)
- When I would think of "mayor's hotline" strategy

These are contingent factors
```

**Factor 2: Other Party's Real Critical Point**

```
Official's critical point might be:
- Mayor's hotline (actual)
- Could be other threats
- Or might not leap at all

I cannot fully know his tension threshold
```

**Factor 3: External Environment Mutations**

```
If suddenly:
- Seller goes bankrupt
- Official gets transferred
- I encounter other emergencies

Entire game structure suddenly changes
Previous tension accumulation may reset to zero
```

## Predictability Boundaries of Leap

Understanding leap mechanism, what can we do?

### What You Can Do

**1. Identify Current Stage**

```
Early stage: Tension just starting to accumulate
Middle stage: Tension accelerating accumulation
Late stage: Approaching critical point
Explosion: Leap occurs
```

Judging stage helps decision-making:

- Early: Can patiently wait
- Middle: Should start applying pressure
- Late: Prepare to detonate or retreat
- Explosion: Seize timing

**2. Create Trigger Factors**

```
Active behaviors:
- Discovering sign loophole (information update)
- Threatening mayor's hotline (external intervention)
- Persistently not giving up (internal accumulation)

All actively creating leap conditions
```

**3. Assess Tension Level**

```
Observe other party:
- Are they near collapse?
- Is resistance weakening?
- Is tone softening?

Judge: Is now suitable to apply pressure
```

### What You Cannot Do

**1. Precisely Predict Leap Moment**

```
You can only know "it's coming"
But cannot know "37 more minutes"

Because:
- When trigger factor appears is uncertain
- Other party's critical point is opaque
- External interference can happen anytime
```

**2. Control All Trigger Factors**

```
Other party might:
- Suddenly cooperate (unexpected)
- Suddenly become tough (unexpected)
- Suddenly exit (unexpected)

You can only create conditions
Cannot completely control
```

**3. Force Structure Change**

```
If tension hasn't accumulated to position yet
No matter how much you press it won't work

Like rubber band hasn't reached limit yet
You pinching it won't break it
```

**4. Avoid All Uncertainty**

```
Essence of leap is:
Making decisions in uncertainty

You can never completely eliminate uncertainty
Can only act under imperfect information
```

## How Is This Different from Familiar Theories?

You may have heard sayings like "quantitative change leads to qualitative change." How is leap mechanism different?

### Core Differences

**Traditional dialectics: Quantitative→Qualitative**

```
Emphasizes:
- Quantitative accumulation
- Qualitative leap
- Negation of negation

But doesn't answer:
- Why leap at that moment?
- What drives leap?
- How to identify critical point?
- Why hard to revert after leap?
```

**Leap mechanism: Tension→Critical point→Release**

```
Emphasizes:
- Gap between intention and reality (tension)
- Weight change in subjective cognition
- Four types of trigger factors
- Two forms of irreversibility

Provides:
- Operational analysis tools
- Identifiable critical signals
- Designable trigger strategies
```

|Dimension|Traditional Dialectics|Leap Mechanism|
|---|---|---|
|Focus|Objective process|Subjective cognition|
|Driving force|Quantitative accumulation|Tension accumulation|
|Trigger|Naturally occurs|Can actively create|
|Predictability|Philosophical generalization|Partially identifiable|
|Operability|Theoretical explanation|Provides tools|

**Key insight**:

Leap mechanism doesn't negate quantitative-qualitative change, but **provides finer analysis framework at subjective cognition level**—tells you what "quantity" is (tension), where "change" is (critical point), how to "trigger qualitative change" (trigger factors).

## Final Insight

Leap isn't mysterious or elusive.

Leap is the **inevitable phase transition** of cognitive system when tension accumulates to critical point.

Just like water must boil at 100°C—not coincidence, but inevitable manifestation of system characteristics.

**What you can do**:

1. **Identify tension**: Observe gap between intention and reality
2. **Judge stage**: Assess how far from critical point
3. **Create trigger**: Actively design leap conditions
4. **Seize timing**: Detonate or retreat at critical point
5. **Leverage chain**: One leap can trigger next

**What you cannot do**:

1. Precisely predict leap moment
2. Control all trigger factors
3. Force leap not at critical point
4. Completely eliminate uncertainty

**Essence of leap**:

> Under intention drive, when tension in subjective cognition exceeds critical point through trigger factors, cognitive structure instantly reorganizes and assigns higher weight to new structure.

This isn't "mysterious intuition," but cognitive process with mechanism, regularity, analyzable, and operable.
