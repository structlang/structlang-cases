In the fake dog refund incident, there's a question we've never directly answered:

**Why did I generate the intention to "recover the cheated money"?**

You might say: Obviously, if you're cheated you want to recover it.

But is it really "obvious"?

If I'd been cheated of five yuan, would I spend a week protecting my rights?

If I was busy with other important things then, would I immediately activate this intention?

If I'm a "better to avoid trouble" person, would I choose to accept the loss?

**Same situation of being cheated, different people generate different intentions.**

Some people's intention is "forget it, consider it a lesson bought."

Some people's intention is "too infuriating, must get an explanation."

Some people's intention is "handle it quickly, don't let it affect mood."

**Intention isn't automatically generated, but has its sources and mechanisms.**

---

## Three Sources of Intention

### Source 1: Physiological Drive

You're walking down the street and suddenly feel hungry.

At this moment, an intention automatically generates: `<find food>`

This intention isn't something you "decided" to have, but your body sending a signal.

**Characteristics of physiologically driven intention**:

- **Compulsory**: You can't choose not to be hungry
- **High priority**: When hungry enough, other intentions will be suppressed
- **Immediacy**: Feel physiological need, intention immediately generates

**Other examples**:

- Thirsty → `<find water>`
- Tired → `<find place to rest>`
- Cold → `<keep warm>`
- Pain → `<stop pain>`

These intentions come from the body's underlying needs, which you can hardly resist.

---

### Source 2: External Trigger

You're resting at home, your phone rings.

The screen shows: Boss sent a message.

Instantly, an intention generates: `<check message>`

This intention isn't something you actively wanted, but triggered by external stimulus.

**Characteristics of externally triggered intention**:

- **Passivity**: Not something you planned to do
- **Reactivity**: Immediate response to external events
- **Resistibility**: You can choose not to respond (but may have consequences)

**External triggers in the fake dog case**:

```
T0: Discovered dog isn't purebred
→ External event: Visual information (dog's appearance) doesn't match expectation
→ Triggered intention: <confirm if cheated>

After confirming being cheated
→ External fact: Money was cheated
→ Triggered intention: <recover cheated money>
```

**Other examples**:

- See red light → `<stop car>`
- Hear fire alarm → `<escape>`
- Receive bill → `<pay>`
- Friend seeks help → `<help>`

The external world constantly sends you information, each piece may trigger a new intention.

---

### Source 3: Experience Drive

You've worked at a company for three years and seen too many failed project cases.

Now, the leader proposes a new plan.

Before he finishes, you already have an intention: `<find flaws in this plan>`

This intention isn't a physiological need, nor external stimulus, but from your **past experience**.

**Characteristics of experience-driven intention**:

- **Predictive**: Based on past, predicting future
- **Spontaneous**: Generates without external trigger
- **Plasticity**: Changes as experience accumulates

**Experience drive in the fake dog case**:

```
My experience: Legal origin has highest weight
→ Spontaneously generates intention: <anchor legal origin>

My experience: Deadlocks always have weaknesses
→ Spontaneously generates intention: <persistent pressure without giving up>
```

These intentions didn't generate at T0, but are results of my long-term experience accumulation. When I encounter this situation, these intentions **automatically activate**.

**Other examples**:

- People with experience being cheated, seeing unfamiliar phone calls → `<beware scams>`
- People with failed startup experience, seeing new opportunities → `<evaluate risks>`
- People with emotional hurt experience, facing new romance → `<protect self>`

Experience shapes your intention patterns. Same situation, people with different experiences generate completely different intentions.

---

## Interweaving of Three Sources

In reality, intention often isn't from a single source.

**Scenario: Working overtime late at night**

```
Physiological drive: Tired → <go home and sleep>
External trigger: Boss requires overtime → <complete work>
Experience drive: Last time not working overtime was criticized → <avoid criticism>
```

Three intentions exist simultaneously, conflicting with each other.

The final behavior depends on **which intention has higher weight**.

**Multiple sources in the fake dog case**:

```
Physiological: Very angry being cheated (emotion is also a physiological state)
→ <vent this anger>

External: Dog already bought, money already paid
→ <handle this problem>

Experience: Past successful rights protection experience
→ <believe rights protection works>

Three combine → <recover cheated money>
```

If I wasn't angry (say I'm Buddhist-style), if I had no rights protection experience (say first time encountering), the final intention might be completely different.

## Intention Conflict

Intentions aren't singular. Many times, multiple intentions **exist simultaneously**.

### Scenario: Should I Quit

You work at a company and feel very unhappy.

**Intention A: Escape Pain**

```
<Leave this company>
Weight: +0.8 (very strong)
Reason: Work pressure high, colleague relationships poor, see no future
```

**Intention B: Maintain Stability**

```
<Keep this job>
Weight: +0.7 (also very strong)
Reason: Mortgage to pay, parents to support, finding new job has risks
```

**Intention C: Personal Growth**

```
<Find better opportunity>
Weight: +0.6 (medium)
Reason: Should try more when young, can't always stay in comfort zone
```

Three intentions conflict with each other.

You're tangled: Should I quit or not?

**Why tangled?**

Because no single intention's weight is significantly higher than others. They're pulling at each other:

```
Intention A: Leave(+0.8) vs Intention B: Keep(+0.7)
→ Difference only 0.1, insufficient to make decision
```

### Three Outcomes of Intention Conflict

**Outcome 1: One intention's weight rises**

One day, boss scolds you again.

```
Intention A: Leave → +0.8 jumps to +0.95
```

Weight suddenly rises, overwhelming other intentions.

You resign on the spot.

**Outcome 2: One intention's weight drops**

One day, you see news that economy is bad, many people unemployed.

```
Intention B: Keep → +0.7 jumps to +0.9
```

Maintaining stability's intention weight rises, overwhelming the impulse to leave.

You decide to endure more.

**Outcome 3: Intention Reorganization**

One day, you figure it out: Actually the problem isn't "should I leave," but "what do I really want."

Your intention shifts from:

```
A: <leave> vs B: <keep> vs C: <grow>
```

Reorganizes to:

```
New intention: <find what I really want to do>
```

This is the appearance of **Meta-Intention**—intention about intention itself.

## Intention Switching Is Origin Leap

Back to Chapter 2, we discussed origin leap:

```
Old origin: Taste
New origin: Hygiene
```

When eating noodles and biting into hair, origin leaped from "tasty or not" to "clean or not."

**Now we can say: The essence of origin leap is intention switching.**

```
Old intention: <enjoy food>
→ Old origin: Zero point on taste axis

New intention: <avoid health risks>
→ New origin: Zero point on hygiene axis
```

Intention changed, entire judgment framework changed.

**Intention switching in fake dog case**:

```
Before T2:
Intention: <refund>
Origin: Business credibility

After T2 (discovered lack of qualifications):
Intention: <protect rights>
Origin: Legal compliance
```

Intention switched from "commercial refund" to "legal rights protection," origin leaped accordingly.

**Why did this switch happen?**

Because new information (sign loophole) triggered experience-driven intention:

```
My experience: Legal origin has highest weight
New information: Seller violated law
→ Triggered intention switch: From commercial level to legal level
```

## Meta-Intention: Intention About Intention Itself

Sometimes, you generate new intention about your own intention.

### Scenario: Quit Smoking

You want to quit smoking.

```
Intention A: <smoke> (physiological drive)
Weight: +0.9 (very strong)

Intention B: <quit smoking> (experience drive: know smoking is harmful)
Weight: +0.6 (medium)
```

Intention A is stronger, so you still smoked.

**But one day, you generate a new intention:**

```
Meta-intention: <change my intention structure>
```

You realize: The problem isn't "should I smoke this cigarette," but "why can't I quit."

You start analyzing:

- Intention A comes from nicotine dependence (physiological)
- Intention A is reinforced by habit (experience)
- Intention B is just rational cognition, lacking deep drive

**Role of meta-intention: Reconstruct underlying intention**

You start taking action:

- Reduce contact with cigarettes (weaken external trigger)
- Exercise to release pressure (substitute physiological need)
- Build new habits (rewrite experience drive)

You're not "fighting" the smoking intention, but **changing the mechanism that generates this intention**.

### Meta-Intention in Fake Dog Case

Throughout the event, I actually had a hidden meta-intention:

```
Meta-intention: <use this experience to verify StructLang>
```

I didn't just want to refund, I also wanted to see:

- Whether legal origin really has highest weight
- Whether leaps can be guided
- Whether time windows can be utilized

This meta-intention influenced my decisions:

- Why was I so persistent? Partly meta-intention supporting
- Why did I choose these strategies? Partly verifying theory
- Why did I record so detailed? Meta-intention needs complete case

**Meta-intention isn't necessarily conscious.**

Many times, you have meta-intention but don't know it yourself. For example:

- You think you're pursuing success, actually you're pursuing parents' approval (meta-intention: <get parents' approval>)
- You think you're making decisions, actually you're avoiding responsibility (meta-intention: <avoid bearing consequences>)
- You think you're helping others, actually you're proving your worth (meta-intention: <establish self-worth>)

## Temporal Characteristics of Intention

Intention isn't eternal, but has temporal characteristics.

### Instant Intention

```
See red light → <stop>
```

This intention generates fast, disappears fast. Red light turns green, intention immediately disappears.

**Characteristics**:

- Generates fast
- Lasts short
- Weight might be very high (like avoiding danger)

### Delayed Intention

```
Today is Monday → <go see movie on Friday>
```

This intention generates now, but executes on Friday.

**Characteristics**:

- Time gap between generation and execution
- Needs memory to maintain
- May be covered by other intentions during waiting period

### Persistent Intention

```
<recover cheated money>
```

In fake dog case, this intention lasted a week.

**Characteristics**:

- Long-term existence
- Continuously activates related point structures
- Weight may change over time

### Cumulative Intention

```
<become better>
```

This kind of intention isn't generated at one moment, but result of long-term experience accumulation.

**Characteristics**:

- No clear starting point
- Weight gradually strengthens
- May suddenly erupt ("I've had enough, must change")

**Four temporal characteristics in fake dog case**:

```
Instant: See dog is wrong → <confirm breed>
Delayed: After refund → <be more careful buying things in future>
Persistent: T0-T6's <recover cheated money>
Cumulative: Long-term <protect own rights>
```

## Recursiveness of Intention

Finally an interesting phenomenon: **Intentions can nest**.

### Intention Chain

```
<recover cheated money>
  → To achieve this intention, generated:
    <find evidence>
      → To achieve this intention, generated:
        <observe sign>
          → To achieve this intention, generated:
            <go to seller's shop>
```

Each intention may trigger the next intention.

### Intention of Intention of Intention

```
<refund> ← Surface intention
  ↓ Why want to refund?
<recover loss> ← Deeper intention
  ↓ Why care about this loss?
<maintain fairness> ← Deeper intention
  ↓ Why care about fairness?
<prove I'm not easy to bully> ← Deepest intention?
```

You think your intention is A, but digging deep, the real intention might be D.

**This recursiveness is often something you yourself don't realize.**

## Why Understand Intention's Deep Mechanism

Because:

**1. You can understand your own behavior**

Many times, you don't know why you did something.

"I don't know why I'm so angry."

"I don't know why I can't let go."

Understanding intention's sources and mechanisms, you can see: These reactions have their physiological, external, experiential reasons behind them.

**2. You can handle intention conflicts**

When you're tangled, it's not because you're "indecisive," but because multiple intentions are competing.

Understanding this, you can:

- Identify which intentions are in conflict
- Evaluate each intention's weight
- Actively adjust one intention's weight
- Or reorganize intention structure

**3. You can actively shape intentions**

Intentions aren't fixed.

- Physiological drive can be changed through training (athletes can increase pain tolerance)
- External triggers can be adjusted through environment (put phone farther, reduce checking impulse)
- Experience drive can be rewritten through new experience (deliberate practice changes habits)

**4. You can identify others' real intentions**

Seller's surface intention is "don't want to refund," deep intention is "preserve profit."

Official's surface intention is "don't want to handle," deep intention is "avoid trouble."

Understanding intention's layers, you can find the real leverage points:

- For seller: Threaten greater loss (legal risks)
- For official: Threaten greater trouble (superior accountability)

## Complete Picture of Intention, Origin, and Leap

Now we can connect these three concepts:

**Intention → Determines origin → Origin determines judgment → Judgment accumulates to form structure → Structure leaps → Intention switches → New origin**

```
T0: Intention<refund> → Origin:business credibility → Judgment:seller dishonest(-0.8)

T2: New info triggers → Intention switches<protect rights> → Origin:legal compliance → Judgment:seller violated law(-1.0)

T3': Official misspoke → Intention expands<pursue accountability> → Origin:regulatory responsibility → Judgment:official derelict

T4: Mayor's hotline threat → Intention strengthens<avoid accountability> → Origin:superior pressure → Judgment:must handle immediately
```

**The entire event is a process of intention constantly evolving, origin constantly leaping, structure constantly reorganizing.**

## Coordinated Operation of Intention, Causality, Leap, Compression

Now we can look back at the fake dog refund incident and see how these concepts work simultaneously.

### T0: Intention Generation

```
External trigger: Discovered dog wrong
→ Intention generates: <confirm if cheated>
→ Origin anchors: Product authenticity
→ Manifestation: dog*, seller*
```

### T1-T2: Experience-Driven Persistence

```
Intention: <recover cheated money>
→ Invoke causal impression: Rights protection experience
→ Continuous action: Go to shop daily
→ Compression judgment: Seller(-,+,-)
→ Time window: Deadlock stage
```

Why could deadlock maintain?

- **Intention weight high enough** (amount cheated worth it)
- **Causal impression supports** (believe deadlock will break)
- **Compression stable** (judgment of seller unchanged)

### T2: Leap Trigger

```
New information: Sign loophole
→ Intention switches: <refund> becomes <protect rights>
→ Origin leaps: Business credibility→legal compliance
→ Decompression reconstruction: Judgment of seller from "dishonest" to "violated law"
→ Causal impression activates: Legal origin has highest weight
```

Why did leap happen?

- **New information triggers** (external source)
- **Experience drive responds** (legal impression has high weight)
- **Intention automatically switches** (legal>commercial)
- **Compression method changes** (re-evaluate situation)

### T3-T4: Chain Leap

```
Official misspoke
→ Compression failed: Exposed knowing but not handling
→ Intention updates: From <seek help> to <apply pressure>
→ Introduce threat: Official complaint channels
→ Trigger leap: Official's intention from <stonewall> leaps to <avoid accountability>
→ Chain reaction: Seller leaps
```

Why did chain leap happen?

- **One leap creates new trigger factor** (official's attitude change)
- **Compression chain transmits** (official→seller)
- **Time window compresses** (must respond immediately)
- **Intention priority reorders** (avoid accountability>don't want to handle)

### Complete Coordination

```
Intention → Determines origin → Selects manifestation → Forms judgment
↓ Judgment accumulates → Forms causal impression → Quick invocation
↓ New trigger → Intention switches → Origin leaps → Structure reorganizes
↓ Compression transmits → Decompression differs → Misunderstanding/consensus
↓ Cycle repeats
```

**This isn't linear process, but circular system:**

- Intention affects compression, compression affects causal impression
- Causal impression affects leap judgment, leap rewrites causal impression
- Compression produces misunderstanding, misunderstanding triggers intention adjustment

**All concepts operate simultaneously, mutually influencing, constituting a dynamic cognitive system.**

## Summary

Intention is the driving force of the entire system.

**Its sources are three**:

- Physiological drive: Body's needs
- External trigger: World's events
- Experience drive: Past accumulation

**It's not singular**:

- Multiple intentions exist simultaneously
- They conflict with each other
- Weight determines final behavior

**It's not fixed**:

- Intentions switch (origin leaps)
- Intentions nest (recursiveness)
- Intentions reorganize (meta-intention)

**It drives everything**:

- Determines what manifests
- Determines how to compress
- Determines when to leap
- Determines what causal impressions form

**Understanding intention, you can understand:**

- Why there's such thinking
- Why make such decisions
- Why fall into tangles
- How to actively change
