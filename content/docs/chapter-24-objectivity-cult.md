In the previous chapter, we argued: Consensus doesn't need the unverifiable metaphysical assumption of "objective ontology." Science is stable not because it's "closer to objectivity," but because of: high-frequency validation + extensive overlap + precise prediction.

But if consensus stability doesn't depend on "objectivity," why is the concept of "objectivity" so important?

Observe this phenomenon:

- Scientists say "pursuing objective truth"
- Judges say "based on objective facts"
- Media says "objective reporting"
- People say "you're too subjective"

**"Objectivity" is often used to suppress dissenting opinions, establish authority, and create hierarchies.**

What does this resemble?

This chapter will reveal: **The theological structure of objectivity worship**.

## I. The Original State

Let's return to the most elementary starting point.

A baby opens their eyes and sees their mother's face. They don't wonder "Is what I'm seeing real?" They just see.

You're hungry, you eat, you're full. You don't wonder "Is my fullness subjective or objective?" You're just full.

You trip on a stone, fall, and it hurts. You don't wonder "Does this pain conform to objective reality?" It just hurts.

**This is the most direct, most indisputable fact. No intermediary, no room for doubt.**

You are you. What you see is what you see, what you experience becomes your memory, what you feel is what you feel.

Originally, everything was simple.

## II. The Invasion of a Concept

Then, someone tells you:

**"What you see isn't real."**

"What?"

"What you see is just appearance, the true reality is beyond your perception."

"But I clearly saw it."

"That's your subjectivity. You need to pursue objectivity."

**"What is objectivity?"**

"Objectivity is the reality that transcends your subjectivity."

"Then how do I know what's objective?"

"You can never fully know, but you must constantly pursue it."

"How do I pursue it?"

"Through reason, through science, through overcoming your biases."

**"What if my feelings don't match objectivity?"**

"Then you're wrong. You're too subjective."

Stop. Let's carefully examine the structure of this conversation.

Do you feel this conversation is familiar?

**But what's the cost?**

## VII. The Real Function of Objectivity

Let's honestly admit: Introducing the concept of "objectivity" did solve some problems.

### Positive Functions of the Objectivity Concept

**1. Provided Common Reference**

- When two people disagree, can say "let's look at objective facts"
- This gives a temporary coordination solution

**2. Promoted Scientific Collaboration**

- Scientists need a common standard to evaluate theories
- "Objective verification" provides this standard

**3. Established Evaluation Mechanism**

- Need to distinguish "rigorously validated knowledge" from "personal speculation"
- "Conforming to objectivity" becomes a filtering standard

**4. Resisted Relativism**

- Prevents falling into "everyone talks past each other" chaos
- Provides criterion transcending individual opinions

**These are all real functions.**

### But the Problem Is

**For these functions, we paid an unnecessary ontological cost.**

We don't need to assume a "transcendent objective ontology" to achieve the above functions:

|Functional Need|Traditional Solution (Metaphysical)|Alternative Solution (Methodological)|
|---|---|---|
|Common reference|"Conforms to objective ontology"|Stable overlap of multiple subjectivities|
|Scientific collaboration|"Discover objective laws"|Consensus sedimentation from high-frequency validation|
|Evaluation mechanism|"Is it objective"|Repeatability, predictive power, consistency|
|Resist relativism|"Objective truth exists"|Acknowledge different stabilities and weights of consensus|

**In other words**:

❌ Don't need: "An objective ontology independent of cognition exists" (metaphysical assumption)  
✅ Only need: "Establish operational reliability standards" (methodological tool)

## X. Resistance of Hard Reality

Someone might ask:

**"If there's no objective ontology, how do you explain the 'resistance' of 'hard reality'?"**

"I subjectively think I can fly, but if I jump I'll still fall. What's 'resisting' my subjectivity? Isn't it the objective world?"

This is an important question. But the premise of the question itself is wrong.

### "Falling" Isn't Hard Constraint, It's Conditionally Dependent

Let's carefully dissect the seemingly "absolutely hard" constraint of "jumping will cause falling."

**For "jumping from building will cause falling" to hold, needs to satisfy all these conditions**:

|Condition Type|Specific Conditions|
|---|---|
|**Physical conditions**|Earth's gravity field (9.8m/s²)<br>Sufficient height<br>No cushioning|
|**Biological conditions**|Living body<br>No special training<br>Limited bone strength|
|**Cognitive conditions**|Height perception ability<br>Injury expectation<br>Understanding "falling" meaning|
|**Intention conditions**|Care about own safety<br>Want to avoid injury|
|**Time window conditions**|In waking window (not dream)<br>On Earth window (not in space)|

**Once any condition changes, the "hard constraint" disappears or changes**

### Counterexamples: When Conditions Change, "Falling" Disappears

|Situation|Which Condition Changed|Result|
|---|---|---|
|**Space station**|No Earth gravity field|Jumping "down" won't cause falling<br>"Hard constraint" completely disappears|
|**Dead person**|No conscious subject|Physically will hit ground<br>But "falling" as meaningful manifestation doesn't exist|
|**Gymnast**|Professional training<br>Has cushioning technique|Jump from high platform, perfect landing<br>Won't get injured|
|**Paramecium**|No height perception<br>No injury expectation|No concept of "falling"<br>For it "height" doesn't exist|
|**In dream**|Non-waking window|Might fly up<br>Might land safely<br>Rule "will fall" doesn't hold|

### The Truth of "Hard Constraint"

**The truth of so-called "hard constraint" is**:

On Earth, in waking state, for ordinary living people, the experience "jump will fall" stably repeats in extremely wide conditional ranges, therefore **appears "hard"**.

But this isn't "absolute constraint transcending conditions," but **"high stability conditional on conditions"**.

**Symbolic expression**:

```
Stability of "jump→fall" = f(condition satisfaction)

When condition satisfaction = 100% (Earth, awake, living, normal state)
  → Stability ≈ 99.99%
  → Appears "hard"

When condition satisfaction < 100% (space, dream, dead)
  → Stability → 0
  → "Hard constraint" disappears

No case where condition satisfaction = constantly 100%
Therefore no true "hard constraint"
```

### Relationship with Chapter Twenty-Three: Unification of Two Perspectives

You may have noticed:

**Chapter Twenty-Three said**: Stability of "jump will fall" comes from high-frequency validation + extensive overlap.

**This chapter says**: "Jump will fall" is conditionally dependent, changes when conditions change.

**Do these two statements conflict?**

No conflict. They're two sides of the same phenomenon:

```
Chapter 23's Perspective (Consensus Formation):
"Jump will fall" on Earth, for living people
→ Validated by countless people in countless situations

→ This is "high-frequency validation + extensive overlap"

Chapter 24's Perspective (Conditional Dependence):

Unified Understanding:
"High-frequency validation + extensive overlap" is the phenomenon

Under specific conditions, causal relationships are high-frequency stable

→ Appears "hard"

Change conditions, causal relationship fails

```

**Key insight**:

"Hard constraint" isn't "absolute truth transcending conditions," but "extremely stable consensus under specific conditions."

- Chapter 23 explains: Why consensus is stable (high-frequency + overlap)
- Chapter 24 explains: Why stability has boundaries (conditional dependence)

**Both combined form the complete picture.**

### True Hard Constraints

**Question: Are there true "hard constraints"?**

**Yes, but not what you think.**

True "hard constraints" aren't inductive from experience (like "will fall" "will be full"), but **structural prerequisites of cognitive activity**.

**Returning to the "Three Meta-Constraints" discussed in Chapter Twenty**:

**These are the true "hard constraints"**:

- Not inductive from experience (like "jump will fall")

**But even these don't claim "absolute truth"**:

**All manifestations form a continuous weight spectrum**:

```

├─ Falling (Earth normal conditions)

High weight (appears "reliable"):
```

**Key insight**:

The illusion of "hard constraint" comes from:

- ❌ Confusing "high weight" with "absolute truth"

**Understanding "hard constraints" this way**:

On the contrary, freed from "objectivity worship," science can truly return to its essence: not a religion pursuing transcendent truth, but a method for establishing reliable consensus.
