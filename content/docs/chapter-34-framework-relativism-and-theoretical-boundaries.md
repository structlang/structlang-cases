## Section 1: Framework Relativism

### 1.1 No "Objectively Correct" Framework

Vectorization and transferability theory solved technical problems, but raised deeper philosophical questions:

**What is the "correct" framework?**

I originally thought A/Q/T was an "objective" decomposition method, the "true structure of human nature."

But when I tried to define A/Q/T, I discovered: **The definition of each dimension depends on intention.**

**Action (A): What counts as "action"?**

- In combat: Punch, shoot, charge
- In romance: Confess, date, give gifts
- In negotiation: Make offer, apply pressure, compromise

"Action" is not objective, but relative to intention.

**Quality (Q): What counts as "capability"?**

- In combat: Force, equipment, tactics
- In romance: Appearance, wealth, personality
- In negotiation: Bargaining chips, information, relationships

"Capability" is also not objective, but relative to intention.

**aTtitude (T): What counts as "attitude"?**

- In combat: Courage, determination, morale
- In romance: Sincerity, gentleness, thoughtfulness
- In negotiation: Good faith, cooperation, trust

"Attitude" is likewise not objective, but relative to intention.

### 1.2 Framework as Convention

A/Q/T is not "discovery," but "convention."

**This is a conventional framework:**

- We agree to decompose complex relationships using these three dimensions
- We agree to use this symbol system to represent states
- We agree to compress and reason this way

**This convention is effective in specific domains:**

- In business, competition, survival scenarios
- In games, negotiation, collaboration scenarios
- In scenarios requiring rapid decision-making

**But this convention is not unique:**

- There can be other three-dimensional decompositions
- There can be other symbol systems
- There can be other compression rules

### 1.3 Other Possible Frameworks

Let me show several alternative frameworks:

**Psychology Framework:**

Instead of A/Q/T, use:

- Cognition (C): How to understand situation
- Emotion (E): How to feel relationship
- Behavior (B): How to actually act

This framework emphasizes internal psychological processes, suitable for clinical psychology and counseling.

**Sociology Framework:**

Instead of A/Q/T, use:

- Capital (K): Economic/cultural/social capital
- Network (N): Position in relationship network
- Norms (R): Social norms followed

This framework emphasizes social structure and position, suitable for macro social analysis.

**System Dynamics Framework:**

Instead of A/Q/T, use:

- Stock (S): Current resources and energy
- Flow (F): Input/output rate
- Loop (L): Positive or negative feedback

This framework emphasizes system dynamics, suitable for long-term trend prediction.

**Each framework can analyze the same situation, but will produce different insights.**

### 1.4 No "Optimal" Framework

Which framework is best? **Depends on your purpose.**

If purpose is:

- Rapid decision-making → A/Q/T framework (concise)
- Deep understanding → Psychology framework (detailed)
- System prediction → Dynamics framework (precise)
- Social change → Sociology framework (macro)

**There is no "objectively optimal" framework, only "more suitable relative to purpose" framework.**

This is **framework relativism**:

- Framework is not discovery, is creation
- Framework is not truth, is tool
- Framework's value lies in practicality, not "correctness"

### 1.5 Critique of "Objectivism"

Many theories claim to have discovered "objective laws":

- "The essence of human nature"
- "Laws of behavior"
- "Iron laws of society"

But we must question:

**Problem 1: Observation depends on framework**

You see "people are selfish" because you observe using "interest" framework.  
If using "relationship" framework, you'll see "people cooperate."  
If using "meaning" framework, you'll see "people pursue transcendence."

**What you observe depends on what framework you use.**

**Problem 2: Verification depends on scenario**

You verified "people are selfish" in business scenarios, doesn't mean it holds in family scenarios.  
You verified "survival of fittest" in competition scenarios, doesn't mean it holds in cooperation scenarios.

**Verification's validity depends on scenario boundaries.**

**Problem 3: Prediction depends on stability**

You verified a pattern in 2020, doesn't mean it still holds in 2030.  
Society changes, frameworks evolve, people evolve too.

**Prediction's reliability depends on environmental stability.**

**So-called "objective laws" are often:**

- Under specific framework
- In specific scenarios
- During specific period
- Highly verified patterns

They have value, but are not "eternal truths."

## Section 2: Theory's Boundaries and Limitations

### 2.1 What Our Theory Cannot Explain

Let me honestly list phenomena this theory **cannot explain or explains poorly**:

**1. Emergent Creativity**

An artist suddenly has inspiration, creates amazing work.  
This kind of "emergence" is hard to explain with A/Q/T.

Theory's limitation:

- Creativity often nonlinear
- Hard to decompose into few dimensions
- May need completely different framework

**2. Deep Emotional Bonds**

Two people have no interest relationship, yet willing to sacrifice for each other.  
This kind of "love" is hard to explain with game theory framework.

Theory's limitation:

- Emotions not always rational
- Relationships not always games
- Some values transcend instrumental rationality

**3. Pursuit of Meaning**

A person gives up high-paying job to do unrewarding public service.  
This "meaning pursuit" is hard to explain with interest framework.

Theory's limitation:

- Meaning ≠ Interest
- Value rationality ≠ Instrumental rationality
- Some behaviors cannot be measured by efficiency

**4. Social System Emergence**

A social movement suddenly erupts, changes entire society.  
This "emergence" is hard to explain by superposing individual relationships.

Theory's limitation:

- Whole not equal to sum of parts
- System has emergent properties
- Needs higher-level theory

**5. Deep Cultural Differences**

Same behavior has completely different meanings in different cultures.  
This "cultural relativity" is hard to explain with unified framework.

Theory's limitation:

- Framework itself is cultural product
- Cross-cultural transfer has extreme risk
- Needs cultural sensitivity

### 2.2 Three Fundamental Limitations of Vectorization

Even in theory's applicable domains, vectorization method has limitations:

**Limitation 1: Linearity Assumption**

Vector operations assume linear superposition, but cognition may be nonlinear.

Example:  
Time pressure (+1) + Abundant resources (+1) = Efficiency increase (+2)?

Reality may be:  
Time pressure + Abundant resources → Over-investment, pursue perfection → Efficiency decrease (-1)

**Superposition produces nonlinear effects, vector addition fails.**

**Limitation 2: Discretization Information Loss**

Real intentions are continuous:  
t = +0.7354..., b = +0.8921..., r = -0.6483...

But we simplify to:  
t ≈ +1, b ≈ +1, r ≈ -1

**Discretization inevitably loses information, but this is necessary price for operability.**

**Limitation 3: Context Sensitivity**

Same +1 may have different meanings in different contexts:

"Time urgency +1":

- In daily work: May be 2-day deadline
- In emergency room: May be 2 minutes
- On cosmic scale: May be 200 years

**Vector operations assume +1 has unified meaning, but actually context-dependent.**

### 2.3 Theory's Applicable Boundaries

Based on above analysis, we can clearly define theory's applicable boundaries:

**Applicable domains:**

- Business transactions
- Workplace collaboration
- Competitive games
- Survival decisions
- Resource allocation
- Short-term games
- Situations with clear goals

**Non-applicable domains:**

- Intimate relationships (love, family)
- Artistic creation
- Spiritual pursuits
- Value judgments
- Meaning seeking
- Transcendent experiences
- Long-term cultural evolution

**Core judgment criterion:**

If a situation mainly involves:

- Interest calculation
- Strategic games
- Efficiency optimization → Theory may be useful

If a situation mainly involves:

- Emotional connection
- Value realization
- Meaning pursuit → Theory may be harmful

**Key principle: Don't extend instrumental rationality to all domains.**

## Section 3: Theory's Ethical Responsibility

### 3.1 Knowledge's Power Effect

Theory is not neutral. **Mastering theory is mastering power.**

**Information asymmetry produces power:**

You understand 64-Pattern theory, other party doesn't  
→ You can predict other party's reactions  
→ You can design strategies to manipulate other party  
→ You gain power advantage in relationship

This is not "knowledge is strength," but **"knowledge is power"**.

**Framework defines reality:**

You analyze relationship using A/Q/T framework  
→ Relationship simplified to these three dimensions  
→ Other dimensions (emotion, history, meaning) ignored  
→ Relationship "reified" as calculable object

**This is a kind of cognitive violence.**

When you label a person as (-+-):

- You simplify this person's complexity
- You ignore this person's uniqueness
- You turn this person into an operable object

This simplification has practical value, but ethical cost.

### 3.2 Theory's Misuse Risks

This theory can be used for:

**Defensive application (legitimate):**

- Protect one's own legitimate rights
- Identify others' manipulation
- Game within legal framework
- Example: Fake dog refund case, protecting consumer rights

**Offensive application (questionable):**

- Manipulate others' judgment
- Exploit information asymmetry
- Walk moral boundaries
- Example: Use theory to design manipulative rhetoric

**Exploitative application (inappropriate):**

- Systematically oppress the weak
- Deliberately deceive and exploit
- Illegal criminal acts
- Example: Use theory to defraud vulnerable groups

**Our stance:**

Theory can be used for self-protection, but should not be used to oppress others.

If you use this theory to get refund for fake dog, this is legitimate.  
If you use this theory to manipulate vulnerable groups for profit, this is unethical.

**Same tool, different users, different ethical nature.**

### 3.3 When to Set Theory Aside

In some contexts, **should not use this theory**:

**Example 1: Intimate Relationships**

If always calculating:

- "How much I give, how much you return"
- "What's this relationship's ROI"

→ Relationship withers  
→ Love becomes transaction

**Example 2: Raising Children**

If always strategic:

- "How to make child efficiently successful"
- "How to optimize education investment"

→ Ignore child's intrinsic value  
→ Ignore process meaning of growth

**Example 3: Spiritual Pursuit**

If always calculating:

- "What's the use of this"
- "Where's the return"

→ Cannot experience transcendence  
→ Cannot touch meaning

**Example 4: Helping the Weak**

If always analyzing:

- "This person -+-, not worth helping"
- "Helping's ROI too low"

→ Lose compassion  
→ Lose moral sense

**Recommendation:**

Use theory in instrumental contexts (work, transactions, competition)  
Set theory aside in value contexts (love, art, faith, morality)

### 3.4 Toward Reflexive Practice

What is reflexive practice? Not blindly using theory, but:

**First layer: Use theory**  
Analyze situation, formulate strategy

**Second layer: Reflect on theory**

- Is this theory applicable?
- Where are limitations?
- Am I over-relying?

**Third layer: Reflect on self**

- Why did I choose this framework?
- What are my motivations?
- What did I ignore?

**Fourth layer: Reflect on relationship**

- After using theory, how did relationship change?
- Better or worse?
- Any unexpected consequences?

**Fifth layer: Reflect on ethics**

- Is what I'm doing legitimate?
- Is other party treated as means or end?
- Am I helping or manipulating?

**This is "theory-informed practice," not "theory-driven practice."**

Ideal state:

When needed:

- Use clarity theory provides
- Use strategies theory provides

When should:

- Set framework aside
- Return to direct human connection

Key: Cultivate judgment, know when to use, when not to use.

## Section 4: Theory's Humble Positioning

### 4.1 What We Are Providing

**Not:**

- "Ultimate truth about human behavior"
- "Solution to all problems"
- "Universally applicable laws"
- "Scientific predictive model"

**But:**

- "Useful analytical tool in specific domains"
- "One possible way of understanding"
- "Framework with applicable boundaries"
- "Methodology that can be continuously improved"

### 4.2 Theory's Value

This theory:

- Helped me solve fake dog refund problem
- Helped me understand many workplace games
- Helped me see through some relationship structures
- Provided a teachable method

In its applicable domains, it's a valuable tool.

### 4.3 Theory's Limitations

But:

- Cannot solve all problems
- Cannot understand all relationships
- Cannot answer all questions
- Cannot replace other perspectives

It's my attempt to establish usable analytical tools within business/survival/competition framework.

**It has value, but also has limitations.**

### 4.4 Theory's Self-Critique

Let me list this theory's biggest problems:

**Problem 1: Framework's Cultural Bias**

A/Q/T framework may reflect:

- Western individualistic culture
- Business competition logic
- Instrumental rationality thinking

In other cultures, this framework may not apply or need major adjustment.

**Problem 2: Over-Simplification of Complexity**

Simplifying a person to (+++) or (-+-):

- Ignores person's complexity
- Ignores situation's fluidity
- Ignores history's path dependence

Simplification has practical value, but also cognitive violence.

**Problem 3: Risk of Instrumental Rationality Expansion**

If this tool is misused:

- Will turn all relationships into games
- Will instrumentalize all values
- Will forget human dignity and meaning

Theory itself innocent, but users need vigilance.

**Problem 4: Over-Confidence in Prediction**

Vectorization calculation gives illusion of "precise science":

- But humans are not machines
- Relationships are not physical systems
- Prediction always has uncertainty

Don't be superstitious about numbers and formulas.

### 4.5 Expectations for Readers

We hope readers:

**Don't:**

- Treat theory as religion
- Treat framework as absolute truth
- Use in all contexts
- Replace ethical judgment with theory
- Use theory to escape emotional responsibility

**But:**

- Treat theory as tool
- Understand tool's boundaries
- Use in appropriate contexts
- Maintain reflexive critique
- Cultivate multiple perspectives
- Transcend single framework
- Always remember human dignity

**Most importantly:**

Don't think you're "smarter" than others because you mastered theory.  
Don't forget relationship's warmth because you can analyze relationships.  
Don't ignore life's meaning because pursuing efficiency.

Theory is tool, not purpose.  
People are purpose, not means.

## Section 5: Open Ending

### 5.1 Theory's Incompleteness

This theory reaches here, but is not complete.

**Why incomplete?**

Not because:

- "Not finished writing"
- "Insufficient time"

But because:

- Theory is inherently open
- Reality changes, new contexts constantly emerge
- Frameworks evolve, new frameworks emerge
- Cognition deepens, new insights emerge

Therefore: This is not "final version," but "current version."

### 5.2 Questions Left Behind

**Theoretical questions:**

1. Is intention space optimal? Is Time-Benefit-Risk the best three dimensions?
2. Is compression algorithm unique? Besides A-Q-T, are there other effective decompositions?
3. Is leap theory complete? Are there other types of leaps?
4. Is transferability framework sufficient? Are six-layer judgments enough?

**Application questions:**

1. How to handle dynamic systems? Relationships aren't static, how to build dynamic models?
2. How to handle multi-agent games? Complex relationships with three or more parties, how to model network effects?
3. How to handle long-term evolution? Historical path dependence of relationships, how to predict long-term trends?
4. How to handle cultural differences? How to incorporate cross-cultural transfer special issues into theory?

**Philosophical questions:**

1. Nature of subjectivity: Where does intention come from? How to understand free will?
2. Nature of causality: Are there really "causes" and "effects"? Or just our compression method?
3. Nature of temporality: Why is time so important? What is the nature of time?
4. Foundation of knowledge: What's the justification for experiential knowledge? How is inductive reasoning possible?
