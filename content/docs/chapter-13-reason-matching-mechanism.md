## Introduction: What Is Thinking?

When asked "Why were you late today?", how would you answer?

- "Because of traffic."
- "Because my alarm didn't go off."
- "Because I didn't sleep well last night."
- "Because this meeting isn't important."

Each answer "makes sense," but you'd give different reasons depending on who's asking (boss, colleague, friend).

When facing "How can I avoid being late?", what would you consider?

- "Leave earlier."
- "Take a different route."
- "Buy a louder alarm."
- "Live closer to the office."

Each solution is "feasible," but you'd choose different paths based on resources (money, time), environment (housing prices, commute).

**These two thinking activities seem different, but are essentially the same: both are reason matching.**

Traditional epistemology tells us:

- "Why" discovers the real cause
- "How" finds the correct method
- Rational thinking leads to unique truth

But within the StructLang framework, the truth is completely different:

**Thinking is not discovery, but matching.**

You're not "discovering" why you were late, but among many possible reasons, matching one that "makes sense" in the current situation.

You're not "finding" the only correct method, but among possible paths, matching one that's "feasible" under current conditions.

This chapter reveals the real mechanism of thinking, showing how "why" and "how" operate within the same framework.

## Scope of Reason Matching

Reason matching discussed in this chapter specifically refers to two types of basic reasoning activities:

- Retrospective reasoning ("Why")
- Prospective planning ("How")

Reason matching isn't all thinking, but it's the core mechanism of human reasoning activity.

Human thinking also includes other types:

- Recognition & classification ("What is it") — discussed in Chapter 2 point structure
- Analogy & metaphor ("What is it like") — involves structure matching
- Value judgment ("Is it good") — discussed in Chapter 3 line structure
- Direct perception (seeing, hearing, feeling) — raw layer of manifestation
- Memory recall — time window switching, structure matching
- Epiphany understanding — structure matching, structural leap

Others are either variants/combinations of these, or more automatic neural processes (like association), not active thinking.

## Section 1: Essence of Thinking—Reason Matching

### Definition of Reason Matching

Before starting, we need to clearly define what **reason matching** is:

> **Reason matching**: Under intention drive, based on currently available materials (knowledge, experience, causal impressions), under constraints (resources, time, environment), the cognitive process of generating or matching an explanation or path that "makes sense."

Let's unpack this definition:

**"Under intention drive"** (see Chapters 1-2):

- Without intention, there's no direction for thinking
- Different intentions lead to different reason matching
- Asking boss vs. asking friend, different intentions, different reasons

**"Based on currently available materials"**:

- **Knowledge structures**: Theories and concepts you've learned (see Chapter 9)
- **Experience structures**: Events and patterns you've experienced (see Chapter 9)
- **Causal impressions**: High-frequency stable connections you've formed (see Chapter 9)

**"Under constraints"**:

- **Resources**: What you have available (money, tools, connections)
- **Time**: Your time window length (see Chapter 22)
- **Environment**: What situation you're in

**"Generate or match"**:

- Sometimes directly invoke ready-made reasons (matching)
- Sometimes combine multiple causal impressions to generate new reasons (generation)

**"Makes sense"**:

- Not "objectively true"
- But "rationalized" in current manifestation
- Can be self-consistent, can explain, can persuade

### Difference Between Reason Matching and Traditional "Thinking"

|Traditional Epistemology|Reason Matching Framework|
|---|---|
|Thinking = discovering truth|Thinking = matching reasons|
|Has unique correct answer|Reasons are condition-dependent|
|Rationality transcends bias|Reasons constrained by materials and conditions|
|Reasoning leads to truth|Reasoning generates self-consistency|
|Objective causes exist|Origin selection determines reasons|

**Core difference**:

Traditional view thinks "Why does it rain?" has a "real cause" waiting to be discovered.

Reason matching framework thinks:

- Physical origin: "Because water vapor condenses"
- Agricultural origin: "Because monsoon arrives"
- Mythological origin: "Because dragon king sends rain"
- Personal origin: "Because I forgot my umbrella"

**Each reason is "correct"**, depending on:

- What's your intention (explaining physical phenomenon? Planning farming? Emotional venting?)
- What knowledge do you have (physics? meteorology? mythology?)
- What's your origin (see Chapter 2)

**No "real cause" transcending conditions**

### Two Directions of Reason Matching

Reason matching has two directions, corresponding to two basic thinking activities:

```
        Causal impression: A → B
              ↓
    ┌─────────┴─────────┐
    ↓                   ↓
Match to past          Match to future
"Why B?"              "How to get B?"
(Retrospective)       (Planning)
```

**To the past**: Match causes for events that already happened

- Event E already manifested
- Search in causal impression library: What→E
- This is "why"

**To the future**: Match paths for desired goals

- Goal G not yet manifested
- Search in causal impression library: Current→What→G
- This is "how"

**Essentially same mechanism applied bidirectionally**

## Section 2: Conditional Dependence of Reasons

Reasons aren't "objectively discovered," but "conditional products." Under different conditions, different reasons inevitably arise.

### Three-Layer Structure of Conditions

Reason matching depends on three types of conditions:

#### 1. Subject Internal Conditions

**Intention** (Chapters 1-2):

- Why ask this question?
- What goal to achieve?
- Different intentions→different origins→different reasons

**Example**:

```
Same question: "Why did the project fail?"

Boss's intention: Accountability → "Because team didn't work hard"
Employee's intention: Self-protection → "Because insufficient resources"
Consultant's intention: Improvement → "Because process has problems"
Investor's intention: Cut losses → "Because market timing was wrong"
```

**Knowledge structures** (Chapter 9):

- What theories did you learn?
- What concepts do you master?
- Knowledge framework determines available reason types

**Example**:

```
Same phenomenon: Person gets sick

Ancient Greek doctor: Four humors theory → "Humoral imbalance"
Traditional Chinese medicine: Yin-Yang five elements → "Qi-blood obstruction"
Modern doctor: Microbiology → "Bacterial infection"
Witch doctor: Soul theory → "Evil spirit possession"
```

**Experience structures** (Chapter 9):

- What did you experience?
- What causal impressions did you form?
- Experience determines invokable patterns

**Example**:

```
Question: "How to succeed?"

Successful entrepreneur's experience: Risk-taking + persistence
Employee's experience: Stability + obedience
Investor's experience: Choose right track
Failed person's experience: Avoid my mistakes

Different experiences→different causal impressions→different solutions
```

#### 2. Subject External Conditions

**Environment**:

- Physical environment (climate, terrain)
- Social environment (culture, institutions)
- Technical environment (tools, equipment)

**Example**:

```
Question: "How to move quickly?"

Ancient environment: Horse carriage, boat
Modern environment: Car, plane
Future environment: Teleportation?

Environment determines feasible paths
```

**Available resources**:

- Material resources (money, goods)
- Human resources (connections, team)
- Information resources (data, knowledge)

**Example**:

```
Question: "How to treat illness?"

Have money: Go to best hospital
No money: Buy cheap medicine
Extreme poverty: Find folk remedies, rely on self-healing

Resources determine selectable options
```

**External structures** (Chapter 14):

- Existing social structures
- Established power relationships
- Borrowable institutions

**Example** (fake dog refund case, Chapter 10):

```
Question: "How to get refund?"

External structures I invoked:
- Industrial and commercial supervision system
- Mayor's hotline deterrence
- Law as highest-weight origin

Different external structures→different pressure paths
```

#### 3. Time Constraints

**Time window** (Chapter 22):

- How much available time?
- Time window determines explorable depth
- Short window→quickly match familiar reasons
- Long window→deeply search new reasons

**Example**:

```
Boss asks: "Why did it go wrong?"

5-second answer: "I wasn't careful" (quick match)
5-minute answer: "Because process has loopholes" (analysis)
5-hour answer: "Systematic cause analysis report" (deep dive)

Time window determines reason refinement
```

### Formalized Expression of Condition Interaction

We can symbolically express condition dependence of reason matching:

```
Reason(R) = f(Ic, Kc, Ec | Env, Res, Es | Tw)

Where:
Ic  = Intention condition
Kc  = Knowledge condition
Ec  = Experience condition
Env = Environment condition
Res = Resources condition
Es  = External structure condition
Tw  = Time window

Symbol "|" means "constrained by".
```

**Verbal expression**:  
Matched reason R is function output generated from intention, knowledge, experience constrained by environment, resources, external structures, within specific time window.

**Key insight**:

Change any one condition, reason changes.

No "unique correct reason transcending conditions" exists.

### Case of Condition Change: Disease Treatment

Let's see through complete case how conditions interact to affect reasons:

**Question**: Person has fever, what to do?

|**Item**|**Ancient China**|**Ancient Europe**|**Modern Medicine**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Intention**|Reduce fever, restore health|Reduce fever|Reduce fever, treat root cause|
|**Knowledge**|Yin-Yang five elements, meridians|Four humors theory|Microbiology, physiology|
|**Experience**|Some improved after acupuncture|Some fever reduced after bloodletting|Antibiotics high success rate|
|**Environment**|Agricultural society, scarce medical resources|Urban society|Industrial society, complete medical system|
|**Resources**|Herbs, silver needles|Scalpel, bloodletting equipment|Laboratory, antibiotics, monitoring equipment|
|**External Structure**|TCM system, master-apprentice inheritance|Medical guild, university system|Evidence-based medicine, FDA certification|
|**Time Window**|Can slowly adjust|Need quick results|Can quickly diagnose|
|**Matched Reason (Why)**|Internal Yin-Yang imbalance, heat toxin accumulation|Excess and unbalanced humors, blood has heat|Bacterial or viral infection, immune response causes fever|
|**Matched Solution (How)**|Acupuncture to clear meridians + heat-clearing herbs|Bloodletting, drain excess hot blood|Detect pathogen + antibiotics/antivirals + fever reducers|
|**Rationality Explanation**|"Reasonable" under then conditions|"Reasonable" under then conditions|"More effective" under current conditions|

### Key Insights

**Not "modern medicine discovered truth, ancient medicine was wrong"**

But rather:

- Different conditions matched different reasons
- Modern medicine's reasons stable and effective under more conditions
- But still "reasons matched under current knowledge, technical conditions"
- Future may have completely different reason systems

**Each era's reasons are "rationalization" results under its conditions**

## Section 3: "Why"—Reason Matching to the Past

### Mechanism of "Why"

When you ask "why," what are you actually doing?

**Traditional understanding**: Tracing back to real cause

**Reason matching framework**:

```
"Why" operation flow:

1. Anchor event E (manifested occurrence)
   ↓
2. Choose origin P (Chapter 2)
   ↓
3. Invoke causal impression library C (Chapter 9)
   ↓
4. Search: What(A) → E
   ↓
5. Match A that "makes sense"
   ↓
6. Output reason: "Because A"
```

**Symbolic expression**:

```
Why(E) = Choose origin(P) × Invoke causal impression(C_p) → Match(A)

Where:
E = Event that occurred (manifestation point)
P = Chosen origin (determines search direction)
C_p = Causal impression set invokable under origin P
A = Matched antecedent event (reason)

Constraint: A → E holds in causal impression C_p
```

### Origin Dependence of "Why"

Same event, choose different origins, get completely different reasons.

**Case: Why fall down after jumping?**

|Origin|Reason|Corresponding Intention|
|---|---|---|
|Physical origin|Because gravitational acceleration 9.8m/s²|Understand physical mechanism|
|Biological origin|Because human skeleton can't withstand impact|Design protective equipment|
|Engineering origin|Because no cushioning facilities|Building safety design|
|Psychological origin|Because judgment error/impulsiveness|Psychological intervention|
|Social origin|Because lack of safety education|Institutional improvement|
|Legal origin|Because builder had no protective measures|Pursue responsibility|

**Each reason is "correct"** because:

- They correspond to different intentions
- They're based on different knowledge structures
- They serve different purposes

**No "real" cause**, only "reasons matched under specific origin"

### "Why" as Decompression Process

From compression-decompression perspective (Chapter 8), "why" is a decompression process:

**Compression result**:

- You see: T1 (rooftop) → T2 (ground injured)
- Middle process compressed

**"Why"**:

- Try to decompress: What happened between T1 and T2?
- Fill gap in manifestation

**But decompression path isn't unique**:

```
Path 1 (Physical):
T1 → Loss of support → Gravity acceleration → Ground impact → T2

Path 2 (Psychological):
T1 → Emotional loss of control → Made decision → Jumped → T2

Path 3 (Social):
T1 → Lack of supervision → No one stopped → Accident occurred → T2
```

**Which path is "correct"?**

All correct, depends on which origin you choose.

### Recursive Trap of "Why"

"Why" can recurse infinitely:

```
"Why fall after jumping down?"
"Because of gravity."

"Why is there gravity?"
"Because mass curves spacetime."

"Why does mass curve spacetime?"
"Because this is prediction of general relativity."

"Why is general relativity correct?"
"Because experiments verified it."

"Why does experimental verification count?"
"Because..."
```

**When does this recursion stop?**

**Traditional view**: When touching "first cause" "ultimate truth"

**Reason matching framework**:

- Recursion stops at **current time window boundary**
- Or stops at **current knowledge structure boundary**
- Or stops at **when questioner satisfied**

**No "absolute endpoint"**

Each "why" is reason matching under current conditions, not approximating ultimate truth.

### Case: "Why" in Fake Dog Refund

Reviewing fake dog refund case (Chapter 10), we can re-analyze with reason matching framework:

**Question: "Why did seller finally refund?"**

**Different perspectives, different reasons:**

|Perspective|Origin|Reason|
|---|---|---|
|Me|Legal origin|Because seller operated illegally, evidence conclusive|
|Seller|Pressure origin|Because official pressured, had to refund|
|Official|Risk origin|Because mayor's hotline threat, risk too great|
|Observer|Power origin|Because consumer uses institutional weapons|

**Which is "correct"?**

**All correct**, each is reasonable reason matched under their respective intentions and origins.

**No "real cause" transcending perspective**

### Practical Value of "Why"

Though "why" isn't "discovering truth," reason matching still has value:

**Value 1: Reinforce causal impressions**

- Each reason matching reinforces some causal impression
- "Illegal→punished" impression reinforced

**Value 2: Guide "how"**

- Reasons matched by "why" provide materials for "how"
- If "because illegal," then "how" is "legalize"

**Value 3: Self-rationalization**

- Reason matching helps us explain our behavior
- Maintain cognitive consistency

**Value 4: Social coordination**

- Shared reason matching (consensus) makes collaboration possible
- "Everyone believes illegal should be punished"→institution operates

## Section 4: "How"—Reason Matching to the Future

### Mechanism of "How"

When you ask "how," what are you actually doing?

**Traditional understanding**: Finding correct method

**Reason matching framework**:

```
"How" operation flow:

1. Anchor goal G (desired manifestation)
   ↓
2. Assess current state S₀
   ↓
3. Choose origin P
   ↓
4. Invoke causal impression library C
   ↓
5. Reverse search: S₀ →?→ G
   ↓
6. Match feasible path
   ↓
7. Evaluate cost/risk/time
   ↓
8. Output solution: "Do A"
```

**Symbolic expression**:

```
How(G) = Anchor intention(I_G) × Assess current(S₀) × Reverse causal impression(C⁻¹) 
       → Match path(Path) 
       → Filter(Cost, Risk, Time)
       → Solution(A)

Where:
G = Goal state (desired manifestation)
I_G = Intention to achieve G
S₀ = Current state
C⁻¹ = Reverse application of causal impressions
Path = Set of possible paths
A = Chosen action plan

Constraint: A makes probability of S₀ → G increase in subjective manifestation
```

### Unity of "How" and "Why"

"How" is essentially reverse application of "why":

```
Causal impression: A → B

"Why B?" = Trace back from B to A (known B, find A)
"How to get B?" = Derive from A to B (known goal B, find path A)
```

**Example**:

**Causal impression**: Push door hard → Door opens

- "Why did door open?" (backward): Because someone pushed
- "How to open door?" (forward): Push hard

**Same causal impression, used bidirectionally**

### Conditional Dependence of "How"

Like "why," "how" is also highly condition-dependent.

**Case: How not to fall?**

**Condition 1: Sufficient resources**

- Option A: Install elevator (high cost, good effect)
- Option B: Build ramp (medium cost, long-term effective)

**Condition 2: Limited resources**

- Option C: Take stairs (no cost, high time cost)
- Option D: Strengthen safety education (low cost, effect depends on execution)

**Condition 3: Emergency situation**

- Option E: Use parachute (high cost, immediately effective)
- Option F: Wait for rescue (no cost, long time)

**No "unique correct solution"**, only "optimal match under current conditions"

### "How" as Structural Leap

From subject-object structure perspective (Chapter 5), "how" is planning structural leap:

**Current structure**:

- Me (+++): Have intention, have capability, active
- Problem (---): Exists, obstructs, negative

**Goal structure**:

- Me (+++): Maintain
- Problem (++-or disappears): Solved or avoided

**"How"**:

- Find trigger factor
- Guide structural leap
- From current structure → goal structure

**Example: Fake dog refund**

```
Current structure (T0):
Me (+++

) vs Seller (-+-) → Deadlock

"How?"→ Introduce external structure:
Me (+++​) + Law/mayor's hotline (+++​) vs Seller (-+-)

Goal structure (T5):
Me (+++​) + Official (+++​) vs Seller (---) → Refund
```

**Essence of "how": Design trigger factors, guide leap**

### Path Evaluation in "How"

After matching multiple possible paths, how to choose?

**Evaluation dimensions**:

**1. Cost**:

- Material cost (money, goods)
- Time cost (how long)
- Energy cost (how tiring)
- Relationship cost (who to offend)

**2. Risk**:

- Failure probability
- Failure consequences
- Uncertainty

**3. Time window**:

- How long to see results?
- Can complete within time window?

**4. Stability**:

- Short-term or long-term effective?
- Will it create new problems?

**Symbolic expression**:

```
Optimal solution = arg min [Cost(A) + Risk(A) - Stability(A)]
                  A∈Path
           
Constraint: Time(A) ≤ Time_window
```

**Verbal expression**:

Under time window constraint, choose solution with lowest cost, lowest risk, highest stability.

**But "optimal" is subjective**:

- Different people have different weights for cost/risk/time
- Therefore "optimal solution" is relative

## Section 5: Case Analysis—From Bloodletting to Antibiotics

Let's see through complete case in medical history how reason matching operates in different eras.

### Case Background: Humanity's Struggle with Disease

From ancient times to now, humanity faces same problem: **disease**

But in different eras, reason matching conditions completely different, leading to completely different reasons and solutions.

### Ancient Greece-Medieval Europe: Four Humors Theory

#### Condition Analysis

**Subject Internal Conditions**:

- **Intention**: Cure disease, restore health
- **Knowledge structure**:
    - Four humors theory (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile)
    - Believed health = four humors balance
    - Believed disease = humors imbalance
- **Experience structure**:
    - Some people improved after bloodletting
    - Some symptoms relieved after laxatives
    - These experiences compressed into causal impressions

**Subject External Conditions**:

- **Environment**: European cities, medical resources concentrated in nobility
- **Resources**: Scalpel, bloodletting equipment, laxatives
- **External structure**: Medical guild, university system, master-apprentice inheritance

**Time window**: Disease usually allows long observation and treatment time

#### Reason Matching

**"Why get sick?"**

```
Choose origin: Humoral balance origin
Invoke causal impression: Imbalance → Disease
Match reason: "Because humors too much or too little, lost balance"
```

**"How to treat?"**

```
Anchor goal: Restore humoral balance
Reverse causal impressions:
- Too much blood → Bloodletting can reduce
- Too much phlegm → Emetics can expel
- Too much bile → Laxatives can clear

Match solution:
1. Observe symptoms, judge which humor imbalanced
2. Bloodletting (most common, because blood most "visible")
3. Or use emetics, laxatives

Evaluate (under then conditions):
- Cost: Medium (needs doctor)
- Risk: High (but unknowing, thought "curing")
- Time: Needs multiple treatments
- Stability: Low (but thought effective)
```

#### Historical Facts

**Some did improve**:

- In certain cases, bloodletting could:
    - Lower blood pressure (effective for hypertension emergencies)
    - Reduce inflammatory response (modern medicine confirms anti-inflammatory effect of small bloodletting)
- This reinforced "bloodletting effective" causal impression

**More people died**:

- But many died from excessive bloodletting
- Explained as "humoral imbalance too severe" or "treatment too late"
- Not "theory itself has problems"

**Under then conditions, this was "reasonable" reason matching**

### Ancient China: Traditional Chinese Medicine Theory

#### Condition Analysis

**Subject Internal Conditions**:

- **Intention**: Heal and save people, regulate body
- **Knowledge structure**:
    - Yin-Yang five elements theory
    - Meridian theory
    - Qi-blood theory
- **Experience structure**:
    - Some symptoms relieved after acupuncture
    - Some recovered after herbs
    - Thousands of years accumulated clinical experience

**Subject External Conditions**:

- **Environment**: Agricultural society, dispersed medical resources
- **Resources**: Herbs, acupuncture
- **External structure**: Master-apprentice inheritance, classic records

#### Reason Matching

**"Why get sick?"**

```
Choose origin: Yin-Yang balance origin
Invoke causal impression: Imbalance → Disease
Match reason:
- "Yin-Yang disorder"
- "Qi-blood obstruction"
- "External pathogen invasion"
```

**"How to treat?"**

```
Anchor goal: Harmonize Yin-Yang, clear meridians
Reverse causal impressions:
- Meridian obstruction → Acupuncture can clear
- Yin deficiency Yang excess → Yin-nourishing Yang-reducing medicine
- External pathogen invasion → Sweat-inducing exterior-resolving

Match solution:
1. Inspection, auscultation, inquiry, palpation; syndrome differentiation
2. Acupuncture to clear meridians
3. Herbs to regulate Yin-Yang

Evaluate (under then conditions):
- Cost: Low (herbs easily available)
- Risk: Low (relatively small side effects)
- Time: Slow (needs regulation)
- Stability: Medium (partially effective)
```

#### Modern Evaluation

**Effective parts**:

- Some herbs contain active ingredients (like artemisinin)
- Acupuncture's neural regulation effect
- Holistic regulation's auxiliary effect on chronic diseases

**Limitations**:

- Difficult to handle acute infections
- Theoretical framework incompatible with modern physiology
- Large individual differences in effects

**Under then conditions, this was also "reasonable" reason matching**

### African Tribes: Witchcraft and Herbs

#### Condition Analysis

**Subject Internal Conditions**:

- **Intention**: Expel disease demons, restore health
- **Knowledge structure**:
    - Soul/spirit beliefs
    - Believed disease = evil spirit possession
    - Believed witch doctors can communicate with spirit world
- **Experience structure**:
    - Some recovered after rituals
    - Herbs have actual effects
    - These experiences integrated into soul theory

**Subject External Conditions**:

- **Environment**: Tribal society, scarce resources
- **Resources**: Local herbs, ritual tools
- **External structure**: Witch doctor's authority, tribal traditions

#### Reason Matching

**"Why get sick?"**

```
Choose origin: Soul origin
Invoke causal impression: Evil spirit/curse → Disease
Match reason:
- "Possessed by evil spirit"
- "Cursed"
- "Violated taboo"
```

**"How to treat?"**

```
Anchor goal: Expel evil spirit, restore normal
Reverse causal impressions:
- Evil spirits fear rituals → Hold exorcism ritual
- Herbs have spiritual power → Take specific herbs
- Ancestors protect → Pray to ancestors

Match solution:
1. Witch doctor diagnose (determine which evil spirit)
2. Hold exorcism ritual
3. Take herbs
4. Follow taboos

Evaluate (under then conditions):
- Cost: Medium (need pay witch doctor)
- Risk: Low (ritual itself harmless)
- Time: Fast (ritual completed in days)
- Stability: Low (but faith provides psychological comfort)
```

#### Key Insight

**Why did some also recover?**

- Some herbs actually effective
- Ritual's psychological comfort effect (placebo effect)
- Self-limiting diseases naturally recover

**This reinforced "witchcraft effective" causal impression**

**Under then conditions, this was also "reasonable" reason matching**

### Modern Medicine: Microbiology Revolution

#### Condition Analysis

**Subject Internal Conditions**:

- **Intention**: Precise diagnosis, effective treatment
- **Knowledge structure**:
    - Microbiology (bacteria, viruses)
    - Physiology, pharmacology
    - Evidence-based medicine
- **Experience structure**:
    - Antibiotics' high success rate
    - Vaccines' preventive effects
    - Large clinical trial data

**Subject External Conditions**:

- **Environment**: Industrial society, complete medical system
- **Resources**:
    - Laboratory, microscope
    - Antibiotics, vaccines
    - Imaging equipment, monitoring instruments
- **External structure**:
    - Medical schools, research institutions
    - Drug regulation (FDA, etc.)
    - Evidence-based medicine system

**Time window**: Can quickly diagnose, quickly see effects

#### Reason Matching

**"Why get sick?"**

```
Choose origin: Microbial origin
Invoke causal impression: Pathogen invasion → Infection → Disease
Match reason:
- "Bacterial infection" (like Streptococcus pneumoniae)
- "Viral infection" (like influenza virus)
- "Fungal infection"
- Or "Non-infectious disease" (like autoimmune, tumor)
```

**"How to treat?"**

```
Anchor goal: Eliminate pathogen, restore health
Reverse causal impressions:
- Bacterial infection → Antibiotics can kill
- Viral infection → Antivirals or supportive treatment
- Autoimmune → Immunosuppressants

Match solution:
1. Detect pathogen (blood, imaging, culture)
2. Choose targeted drug (sensitivity test)
3. Monitor efficacy (indicator tracking)
4. Adjust plan if necessary

Evaluate (under current conditions):
- Cost: High (but have insurance)
- Risk: Low (through clinical trials)
- Time: Fast (antibiotics effective in days)
- Stability: High (high cure rate)
```

#### Why Is Modern Medicine More Effective?

**Not because "discovered truth"**

But because:

- **Higher verification frequency**: Millions of clinical trials, extremely stable multi-subjective overlap
- **Stronger repeatability**: Any hospital globally can repeat
- **More accurate predictive power**: Antibiotics' effectiveness against specific bacteria can be precisely predicted
- **More precise tools**: Microscope, gene sequencing directly "see" pathogens

**This is reason matching under stricter conditions**

But still "matching," not "discovering objective truth"

## Section 6: Feynman's Wisdom—Science Is Guessing

Richard Feynman, one of 20th century's greatest physicists, Nobel Prize winner, had clear understanding of science's essence.

### Feynman's Original Words

In 1964 Cornell University Messenger Lectures series, Feynman said:

> "In general we look for a new law by the following process: **First we guess it.** Then we compute the consequences of the guess to see what would be implied if this law that we guessed is right. Then we compare the result of the computation to nature, with experiment or experience, compare it directly with observation, to see if it works. **If it disagrees with experiment it is wrong.** In that simple statement is the key to science."

In _The Character of Physical Law_, he said more directly:

> "It doesn't matter how beautiful your theory is, it doesn't matter how smart you are. **If it doesn't agree with experiment, it's wrong.**"

### Profundity of Feynman's Insight

Feynman used simplest language to pierce through "science discovers truth" myth:

**Science's core process**:

1. **Guess** — not "discover," but "guess"
2. **Calculate consequences** — derive predictions from guess
3. **Compare with experiment** — see if prediction stably overlaps in multi-subjective perspectives
4. **Wrong if disagrees** — no "approaching truth," only "right" or "wrong"

**This completely aligns with reason matching framework**:

```
Scientific research = High-frequency verification version of reason matching

1. Guess = Match a possible reason (hypothesis)
2. Calculate consequences = Derive manifestation from this reason
3. Compare with experiment = See if this manifestation stably overlaps in multi-subjectives
4. Wrong if disagrees = Update causal impression, rematch
```

### Science Isn't "Discovery," But "Guess + Verification"

**Traditional philosophy of science narrative**:

- Scientists **discover** objective laws through observation and reasoning
- Scientific theories **describe** objective reality
- Scientific progress is **approaching** ultimate truth

**Feynman's (and reason matching framework's) truth**:

- Scientists first **guess** (match reasons from available materials)
- Scientific theories are **highly verified guesses**
- Scientific progress is **guesses effective under more conditions**

**Key difference**:

|Traditional Narrative|Feynman/Reason Matching|

|Discovery → Truth|Guess → Verification|
|Describe objective|Match stable overlap|
|Approach truth|Expand applicable conditions|
|Ultimate answer|Temporarily effective answer|
