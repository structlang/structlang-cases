Three months later, you finally can't take it anymore.

The candidate you hired—their capability is indeed good, attitude has always been positive, but they just don't advance work.

You decide to have a talk with them.

---

## Assessment Before the Talk

Before the conversation, you first assess both parties' states.

**Them:**

- Action: - (not advancing work)
- Quality: + (solid technical skills)
- aTtitude: + (shows enthusiasm)

They are (-,+,+).

**You:**

- Action: + (need to advance the project)
- Quality: + (have management authority and resources)
- aTtitude: + (hope they can improve)

You are (+,+,+).

But the problem is: **This isn't two independent evaluations—it's the relationship between you and them.**

Your (+,+,+) encountering their (-,+,+), what will happen?

---

## Six-Dimensional Combination

When you and they sit down to talk, the true structure is this:

```
Intention: Get them to advance work

Your three dimensions:
- Your action: + (proactively talking)
- Your quality: + (have resources to support)
- Your attitude: + (hope they improve)

Their three dimensions:
- Their action: - (haven't been advancing)
- Their quality: + (technically no problem)
- Their attitude: + (shows cooperation)

Combined = 6 dimensions
```

These 6 dimensions must be under **the same intention**.

If your intention is "get them to advance work," but their intention is actually "keep this job but don't want to work too hard," you're not in the same structure at all.

**Origins must coincide for the six dimensions to be meaningful.**

---

## What Happened in the Conversation

**You say**: "The project progress is a bit slow, do you have any difficulties on your end?"

This is your action (+): proactively advancing.

**They say**: "No difficulties, I'll complete it as soon as possible."

This is their attitude (+): showing cooperation.

But in the following two weeks, their action (-) still stays in place.

**You find them again**: "The progress we agreed on last time, why hasn't it moved?"

They start explaining: technical difficulties, unclear requirements, other tasks taking up time...

At this point you realize: **Their "attitude +" might not be genuinely positive, but superficial cooperation.**

Or rather, their attitude appears + to you (cooperative), but on their own side might be - (don't want to really commit).

**This is the key to subject-object structure: The same dimension can have different judgments from different perspectives.**

---

## Opposing Perspectives

Now switch to their perspective:

```
Structure in their eyes:

Your three dimensions:
- Your action: + (constantly pushing, they feel pressure)
- Your quality: + (have authority, they can't refuse)
- Your attitude: - (they feel you don't understand their situation)

Their own three dimensions:
- Their action: + (they feel they're already trying hard)
- Their quality: - (they feel the task is too difficult)
- Their attitude: + (they want to do well, but can't)
```

In their view, they are (+,-,+): enthusiastic but lacking capability.

And you in their eyes are (+,+,-): forceful but unsympathetic.

**Same relationship, two completely different structures. Structure isn't mutual description, but mutual projection.**

---

## Why Such Large Discrepancies?

Because you have inconsistent judgments on key dimensions:

**The dimension "their action":**

- Your judgment: - (not advancing)
- Their judgment: + (already trying hard)

**The dimension "their quality":**

- Your judgment: + (solid technical skills)
- Their judgment: - (task too difficult)

**The dimension "your attitude":**

- Your judgment: + (hope they improve)
- Their judgment: - (don't understand them)

**Core difficulty of subject-object structure: Both parties often have inconsistent judgments on the same dimension, yet each believes their own judgment is correct.**

---

## Where Is the Origin?

The deeper question is: Are your intentions truly aligned?

On the surface, your common intention is "complete the project."

But actually:

**Your intention:**

- Primary intention: Complete project on time
- Secondary intention: Help them grow

**Their intention:**

- Primary intention: Keep the job
- Secondary intention: Don't work too hard

If intentions differ, the origins of the six-dimensional structure aren't together.

You're evaluating "whether they're advancing the project" (origin: project progress).

They're evaluating "whether you'll fire them" (origin: job security).

**Two different origins, two different sets of evaluation standards.**

---

## The Essence of Relationship: Six-Dimensional Tension

Subject-object structure isn't simply six numbers side by side, but the **tension relationship** between these six dimensions.

**Where does tension come from?**

1. **Your expectations vs. their performance**
    
    - You expect them (+,+,+), but they are (-,+,+)
    - Gap creates tension
2. **Your actions vs. their feelings**
    
    - Your pushing (+), becomes pressure (-) on their side
    - Interaction creates tension
3. **Their capability self-assessment vs. your capability judgment**
    
    - You think their quality +, they think -
    - Cognitive difference creates tension

**Tension accumulates until a critical point, then a leap occurs. The critical point isn't a fixed threshold, but depends on the time window's length and tension difference.**

Possible leaps:

- They proactively resign
- You decide to fire them
- They suddenly burst out, expressing all problems
- You adjust strategy, giving them more support

The core of subject-object six-dimensional structure is that we're always accustomed to comparing what we consider useful elements between self and the external world, hoping to find parts we consider unreasonable.

---

## Six Dimensions Aren't Six Isolated Judgments

Important to understand: The six dimensions of subject-object structure are **a whole**.

It's not that you first judge "their action," then judge "their quality," then judge "their attitude," then judge "your action"...

Rather, in the interaction process, **six dimensions simultaneously play roles**:

- You push (your action +) → They feel pressure (affects their attitude)
- They explain (their attitude +) → You doubt (affects your judgment of their quality)
- You provide support (your quality +) → They still don't move (reinforces your judgment of their action -)

Six dimensions influence each other, forming a **dynamic game space**.

---

## An Important Preview: 64 Patterns

Before discussing leaps, we need to introduce a concept.

You just saw: subject-object structure has six dimensions, each person has 8 possible three-dimensional states.

The combination of two people is: 8 × 8 = **64 types**

We call these 64 subject-object combinations **64 Patterns**.

This isn't 64 types of "objective classification," but symbolic expression of the situation you're experiencing.

Detailed theory about 64 Patterns will be expanded in Chapter 25. For now, just know:

**64 Patterns are symbolic expressions of your current feeling about the relationship.**

Below we'll briefly explain their function, then continue discussing structure's dynamic changes.

## 64 Patterns: The Situation You're Experiencing

If each person has 8 possible three-dimensional states (2×2×2=8), then the combination of two people is:

8 × 8 = **64 types**

We call these 64 subject-object combinations **64 Patterns**.

But this isn't 64 types of "objective classification."

**64 Patterns are symbolic expressions of the situation you're experiencing.**

---

## Where Do 64 Patterns Come From

Your three months of frustration with that employee has long existed.

That puzzlement of "they clearly have capability and attitude, why just won't they work"—it's been in your head all along.

The function of 64 Patterns is: **Give this puzzlement a symbol**.

```
Vague puzzlement → Concretized as → You(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,+)
```

This symbol isn't for "more accuracy," but for **being able to discuss, compare, and analyze**.

You experienced one situation, I experienced another—if we don't have common language, we can't exchange experiences.

**64 Patterns are this common language.**

## Subjective Judgment Is What's Real

**In your eyes:** You(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,+)

**In their eyes:** Them(+,-,+) × You(+,+,-)

Both judgments are real.

64 Patterns isn't about finding "who's right who's wrong," but:

1. Acknowledging both experiences exist
2. Using symbols to express them
3. Seeing the tension between these two judgments

**This is why 64 Patterns are "snapshots formed by subjectively focused judgments".**

Not objective fact recording, but your current feeling about this situation.

---

## Similar Structures Have Similar Characteristics

**This is the value of 64 Patterns as a diagnostic tool.**

When you symbolize your situation as You(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,+), you can:

**1. Compare with other cases**

"I previously managed someone with this structure—how did it resolve?"

**2. Discover structural patterns**

All situations of (+,+,+) × (-,+,+), **even if these things seem completely unrelated**, might share common characteristics:

- Object has capability and willingness, but no action
- Subject is pushing, but can't push through
- Tension concentrates on "why no movement"

**3. Reference handling experience**

"How does this structure typically develop? What are the triggering factors for leaps?"

**This doesn't give you standard answers, but provides a reference system.**

---

## 64 Patterns Aren't Limited to Interpersonal Interactions

64 Patterns can be any subject-object relationship, depending on your intention and chosen dimensions:

**Interpersonal relationship: You × Colleague**

- Intention: Cooperate to complete project
- Dimensions: Action, Quality, aTtitude

**Investment decision: You × Project**

- Intention: Whether to invest
- Dimensions: Profit potential, Risk level, Market timing

**Business competition: Company × Competitor**

- Intention: Market share
- Dimensions: Product strength, Resources, Brand influence

**Self-reflection: Present me × Past me**

- Intention: Understand my own changes
- Dimensions: Cognition, Capability, Mindset

**The meaning of 64 Patterns depends entirely on:**

- What your intention is
- What three dimensions you choose

Same 64 Patterns, in different scenarios represent completely different things.

---

## 64 Patterns Change Over Time

**Key: 64 Patterns aren't eternal labels.**

```
[T0-T3 time window]: You(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,+)
[T3-T5 time window]: You(+,+,+) × Them(+,+,+)  ← A leap occurred
```

Same two people, in different time windows, your judgment of this relationship might be completely different.

**Judgment changes might be because:**

- Their performance changed
- Your perspective changed
- New information changed your cognition
- Time made you reassess

64 Patterns record **the state of your judgment within a certain time window**.

About this type of change (leap) mechanism, and the stable time range of judgment (time window), we'll discuss in detail in later chapters.

---

## How to Use 64 Patterns

**Wrong usage:** "Let me judge which pattern you are" ← Like attaching labels

**Correct usage:** "The feeling you described, structurally close to X, this type of situation usually..." ← Providing reference

**Specific steps:**

1. **You judge first yourself** "My current feeling is Me(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,+)"
2. **Compare with known cases** "Have I encountered this structure before?"
3. **Reference handling experience** "How do similar situations typically develop? What are possible leap directions?"
4. **Design your own strategy** "Combined with my specific situation, what can I do?"

**64 Patterns are tools, not conclusions.**

## Practical Application of 64 Patterns

**Case 1: Project Management**

```
Intention: Complete project
Dimensions: Progress, Resources, Cooperation willingness

[Week 1]:
My feeling: Us(+,+,+) × Them(-,+,-)
They have resources but unwilling to cooperate

[After introducing senior coordination]:
My feeling: Us(+,+,+) × Them(+,+,+)
Attitude changed
```

**Case 2: Investment Decision**

```
Intention: Whether to invest
Dimensions: Profit potential, Risk control, Timing judgment

[Research phase]:
My judgment: Me(+,-,+) × Project(-,+,-)
I'm optimistic about returns and timing but worried about risk
Project has controlled risk but unclear returns and bad timing

[After obtaining new data]:
My judgment: Me(+,+,+) × Project(+,+,+)
Can invest now
```

**The meaning of 64 Patterns differs in each scenario, but the way you use it to diagnose situations and reference experience is the same.**

---

## Reminder

64 Patterns aren't for "defining" relationships.

Wrong:

- "Their relationship is Pattern 17" (as if it's eternal)
- "This project belongs to Pattern 42" (as if it's objective)

Correct:

- "In this time window, I feel our relationship is close to Pattern 17"
- "At the current stage, this project appears to me to have characteristics of Pattern 42"

**64 Patterns are tools to help you see clearly what you're experiencing.**

Not telling you what you "should" experience, but making your experience visible, discussable, and comparable.

## This Is Just the Beginning

Subject-object structure lets you see the complete picture of relationships:

- Not just "how they are"
- Not just "how I am"
- But "the six-dimensional game between me and them under this intention"

But this isn't enough.

Because your judgment of relationships isn't static—it will **leap**.

That employee, today you feel they're (-,+,+), someday you might suddenly feel they're (+,+,+).

Or someday you completely lose hope, feeling they've become (-,-,-).

**What triggers changes in your judgment? How to anticipate possible leap directions? How to actively create favorable leaps?**

But this still isn't enough.

Because reality is more complex—you need to simultaneously handle multiple dimensions, multiple subjects, multiple time points.
