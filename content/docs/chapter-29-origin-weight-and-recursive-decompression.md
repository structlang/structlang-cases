## Introduction: A Seemingly Simple Question

Thus far, we've completed three major parts of the theoretical system:

- Basic Theory (Chapters 1-13)
- Application Development (Chapters 14-19)
- Meta-Theory (Chapters 20-27)

Now we enter Part Four: **Specialized Deepening**. These topics are independent of each other but all build on the previous theoretical foundation. You can choose what to read based on interest.

For the first topic, we'll explore a seemingly simple but actually complex question: **Do origins have hierarchical relationships?**

For instance, is survival more important than dignity? Is law more authoritative than business reputation? Among all possible judgment origins, is there a "root node" from which all other origins derive?

This question is more complex than it appears. This chapter explores it through two dimensions:

1. **Structural Level**: How intention drives origin selection
2. **Consensus Level**: Statistical patterns of origin weights

Ultimately we'll discover: structure is a network, but network nodes do have significant weight differences at the consensus level.

---

## Part One: Initial Hypothesis — The Survival Degradation Chain

### 1.1 Intuitive Appeal

An intuitive idea is: **all judgments can be traced back to the survival origin**.

```
Survival* (highest weight)
  ↓ Decompresses into
Quality of life dimension
  ↓ Decompresses into
[Comfort × Pain avoidance]
  ↓ Decompresses into
Comfort dimension
  ↓ Decompresses into
[Wealth × Power × Freedom]
```

Following this logic:

- I pursue wealth → for comfort → for quality of life → ultimately for survival
- I want dignity → for social status → for resource acquisition → ultimately for survival
- I seek justice → for social order → for safe environment → ultimately for survival

**This model's appeal**: It provides a simple hierarchical structure that seemingly explains all ultimate human motivations.

### 1.2 But Reality Doesn't Match

**Counter-example 1: Fake Dog Return Case**

In Chapter 6's fake dog return incident, my origin switching path was:

```
T0: Business reputation (get refund)
T2: Legal compliance (defend rights)
T3: Regulatory responsibility (hold accountable)
```

These three origins **are not decompressed from survival**. I didn't defend my rights because the dog affected my survival, but because "I was cheated and need to get justice back."

**Counter-example 2: Martyrs**

Throughout history, countless people have chosen faith, dignity, ideals over survival in life-or-death choices:

```
Wen Tianxiang: "Since ancient times, who has not died? Keep loyal heart to illuminate history"
Socrates: Refused to escape, chose to drink hemlock
Tan Sitong: "I laugh as I face the blade"
```

If survival is the only root node, these behaviors cannot be explained.

**Counter-example 3: Chronic Self-Destructive Behaviors**

Many people do things long-term that harm health:

- Smoking: <enjoyment> weight > <health> weight
- Staying up late: <completing work> weight > <sleep> weight
- Binge eating: <emotional relief> weight > <body burden> weight

If survival has highest weight, these behaviors shouldn't happen.

## Part Two: Mechanism Correction — Multi-Origin Recursive Network

### 2.1 Intention Determines Origin, Not Origin Determines Intention

**Core correction**: Not "survival → other origins" one-way degradation, but **intention determines which origin to start decompressing from**.

```
Scenario 1: Got cheated
Intention: <Get justice back>
→ Origin: Legal compliance
→ Judgment chain: Illegal → Regulation → Accountability

Scenario 2: Hungry
Intention: <Fill stomach>
→ Origin: Survival needs
→ Judgment chain: Food → Restaurant → Order

Scenario 3: Got insulted
Intention: <Maintain dignity>
→ Origin: Social status
→ Judgment chain: Counterattack → Clarify → Demand apology
```

**Key discoveries**:

- Current intention determines which origin to adopt
- Not all origins decompress from survival
- Multiple origins can coexist, weights compete

### 2.2 Intertwined Relationships Between Origins

Origins are not tree structure but **intertwined network**:

```
The concept "power" can be decompressed from multiple paths:

Path 1 (from survival):
Survival* → Quality → Comfort → [Wealth × Power]

Path 2 (from dignity):
Dignity* → Social status → [Recognition × Power]

Path 3 (from justice):
Justice* → Social order → Institution → [Power × Rules]
```

**This is why "power" has rich connotations**:

- It's a convergence point of multiple decompression paths
- Different paths give it different meanings
- In survival context it's "controlling resources"
- In dignity context it's "gaining respect"
- In justice context it's "enforcing rules"

### 2.3 Recursive Nature of Concept Creation

**Deeper discovery**: Decompression and compression are **alternating recursion**.

```
【Decompression Phase】
Survival* → Quality → Comfort → Wealth*

【Compression Phase】
Infinitely complex economic activities → Compressed into "wealth" concept point

【Re-decompression Phase】
Wealth* → [Capital × Assets × Liquidity]

【Re-compression】
Complex capital operations → Compressed into "capitalist*" label

【Re-decompression】
Capitalist* → [Exploiter* × Innovator* × Entrepreneur*]
```

**Each concept is both decompression result of previous layer and compression starting point of next layer.**

This is cognition's recursive nature:

- Compression creates concepts (naming phenomena)
- Decompression expands dimensions (understanding concept connotations)
- Expanded dimensions are compressed into new concepts again
- New concepts can continue to decompress
- Infinite loop

**This explains why**:

- "Capitalist" has rich connotations (multiple paths converge)
- Different people understand same word differently (different decompression paths)
- New concepts keep being created (recursive products of compression-decompression)

## Part Three: Weight Patterns at Consensus Level

### 3.1 Gun vs Lollipop

But here's a key observation: **Although structurally a multi-origin network, at consensus level there are indeed significant weight differences**.

**Experiment 1: Gun pointed at you**

```
Regardless of your current origin:
- Writing dissertation (academic value)
- Pursuing love (emotional fulfillment)
- Doing business (wealth accumulation)

Gun appears → Forced switch to survival origin
Weight: 0.99
Almost everyone will be forcibly switched
```

**Experiment 2: Given a lollipop**

```
Current origin: Work
Intention: <Complete project>
Weight: 0.7

Lollipop stimulus: <Eat candy>
Weight: 0.1

Result: Most people will ignore the lollipop
```

**What does this reveal?**

From statistical consensus perspective, origins indeed have weight hierarchy:

- Physiological threat (gun) → Weight 0.99
- Legal risk → Weight 0.7-0.9
- Moral pressure → Weight 0.5-0.7
- Business interests → Weight 0.3-0.6
- Small rewards (lollipop) → Weight 0.1-0.3

### 3.2 Why Survival Axis Has Highest Weight

From StructLang's three intention sources:

**(1) Physiological Drive's Compulsory Nature**

```
Characteristics of physiological threat:
- Compulsory: You cannot choose not to react
- Priority: Overrides all other intentions
- Immediacy: Instantly triggers origin switch

This is not "choice," it's physiological mechanism's forced override
```

**(2) Evolution's Hardcoded Foundation**

```
Why is survival weight high?

Because:
- Must be alive to do other things
- Death is irreversible
- Natural selection eliminated genes that "don't prioritize survival"

This is a hard constraint written in nervous system
```

**(3) Statistical Convergence of Consensus**

```
99% of people will prioritize survival under life-death threat
→ This is consensus-level pattern

1% may choose martyrdom
→ This is individual intention's extreme case
```

### 3.3 Cultural Differences in Consensus Weights

But even at consensus level, weight distribution is culturally influenced:

**Culture A (Confucian tradition)**:

```
Dignity (face) > Survival
"Scholar can be killed but not humiliated"
"Better die as jade than live as tile"
```

**Culture B (Pragmatism)**:

```
Survival > Dignity
"Keep green hills, not afraid of lacking firewood"
"Bad death not as good as wretched life"
```

**This shows**:

- Consensus weights are statistical patterns, not absolute laws
- Culture, experience, education all influence weight distribution
- Survival axis "usually" has highest weight, but "not always"

## Part Four: Unified Framework — Dual-Layer Model

### 4.1 Distinguishing Two Levels

Now we can establish a unified framework:

**(1) Structural Level: Multi-Origin Network**

```
Mechanism:
- Intention drives origin selection
- Origins can intertwine
- Weights determined by current context
- No fixed unique root node

Expression:
Survival* ⇄ Dignity* ⇄ Justice* ⇄ Wealth* ⇄ Meaning*
  ↓        ↓          ↓         ↓         ↓
Quality  Status     Rights    Capital    Value
  ↓        ↓          ↓         ↓         ↓
[Comfort×Pain] [Recognition×Power] [Fairness×Process] [Assets×Liquidity] [Contribution×Creation]

This is a network, nodes have edges, weights change dynamically
```

**(2) Consensus Level: Weight Patterns**

```
Statistical pattern:
Most people, most of the time:
Survival axis > Legal axis > Moral axis > Business axis > Social axis

This is descriptive pattern, not structural constraint
```

### 4.2 Relationship Between Two Layers

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  Consensus Level: Statistical Weight Patterns  │
│                                  │
│  Survival(0.9) > Law(0.7) > ... │
│  ↑This is "most people, most of the time" pattern │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
           ↓ Based on
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  Structural Level: Multi-Origin Dynamic Network │
│                                  │
│  Intention → Origin selection → Weight competition │
│  ↑This is "each individual, each judgment" mechanism │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Key understanding**:

- Consensus weights are emergent patterns of structural mechanism at large sample
- Each individual judgment still follows intention-driven mechanism
- Statistical patterns don't change structural essence
- But understanding statistical patterns is important for predicting behavior

### 4.3 Why Both Are Important

**Problem with only structural level**:

```
"Theoretically all origins equal, so..."
→ Ignores survival threat's compulsory nature
→ Cannot explain why gun more effective than lollipop
→ Prediction fails
```

**Problem with only consensus level**:

```
"Survival most important, so everything for survival"
→ Cannot explain martyrdom, chronic self-destruction, cultural differences
→ Treats statistical pattern as absolute law
→ Loses ability to explain extreme cases
```

**Advantages of dual-layer model**:

```
Structural level: Explains individual differences, extreme cases, mechanism essence
Consensus level: Predicts most cases, designs effective interventions, understands cultural patterns

Both combined are complete
```

## Part Five: Practical Applications

### 5.1 Understanding Forced Switching

**Under what conditions can you forcibly switch others' origins?**

```
Necessary conditions:
1. Threat weight > current intention weight
2. Threat is immediate, irreversible
3. Threat activates physiological drive (highest compulsion)

Typical cases:
- Life threat (gun) → 0.99 weight
- Legal risk (prison) → 0.7-0.9 weight
- Superior accountability (mayor's hotline) → 0.8 weight
- Economic loss → 0.3-0.6 weight (depends on amount)
```

**Application in fake dog case**:

```
T4: Mayor's hotline threat

Why effective?
- Official's <stall> intention weight: 0.4
- Superior accountability threat weight: 0.9
- 0.9 > 0.4 → Forced switch

Why not survival threat?
- Don't need 0.99 to switch
- 0.9 legal/administrative threat already enough
```

### 5.2 Designing Effective Interventions

**Design intervention strategies based on dual-layer model**:

**Strategy 1: Utilize consensus weights (for most people)**

```
Goal: Change organizational behavior
Method: Introduce high-weight origins

Examples:
- Want companies to value environment → Introduce legal risk (0.8 weight)
- Want individuals to change habits → Introduce health crisis (0.7 weight)
- Want team to value quality → Introduce customer loss (0.6 weight)
```

**Strategy 2: Understand individual differences (for special cases)**

```
Goal: Handle extreme situations
Method: Identify individual's actual origin weights

Examples:
- For some, dignity > survival (need different communication approach)
- For some, creation > wealth (use achievement not bonus as motivation)
- For some, freedom > stability (provide flexibility not security)
```

### 5.3 Cross-Cultural Communication

**Understanding cultural weight differences**:

```
Scenario: US-China business negotiation

Chinese side common weight order:
Relationships > Face > Long-term interests > Short-term interests

US side common weight order:
Efficiency > Interests > Rules > Relationships

Conflict point:
- Chinese side wants to establish relationship first (weight 0.8)
- US side wants to discuss specific terms first (weight 0.7)
→ Both sides feel other party "not focusing on business"

Solution:
- Understand other party's weight ordering
- Create solutions satisfying both sides' high-weight origins
```

## Part Six: Theoretical Significance

### 6.1 Contribution to Cognitive Science

**Beyond traditional hierarchy of needs theory**:

Maslow's hierarchy of needs:

```
     Self-actualization
       ↑
     Esteem needs
       ↑
     Social needs
       ↑
     Safety needs
       ↑
     Physiological needs
```

Problems:

- Assumes fixed hierarchy
- Cannot explain "sacrifice life for righteousness"
- Treats statistical pattern as absolute law

**Dual-layer model's improvement**:

```
Structural level:
No fixed hierarchy, intention drives origin selection

Consensus level:
Most cases physiological needs have highest weight
But individuals and cultures can have different orderings
```

### 6.2 Inspiration for Decision Theory

**Traditional rational choice theory**:

```
Assumption: People always maximize utility

Problems:
- What is "utility"?
- Utility function differs in different contexts
- Cannot explain seemingly "irrational" behaviors
```

**Dual-layer model perspective**:

```
Rationality = Maximizing weight under given origin

But:
- Origin determined by intention
- Intention driven by physiology, external, experience
- Weights have consensus patterns but also individual differences

So:
- "Irrational" behavior may just be different origin
- Understanding origin more important than judging rationality
```

### 6.3 Application to Social Sciences

**Understanding collective behavior**:

```
Group behavior = Composite effect of individual origins × consensus weights

Example 1: Panic buying
- Individual origin: Survival anxiety (0.8 weight)
- Consensus amplification: Herd effect
- Result: Irrational collective behavior

Example 2: Moral movement
- Individual origin: Sense of justice (0.6 weight)
- Consensus amplification: Social identity
- Result: High-intensity collective action

Understanding:
- Not that crowds "lose rationality"
- But specific origin collectively activated
- Weight amplified through social interaction
```

## Conclusion: Network Structure, Weight Patterns

### Core Insights

1. **Structural Essence**: Origins are multi-center network, not tree hierarchy
    
    - Intention drives origin selection
    - Origins can intertwine, recurse
    - No fixed unique root node
2. **Consensus Patterns**: Weights have statistical differences, survival axis has highest weight in most cases
    
    - Gun can forcibly switch everyone
    - Lollipop can almost never forcibly switch
    - This is result of evolution, physiology, statistics
3. **Recursive Mechanism**: Decompression and compression alternate, creating concept network
    
    - Compression creates concepts
    - Decompression expands dimensions
    - Dimensions compressed into new concepts again
    - Infinite recursion
4. **Dual-Layer Unity**: Understanding behavior requires both structural mechanism and statistical patterns
    
    - Structure explains individuals and extremes
    - Statistics predict masses and norms
    - Both indispensable

### Precise Formulation

**From consensus perspective**: There exists obvious weight hierarchy, survival axis has highest weight in vast majority of cases, with strongest forced switching capability.

**From structural perspective**: Intention is multi-origin network, weights determined by physiological drive, cultural experience, current context, no fixed unique root node.

**Unified statement**: Origins are weighted network structure, inter-node weights have statistical patterns at consensus level (survival usually highest), but at structural level dynamically determined by intention, allowing individual differences and extreme cases.

### Final Metaphor

Imagine a spider web:

- Each node is an origin
- Nodes have silk threads connecting (can reach each other)
- Each silk thread has elasticity (weight variable)
- Most of the time, center node (survival) threads thickest (weight highest)
- But in special cases, edge node threads can suddenly thicken (intention switch)
- Spider (cognitive subject) can be on any node (flexible origin)
- Entire web maintains dynamic balance amid vibration (recursive adjustment)

This is the complete picture of origin weight and recursive decompression:

- Structurally a network
- Statistically patterned
- Dynamically balanced
- Understanding requires dual-layer perspective
