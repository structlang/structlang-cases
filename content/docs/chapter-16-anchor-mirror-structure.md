## Introduction: More Than One Method

In Chapter 5, we introduced **subject-object structure**, a parallel method:

- Establish my position (subject)
- Establish other's position (object)
- Six dimensions unfold simultaneously
- Symmetrical analysis

At that time, we mentioned that in actual use, many people encounter confusion:

**"Why must I use elements like action/quality/attitude to establish dimensions? Why must I use this fixed subject-object structure?"**

Yes, we never said methods are limited to subject-object structure and action/quality/attitude elements. This is purely for beginner convenience. Any method that works according to human thinking patterns is a good method, like the method established in Chapter 14.

This chapter introduces **another way to establish subject-object relationships: the anchoring-mirror method**.

**Core idea:**

- Not unfolding both sides simultaneously
- But **first anchor one side, the other side automatically mirrors**
- Whoever is anchored first dominates the interpretation path

This is **another tool** besides the subject-object parallel method.

## Section 1: What is Anchoring-Mirror Structure

### 1.1 Definition

> **Anchoring-mirror structure**: First fix one party's position on the six-layer chessboard (anchoring), the other party inevitably falls into mirror position (mirroring), forming a comparison relationship.

**Two key actions:**

**1. Anchoring**

- Judge who is the initiator of the situation
- Fix this party on the most critical line
- Establish this line as the dominant line

**2. Mirroring**

- Once the primary position is determined, the other party automatically falls into mirror position
- No additional selection needed
- Forms natural comparison relationship

**Core insight:**

**Situation interpretation isn't naturally symmetrical, but determined by "who is anchored first" as primary, deciding the entire deduction.**

### 1.2 Difference from Subject-Object Parallel Method

|Dimension|Subject-Object Parallel Method|Anchoring-Mirror Method|
|---|---|---|
|Establishment method|Both sides determined simultaneously, symmetric unfolding|First anchor one side, other side automatically mirrors|
|Starting point|Clear "my" position|Judge "who is the initiator"|
|Emphasis|Symmetry|Starting bias|
|Applicable scenario|I'm participant, clear position|Quick entry, or need to emphasize dominant party|
|Flexibility|Six dimensions freely combinable|Three comparison lines, main line decides setup|
|Typical case|Me (subject) vs customer (object)|Interview: candidate initiates vs company examines|
|Core question|"My six dimensions vs other's six dimensions"|"Who dominates this situation?"|
|Analysis focus|Specific state of each dimension|Coordination of three comparison lines|

**Key difference:**

- **Parallel method**: Emphasizes "me" and "other" equally important, six dimensions symmetric
- **Anchoring method**: Emphasizes "who moves first" determines situation interpretation path, has dominance

**Not opposing, but complementary:**

- When "my" position is clear → use parallel method
- When need to quickly judge "who dominates" → use anchoring method
- When need to emphasize certain comparison line → use anchoring method

### 1.3 Why Anchoring-Mirror Method Needed

**Scenario 1: Uncertain who is subject**

```
Company A and Company B negotiating, I need to analyze this situation.
Question: Who should I treat as subject?

Parallel method: Need to first determine whose perspective I'm on
Anchoring method: Directly judge "who initiated negotiation" → anchor initiator
```

**Scenario 2: Need to emphasize dominant party**

```
Interview scenario: Candidate vs company
Question: Who dominates this situation?

Parallel method: Can analyze, but dominance not obvious
Anchoring method: Anchor candidate on action line (they actively present) → clear at a glance
```

**Scenario 3: Quick entry analysis**

```
Need to quickly judge situation
Question: Six dimensions too complex

Parallel method: Need to unfold complete six dimensions
Anchoring method: First anchor main line → other two lines as auxiliary → quick focus
```

**Value of anchoring-mirror method: Provides a "starting bias" perspective.**

### 1.4 Difference from Chapter 14 Method

In Chapter 14, we introduced **"Foundation-Interaction-Target" three-layer compression**, used for analyzing events from external observer perspective.

This chapter's **"Quality line-Action line-Attitude line" three comparison lines** is a method for establishing subject-object relationships from participant perspective.

**Core difference:**

|Dimension|Chapter 14 Method|This Chapter Method|
|---|---|---|
|**Perspective**|External observer|One of participants|
|**Purpose**|Judge whether event can succeed|Establish subject-object relationship|
|**Grouping logic**|Foundation(premise)-Interaction(process)-Target(result)|Quality(hardware)-Action(present)-Attitude(willingness)|
|**Applicable scenario**|Observe external structure evolution|I'm active party striking|
|**Output**|Event feasibility judgment|Subject-object relationship positioning|

**Example illustration:**

- **Using Chapter 14 method**: I observe Company A and Company B negotiate, judge whether this cooperation can succeed
- **Using this chapter method**: I go to interview/sell, judge how my subject-object relationship with other party is established

**Can combine both:**

```
Step 1: Use Chapter 14 method to observe external structure
    ↓
Judge power comparison and event possibility
    ↓
Step 2: If I want to intervene, use this chapter method
    ↓
Quickly anchor my position, establish subject-object relationship
```

Both methods aren't opposing, but **tool choices for different scenarios**.

## Section 2: Six-Layer Chessboard and Three Comparison Lines

### 2.1 Structure of Six-Layer Chessboard

In anchoring-mirror method, six dimensions are reorganized into **three comparison lines**:

```
Upper layer: Attitude/stance/rules
  Primary attitude ↔ Other's attitude
  [Attitude line]

Middle layer: Action/interaction/game  
  Primary action ↔ Other's action
  [Action line]

Lower layer: Resources/conditions/quality
  Primary resources ↔ Other's resources
  [Quality line]
```

**Important note: Difference in dimension numbering**

This chapter's grouping method differs from Chapter 5:

- **Chapter 5**: Dimensions 1-3 are subject, dimensions 4-6 are object (symmetric unfolding)
- **This chapter**: Doesn't emphasize 1-6 numbering, but emphasizes **pairing relationship of three comparison lines**
    - Quality line: Primary resources ↔ Other's resources
    - Action line: Primary action ↔ Other's action
    - Attitude line: Primary attitude ↔ Other's attitude

This difference is because: **Anchoring-mirror method focuses on "who is anchored first", not "who is subject"**.

**Why group this way?**

This matches normal people's straightforward thinking patterns:

- **Lower layer**: Basic conditions (what you have)
- **Middle layer**: Present game (what you do)
- **Upper layer**: Rules stance (what you think)

**Logical relationship of three lines:**

```
Quality line: Prerequisite conditions
    ↓
Action line: Present game
    ↓  
Attitude line: Final decision
```

### 2.2 Meaning of Three Comparison Lines

#### Quality Line (1↔4)

**Corresponding content:**

- Primary: Resume, qualifications, capability, resources
- Other: Position requirements, standards, expectations

**Function:**

- Judge basic matching degree
- If quality line gap too large, other two lines won't work even if good
- This is "hard conditions"

**Interview case:**

- Candidate 1: 3 years experience, prestigious school background
- Company 4: Requires 5 years experience, 985 background
- Quality line: Barely matches (slightly insufficient)

#### Action Line (2↔5)

**Corresponding content:**

- Primary: On-site performance, interaction method, presentation ability
- Other: Examination method, questioning rhythm, feedback

**Function:**

- This is the main battlefield of present game
- Action line performance can compensate for quality line insufficiency
- This is "soft power"

**Interview case:**

- Candidate 2: Clear answers, rigorous logic, sincere attitude
- Company 5: Professional questions, good rhythm control, has feedback
- Action line: Smooth interaction (++)

#### Attitude Line (3↔6)

**Corresponding content:**

- Primary: Motivation, mentality, expectations, values
- Other: Cultural preferences, salary framework, team atmosphere

**Function:**

- This is the decisive factor for final decision
- Even if first two lines good, attitude line mismatch will collapse
- This is "values"

**Interview case:**

- Candidate 3: Expects 20K monthly salary, pursues growth
- Company 6: Budget 15K, emphasizes overtime culture
- Attitude line: Conflict (--)

### 2.3 Coordination of Three Lines

Three lines **aren't independent**, but mutually influence:

**Positive coordination:**

```
Quality line matches (++)
    ↓
Action line smooth (++) → Enhance confidence
    ↓
Attitude line fits (++) → Final achievement
```

**Negative transmission:**

```
Quality line insufficient (--)
    ↓
Action line pressure big (-) → Performance fails
    ↓
Attitude line affected (-) → Final abandonment
```

**Compensation relationship:**

```
Quality line slightly insufficient (+)
    ↓
Action line super strong (++) → Compensate quality
    ↓
Attitude line fits (++) → Still achieve

Or:

Quality line excellent (++)
    ↓
Action line average (+) → Pass with quality
    ↓
Attitude line fits (++) → Achieve
```

**Conflict relationship:**

```
First two lines both good (++++)
    ↓
But attitude line conflicts (--)
    ↓
Final collapse (attitude line veto)
```

**Key insight:**

**Action line is most flexible**, can compensate quality, can show attitude, is "main battlefield".

### 2.4 How to Choose Which Line to Anchor

**Anchoring principle: Whoever initiates situation, anchor them on which line.**

**Judgment standards:**

|Scenario type|Initiator characteristics|Anchoring position|Reason|
|---|---|---|---|
|Interview|Candidate actively presents|Action line 2|Candidate must "perform"|
|Sales|Salesperson actively strikes|Action line 2|Salesperson must "persuade"|
|Negotiation|Party proposing conditions|Action line 2|Who proposes first is active|
|Bidding|Bidder actively competes|Action line 2|Bidder must "compete"|
|Job seeking|Job seeker actively submits|Quality line 1|Emphasize "what I have"|
|Blind date|Party actively asking to meet|Attitude line 3|Emphasize "my willingness"|
|Cooperation|Party proposing cooperation plan|Action line 2|Plan is action|

**General pattern:**

- **Most cases anchor on action line 2** (because most situations are "one party actively strikes")
- **If emphasizing resource advantage, anchor on quality line 1** (e.g., famous company executive job seeking)
- **If emphasizing attitude willingness, anchor on attitude line 3** (e.g., pursuer confessing)

**After anchoring, other party automatically mirrors:**

- Anchor at 2 → Other at 5
- Anchor at 1 → Other at 4
- Anchor at 3 → Other at 6

## Section 3: Complete Case—Interview Situation

### 3.1 Scenario Setting

**Roles:**

- Candidate Zhang San: 3 years work experience, wants to switch to an internet company
- Company HR + technical interviewer: Recruiting a product manager

**Interview process:**

1. HR initial screening (20 minutes)
2. Technical interview (1 hour)
3. Final salary negotiation (30 minutes)

**We'll use anchoring-mirror method to analyze this interview situation.**

### 3.2 Anchoring: Candidate on Action Line 2

**Why anchor candidate?**

Because interview is initiated by candidate:

- Candidate submits resume (initiate)
- Candidate comes to site (active)
- Candidate needs to present self (action)

**Why anchor on action line 2?**

Because interview's core is **candidate's on-site performance**:

- Answer questions
- Present abilities
- Express willingness

**After anchoring, other automatically mirrors:**

```
Candidate (primary): Anchored on action line 2
    ↓
Company (other): Automatically mirrors on action line 5
    ↓
Form comparison: Candidate presents ↔ Company examines
```

**This is interview's essence: Forced interaction line 2↔5.**

### 3.3 Complete Unfolding of Six-Layer Chessboard

#### Initial State (Before Interview)

**Quality Line (1↔4):**

```
Candidate 1:
- 3 years product experience
- Led 2 projects
- Second-tier internet company background
- Resume okay, but not outstanding
State: + (Barely passing)

Company 4:
- Requires 3-5 years experience
- Needs independent project leadership ability
- Preferably big company background
State: + (Standards not too high, but have expectations)

Quality line comparison: +↔+ (Basic match, slightly insufficient)
```

**Action Line (2↔5):**

```
Candidate 2 (anchoring point):
- Haven't started interview yet
- Prepared portfolio
- A bit nervous
State: ? (To be revealed)

Company 5:
- Designed standard interview process
- Technical interviewer experienced
- Will examine project details
State: + (Well prepared)

Action line comparison: ?↔+ (Company takes initiative)
```

**Attitude Line (3↔6):**

```
Candidate 3:
- Expects 18K-20K monthly salary
- Hopes for growth space
- Values: Pursue product experience
State: + (Clear motivation)

Company 6:
- Budget 15K-18K
- Team atmosphere good
- Culture: Fast iteration, can accept overtime
State: + (Attractive, but salary may not be enough)

Attitude line comparison: +↔+ (Has fit possibility, but salary gap exists)
```

**Initial structure judgment:**

```
Quality line: +↔+ (Basic match)
Action line: ?↔+ (Company active, candidate to reveal)
Attitude line: +↔+ (Has fit possibility)

Overall: Has chance, but key depends on action line performance
```

#### Mid-term Evolution (During Technical Interview)

**Action line changes:**

```
Candidate 2 (reveal):
- Clear answers, rigorous logic
- Present portfolio, detailed cases
- Actively ask about team situation, show genuine interest
State: ++ (Exceed expectations)

Company 5:
- Interviewer frequently nods
- Asked several deep questions
- Gave positive feedback
State: ++ (Satisfied)

Action line comparison: ++↔++ (Smooth interaction, mutual bonus)
```

**Action line enhancement → Quality line re-estimation:**

```
Candidate 1 (re-estimated):
- Although background average, on-site performance shows strong ability
- Interviewer re-examines resume: "May have underestimated him"
State: + → ++ (Action line bonus)

Company 4:
- Standards remain unchanged
State: +

Quality line comparison: ++↔+ (Candidate pulled up)
```

**Attitude line no obvious change yet, waiting for final stage.**

#### Final Stage (Salary Negotiation)

**Critical moment of attitude line:**

```
Candidate 3:
- Expects 20K
- States "Value growth space more, salary can appropriately compromise"
State: ++ (Flexible and sincere)

Company 6:
- Budget 18K ceiling
- HR says: "Your performance very good, we really want you, but budget really only 18K"
State: + (Has willingness, but budget stuck)

Attitude line comparison: ++↔+ (Candidate flexible, but gap exists)
```

**Attitude line leap:**

```
Candidate's choice:
A. Insist on 20K → May break down
B. Accept 18K → Achieve

Candidate thinks:
"Action line performance very good, company also satisfied (++↔++)
Quality line re-estimated, I have chips (++↔+)
But attitude line has gap, if insist may collapse
18K also acceptable, growth space more important"

Decision: Accept 18K

Attitude line comparison: ++↔++ (Reach consensus)
```

**Final structure (Offer issued):**

```
Quality line: ++↔+ (Candidate overestimated through action line)
Action line: ++↔++ (Both satisfied)
Attitude line: ++↔++ (Reach agreement)

Result: Offer issued, candidate accepts
```

### 3.4 Coordination and Leap of Three Lines

**Coordination 1: Action line compensates quality line**

```
Initial: Quality line slightly insufficient (+↔+)
    ↓
Action line super strong (++)
    ↓
Quality line re-estimated (++↔+)
    ↓
Candidate chips increase
```

**Coordination 2: Action line paves way for attitude line**

```
Action line performs well (++↔++)
    ↓
Company "really wants you"
    ↓
When attitude line negotiates, candidate has confidence
    ↓
Finally even if gap, can negotiate
```

**Leap point: Flexibility of attitude line**

```
If candidate insists on 20K:

Attitude line: ++↔+ → --↔- (Conflict)
    ↓
Even if first two lines good
    ↓
Final collapse

Candidate chooses 18K:

Attitude line: ++↔+ → ++↔++ (Consensus)
    ↓
Three lines all pass
    ↓
Achieve
```

**Key insight:**

**Interview is forced interaction 2↔5, but final decision at 3↔6.**

If attitude line conflicts, first two lines won't work even if good.

### 3.5 Interview from Anchoring-Mirror Method Perspective

**Why is anchoring candidate on action line 2 effective?**

Because this perspective **directly grasps interview's essence**:

```
Interview = Candidate presents (2) ↔ Company examines (5)

Other two lines auxiliary:
- Quality line (1↔4): Prerequisite conditions
- Attitude line (3↔6): Final decision
```

**If using subject-object parallel method:**

```
Subject (me): 1-2-3
Object (company): 4-5-6

Need six dimensions fully unfolded to see clearly
```

**Using anchoring-mirror method:**

```
Anchor candidate at 2 → Company automatically at 5
Main line: Action line (2↔5)
Auxiliary: Quality line (1↔4), attitude line (3↔6)

Clear at a glance
```

**This is value of anchoring-mirror method: Quick focus on main line.**

## Section 4: Operational Methods

Based on interview case, we can extract operational points of anchoring-mirror method.

### 4.1 Three-Stage Strategy

#### Before Entry: Fill Quality Line

**Quality line is prerequisite, must reach minimum standard.**

**Operation points:**

1. **Align keywords**
    
    - Resume matches position requirement keywords
    - Describe own experience in other's language
2. **Prepare proof materials**
    
    - Portfolio, project cases
    - Quantified results (data speaks)
3. **Reduce expectation gap**
    
    - If quality line obviously insufficient, explain in advance "why I can be competent"
    - Don't let other discover gap only during interview

**Interview case:**

Zhang San prepares in advance:

- Resume highlights "independently responsible for 2 projects" (align position requirement)
- Portfolio contains detailed product logic (prove ability)
- In resume states "although only 3 years experience, but project complexity high" (reduce expectation gap)

**Quality line filled to + or above, then qualified for action line game.**

#### During Interview: Seize Action Line

**Action line is main battlefield, determines quality line re-estimation.**

**Operation points:**

1. **Follow other's rhythm**
    
    - Company at 5 (examine), I at 2 (present)
    - Don't disrupt other's questioning rhythm
    - Answers should hit the point
2. **Present not promote**
    
    - Speak with cases, not empty words
    - Let other reach conclusion "this person good" themselves
3. **Two-way interaction**
    
    - Not one-way answering
    - Appropriately ask questions, show interest and thinking
4. **Observe feedback, adjust strategy**
    
    - If other frequently nods → Continue deepening
    - If other frowns → Change angle or simplify

**Interview case:**

Zhang San's operation:

- Interviewer asks project details → Answer using STAR method (Situation-Task-Action-Result)
- When presenting portfolio, not "I did what", but "this product solved what problem"
- Actively ask "what's team's current challenge" → Show genuine interest

**Action line performs well, can pull quality line from + to ++.**

#### At End: Handle Attitude Line

**Attitude line is final decision, should show fit and flexibility.**

**Operation points:**

1. **Clear expectations, but stay flexible**
    
    - State own expectations (salary, growth)
    - But indicate "can discuss"
2. **Emphasize value fit**
    
    - "What I value is ___, this aligns with your company's culture"
    - Make other feel "this person suits us"
3. **Give steps, don't kill conversation**
    
    - If gap exists, don't directly refuse
    - Can say "I need to think about it"
4. **Observe other's budget space**
    
    - If other says "budget is just this much" → May really have no space
    - If other says "I'll try to fight for more" → Have space

**Interview case:**

Zhang San's operation:

- "I expect 20K, but value growth space more" (Clear expectation, stay flexible)
- "I very much identify with your company's fast iteration culture" (Value fit)
- When HR says only 18K, didn't immediately refuse, but said "this salary slightly lower than expected, but if there's clear growth path, I can accept" (Give steps)

**Attitude line handled well, can finally achieve.**

### 4.2 Three-Line Scheduling Strategy

**Core principle: Use advantage line to compensate weak line.**

#### Strategy 1: Action line compensates quality line

```
Quality line insufficient (+)
    ↓
Action line super strong (++)
    ↓
Make other re-estimate quality line
    ↓
"Although less experience, but strong ability"
```

**Applicable scenarios:**

- Newcomer job seeking (insufficient experience, but strong potential)
- Career switcher (background mismatch, but strong learning ability)

#### Strategy 2: Quality line supports action line

```
Quality line super strong (++)
    ↓
Action line average (+)
    ↓
Pass with quality
    ↓
"Prestigious school background/big company experience"
```

**Applicable scenarios:**

- High-end talent (hard background, don't need excessive presentation)
- Scarce positions (supply less than demand)

#### Strategy 3: Attitude line rescue

```
First two lines both average (+)
    ↓
Attitude line super strong (++)
    ↓
"I really want to come, can accept below-market salary"
    ↓
Other moved by sincerity
```

**Applicable scenarios:**

- Especially want to enter certain company
- Position competition fierce

#### Strategy 4: Loss prevention strategy

```
Three lines keep at least one (++)
    ↓
Other two lines can moderately compromise
    ↓
But if all three are (-)
    ↓
Better to actively give up
```

**Bottom line:**

- Quality line (-) + action line (-) + attitude line (-) = Must lose
- At least ensure one line strong, then have negotiation space

### 4.3 Practical Checklist

```
[Pre-Interview Check]

□ Quality line (1↔4)
  □ Are resume keywords aligned with position requirements?
  □ Have proof materials prepared (works/cases)?
  □ Are there obvious shortcomings? How to make up?

□ Action line (2↔5)
  □ Understand interview process?
  □ Prepared common question answers?
  □ Prepared questions to ask other?

□ Attitude line (3↔6)
  □ Clear about own expectations (salary/growth)?
  □ Understand other's culture and budget?
  □ Have compromise space? Where's bottom line?

[During Interview Observation]

□ Action line feedback
  □ Does other frequently nod/ask details? (Good signal)
  □ Does other frown/interrupt? (Bad signal)
  □ Does other give positive feedback? (Good signal)

□ Quality line re-estimation
  □ Does other say "your experience richer than resume looks"? (Pulled up)
  □ Does other say "this project not complex"? (Pulled down)

□ Attitude line probe
  □ Does other actively talk salary? (Has interest)
  □ Does other ask "are you interviewing other companies"? (Want to confirm)

[Post-Interview Review]

□ Actual state of three lines
  Quality line: ___ (As expected?)
  Action line: ___ (How performed? Where better?)
  Attitude line: ___ (Showed fit?)

□ If didn't pass, stuck at which line?
  □ Quality line (insufficient experience/ability)
  □ Action line (performance failed/didn't explain clearly)
  □ Attitude line (expectation mismatch/culture incompatible)

□ Next improvement
  ___
```

## Section 5: Methodology Summary

### 5.1 When Parallel, When Anchoring

**Decision tree:**

```
Question 1: Am I participant?

Yes → Question 2
No → Use third-party perspective (Chapter 14)

Question 2: Is my position clear?

Yes → Use subject-object parallel method
No → Question 3

Question 3: Who's situation's initiator?

Clear → Use anchoring-mirror method (anchor initiator)
Unclear → First judge initiator, then decide

Question 4: Need to emphasize dominance?

Yes → Use anchoring-mirror method
No → Use subject-object parallel method
```

**Simplified version:**

|Scenario|Recommended method|Reason|
|---|---|---|
|I'm clearly subject, need to guide my actions|Subject-object parallel method|Position clear|
|I'm external observer|Third-party perspective|Not participant|
|Need to quickly judge who dominates|Anchoring-mirror method|Emphasize starting bias|
|Need to emphasize certain comparison line|Anchoring-mirror method|Three-line structure clearer|
|Need to comprehensively analyze six dimensions|Subject-object parallel method|Symmetric unfolding more complete|
|Interview, sales, negotiation etc "one party active"|Anchoring-mirror method|Naturally anchor initiator|

**Two methods can combine:**

```
Step 1: Use anchoring-mirror method for quick entry
    ↓
Determine main line (action line/quality line/attitude line)
    ↓
Step 2: Use subject-object parallel method for deep analysis
    ↓
Unfold complete six dimensions, detail each dimension
```

### 5.2 Applicable Scope of Anchoring-Mirror Method

**Suitable scenarios:**

1. **Clearly have "active party" situations**
    
    - Interview (candidate active)
    - Sales (salesperson active)
    - Negotiation (proposing party active)
    - Pursuit (pursuer active)
2. **Need to emphasize "dominance"**
    
    - Who dominates this situation?
    - Whose action more critical?
3. **Need "quick focus"**
    
    - Don't need six dimensions fully unfolded
    - Seize main line sufficient

**Unsuitable scenarios:**

1. **Both parties completely equal status**
    
    - Equal cooperation
    - Two-way selection (neither clear initiator)
2. **Need comprehensive analysis**
    
    - Complex strategic decisions
    - Need dimension-by-dimension detailed analysis
3. **My position very clear**
    
    - I'm the subject, directly using subject-object parallel method more natural

### 5.3 Openness of StructLang

**Core cognition:**

StructLang isn't a closed axiomatic system, but an **open toolbox**.

**Existing methods:**

- Subject-object parallel method (Chapter 5)
- Third-party perspective (Chapter 14)
- Superior-subordinate grouping (Chapter 14)
- Foundation-interaction-target (Chapter 14)
- Anchoring-mirror method (this chapter)
- Other new methods you create yourself

**Key principle:**

**Not "which method correct", but "which method suits current intention".**

Like same cube:

- From front, is square
- From side, is square
- From corner, is hexagon

**Six-dimensional structure is that cube, different methods are different observation angles.**

**StructLang cares not how you construct, but whether constructed structure can explain situation, guide action.**

### 5.4 Method Selection and Combination

**Single method:**

- Simple scenario: Choose one method sufficient
- Interview, sales → Anchoring-mirror method
- My decision → Subject-object parallel method
- Observe external → Third-party perspective

**Combination methods:**

- Complex scenario: Combine multiple methods

**Example: External structure affects my situation**

```
Step 1: Use third-party perspective to analyze external structure
    ↓
Company A and Company B negotiating (superior-subordinate grouping: -+, imbalance structure)
    ↓
Step 2: Use subject-object parallel method to analyze my situation
    ↓
Me (subject) vs B (object)
    ↓
Step 3: Predict which dimension external structure affects me
    ↓
Adjust strategy
```

**Example: I actively strike situation**

```
Step 1: Use anchoring-mirror method for quick positioning
    ↓
I'm initiator, anchor on action line 2
    ↓
Step 2: Use subject-object parallel method to detail
    ↓
Unfold six dimensions, formulate detailed strategy
    ↓
Step 3: Execute and monitor
```

**Method selection and combination depends on:**

1. Current intention
2. Information sufficiency
3. Time window
4. Personal habits
