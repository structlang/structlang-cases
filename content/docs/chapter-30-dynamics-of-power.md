## Introduction: Where Does Power Come From?

In Chapter 28, we explored origin weight and recursive decompression—how intention drives origin selection and the statistical patterns of weight at the consensus level. Origin weight explains the strength of individual intention. But in multi-agent relationships, weight differences generate power.

In Chapter 18, we discussed "origin as power"—manipulating judgments by controlling cognitive origins. That was a technique of cognitive manipulation. This chapter addresses a deeper structural power: how power emerges from relational structures themselves.

Let's return to the fake dog return case from Chapter 6:

**Initial State**: I wanted a refund, the merchant refused. We reached an impasse.

**Key Breakthrough**:

- I discovered the merchant's business license lacked "pet sales" scope (legal loophole)
- I found the manager's slip exposed his knowledge ("that's how this industry works")
- I threatened to call the mayor's hotline

**Result**: Merchant compromised, issued refund.

In this process, I gained a certain "power"—the ability to influence the merchant's behavior. But where did this power come from?

The initial intuition is: **power comes from holding the other party's "weak points."**

But further thought reveals this explanation lacks precision. The real question is:

> **What is the essence of power? How can it be precisely expressed using a symbol system? What dynamic laws does it follow?**

---

## Part One: The Essence of Power — Asymmetry and Potential Difference

### 1.1 Weak Points Are Merely Surface

"Weak points" are just one specific manifestation of power asymmetry. The more essential expression is:

**Power = Controlling what the other party needs, while they cannot control what you need**

In the fake dog case:

- I controlled what the merchant needed to hide (evidence of illegal operations)
- I controlled what the merchant needed to avoid (being reported, license revocation)
- The merchant couldn't control what I needed to avoid (they had no leverage over me)

This formed an **asymmetric relationship**.

### 1.2 Multiple Forms of Asymmetry

Power imbalance isn't just about "weak points," but includes:

|Asymmetry Type|Case|
|---|---|
|**Information Asymmetry**|I knew they operated illegally, they didn't know I knew (initially); I knew the mayor's hotline's power, they were uncertain|
|**Resource Asymmetry**|I had time and energy for sustained pressure, merchant needed quick resolution|
|**Temporal Asymmetry**|I could wait, merchant couldn't (daily risk of being reported)|
|**Substitutability Asymmetry**|I could choose not to buy from them (substitutes available), merchant's loss of this transaction was irreplaceable|
|**Legitimacy Asymmetry**|My claim was legal, merchant's operations were not|
|**Authority Asymmetry**|I could invoke higher authority (mayor's hotline, regulators), merchant couldn't invoke higher authority to protect themselves|

**Core Insight**:

> Power isn't simply "I have your weak points," but rather **"I can influence what you care about, you cannot influence what I care about."**

### 1.3 Power as Potential Difference

Using a physics analogy, power is more like **potential difference**:

```
High Potential → Low Potential
Energy flows spontaneously

High Power → Low Power
Influence transmits spontaneously
```

In the fake dog case:

- I was at "high potential" position: holding evidence, able to invoke higher power, able to endure conflict
- Merchant was at "low potential" position: operating illegally, fearing being reported, unable to endure license revocation

This **potential difference** drove the evolution of the entire game.

## Part Two: Symbolic Expression of Power Relations

### 2.1 Two Symbol Systems

When analyzing power relations, we need to distinguish two levels:

**Level One: Legitimacy Judgment (AQT Three Dimensions)**

- Describes the other party's **performance**: Action (A), Quality (Q), aTtitude (T)
- My judgment of merchant: from (-+-) to (+++)
- This is **my subjective cognitive structure changing**

**Level Two: Power Relation Configuration**

- Describes **relative strength** of both parties
- Using simplified symbols: (+) means having power, (-) means no power
- Initial: Me(+) Merchant(-) = I can influence them, they can't influence me
- Result: Me(-) Merchant(-) = Both lost influence over each other

### 2.2 Four Basic States of Power Relations

```
++ = Both control each other's vulnerabilities (mutual checks)
+- = I can influence you, you can't influence me (I have power)
-+ = You can influence me, I can't influence you (you have power)
-- = Both parties lose influence (power dissolution)
```

**Evolution of the Fake Dog Case**:

```
T0: Me(+) × Merchant(-)
    I discovered loophole, held evidence, could report
    Merchant had no leverage over me

T1-T3: I maintained (+), merchant still (-)
    Stalemate phase, but I was accumulating tension

T4: Manager intervened, I was (+) toward manager too
    Threatened mayor's hotline, manager forced to pressure merchant

T5: Merchant compromised, issued refund
    My goal achieved, lost motivation to continue pressure
    Merchant escaped threat, I had no new leverage
    State transition: Me(-) × Merchant(-)
    **Power dissolution**
```

### 2.3 Key Insight: Merchant Chose Power Dissolution

Why did it become (--) rather than another state after merchant refunded?

```
The essence of --:
- My need satisfied (got money back) → I lost **motivation** to continue pressure
- I also had no new leverage → I lost **means** to continue pressure
- Merchant escaped threat → No longer needed defense
- Both no longer possessed influence over each other
```

This isn't "mutual checks," but **"both no longer possess influence over each other."**

## Part Three: From Mechanics to Game Theory — The Nature of Motion

### 3.1 Initial Mechanical Analogy

Using mechanical analogy for power relations seems reasonable:

|Mechanics Concept|Initial Correspondence|
|---|---|
|Force|Application of power|
|Balance|Power equality|
|Imbalance|Power difference|
|Motion|State change|

**Le Chatelier's Principle**: When external change disturbs system equilibrium, the system moves toward weakening this change.

Seems reasonable:

- I apply pressure (external change)
- Merchant compromises (system response)
- Reaches new balance (tension release)

### 3.2 Fundamental Flaw in Mechanical Analogy

But this analogy has a fatal problem: **social relations are not closed physical systems.**

**Physical System**:

- Particles have no intention
- Follow deterministic mechanical laws
- Motion is passive
- Equilibrium state usually unique

**Social Relations**:

- Agents have intentions and choices
- Don't follow deterministic laws
- Motion is active choice
- Stable states may be multiple

**Key Difference**:

> Physical System: Force → Inevitable motion → Unique equilibrium  
> Social System: Potential difference → Active choice → Multiple stable states

### 3.3 Redefinition: Force, Motion, Balance

We must reunderstand these three concepts:

**Force ≠ My application of force on you**  
**Force = Potential difference configuration of relational structure itself**

```
The state Me(+)Merchant(-) itself contains a "potential difference":
- I controlled what they needed to avoid
- They couldn't control what I needed to avoid
- This potential difference drives system evolution

But it drives not "passive motion," but "active choice"
```

**Motion ≠ Passive displacement**  
**Motion = Agent's active response choice to potential difference**

```
Merchant sees Me(+)Merchant(-) state, actively evaluates:
1. Do I want to change it?
2. Change to what?
3. From which level to change?

This is strategic choice, not passive reaction
```

**Balance ≠ Unique final state**  
**Balance = Multiple possible stable configurations**

```
Starting from Me(+)Merchant(-), possible "equilibrium" states:

1. Me(-)Merchant(--)
   Merchant compromises, power dissolves (what actually happened)

2. Me(-)Merchant(+)
   Merchant finds my weakness, power reverses

3. Me(+)Merchant(+)
   Merchant gains checks capability, power interlocks

4. Me(+)Merchant(-)
   Maintains status quo (but unstable, merchant seeks change)

Which one occurs depends on agents' strategic choices
```

## Part Four: Game Theory's Decision Space

### 4.1 Merchant's Choices

When merchant faces Me(+)Merchant(-) state, they're not passively subject to force, but **actively evaluating options**:

```
Merchant's decision space:

Option 1: Directly compromise (refund dog)
    → Becomes Me(-)Merchant(--)
    → Dissolves my power
    → Cost: One dog's money
    → Benefit: Escapes threat

Option 2: Find my leverage to counter
    → Try to become Me(-)Merchant(+) or Me(+)Merchant(+)
    → Gains checks capability
    → Cost: Time, energy, might not find any
    → Risk: Not finding but escalating conflict

Option 3: Decompress to deeper level
    → Analyze what I really care about
    → Find my weakness
    → Counter from that dimension
```

**Merchant's Assessment**:

```
Option 1 lowest cost, smallest risk
Option 2 high cost, uncertain success rate
Option 3 needs time, but threat is urgent

→ Chose Option 1: Compromise
```

### 4.2 If Merchant Were Smarter

If merchant were an old hand, they might decompress like this:

**Surface Analysis**:

```
This person holds evidence of my illegal operations
Threatens to report me
```

**Middle-Level Analysis**:

```
What do they really want?
→ Just money (refund)

What's their weakness?
→ High time cost, will give up if annoyed
→ Ordinary person, not professional whistleblower
```

**Deep-Level Analysis**:

```
Why can they threaten me?
→ Because regulatory agencies will listen to them

Where's the deep power point?
→ Regulatory agencies are the real power
→ I'm just threatening to invoke regulators, indirectly pressuring
```

**Old Hand's Strategy**:

```
Strategy A: Time-dragging tactic
Let their energy exhaust, they give up

Strategy B: Settle with regulators
Find connections, make regulators ignore them

Strategy C: Manipulate deep power point
If I can make regulators side with me,
their threat becomes ineffective
```

**Why Didn't Merchant Do This?**

```
1. Might not be smart enough, didn't think of it
2. Might assess higher cost after consideration
3. Might not have those resources (connections)
4. Might feel not worth it for one dog's money
```

### 4.3 Layered Nature of Decompression

This brings up a key concept: **decompression**.

```
Surface: A holds B's weak point
     ↓ Decompress
Middle: What does A really care about? What's B's real weakness?
     ↓ Decompress again
Deep: What larger structure is this relation embedded in?
     Are there deeper power points that can be leveraged?
```

**Three-Layer Structure of Fake Dog Case**:

```
Surface: Me(+)Merchant(-)
     I have evidence, can report

Middle: I want money vs Merchant wants not to be reported
     My weakness: Time, energy
     Merchant's weakness: Fears license revocation

Deep: Regulatory agency(+)Merchant(-)
     This is the real power relation
     I'm just threatening to invoke regulatory agency

If merchant smart enough:
Should manipulate deep power point (settle with regulators)
Not tangle at surface (refund or not)
```

**Value of Decompression**:

> See deep power points clearly, find real leverage.  
> Applying force at wrong level, half the result with double the effort.  
> Applying force at right level, achieve much with little effort.

## Part Five: Precise Correspondence with Mechanics Analogy

After reunderstanding, we can now establish precise correspondences:

|Physics Concept|Structural Concept|Explanation|
|---|---|---|
|**Potential Energy**|Degree of relational asymmetry|Me(+)Merchant(-) is a potential energy configuration|
|**Kinetic Energy**|Agent's action intention and capability|My sustained pressure's driving force|
|**Force**|Potential difference itself|Not unidirectional application, but internal tension of relation|
|**Motion**|Agent's strategic choice|Not passive displacement, but active decision|
|**Balance**|Stable state after game|May be multiple, not unique|
|**Work**|Cost of changing structural configuration|Threatening, pressuring, negotiating all "work"|

**Precise Version of Le Chatelier's Principle**:

```
Not "system automatically weakens external change"
But "agents evaluate and choose response strategy minimizing their own loss"

If compromise cost small → Compromise (becomes --)
If counter benefit large → Counter (becomes -+)
If can decompress to find deep power point → Manipulate from there

This is not automatic balance, but strategic optimization
```

## Part Six: Transient Nature of Power

### 6.1 Power Is Necessarily Transient

A profound insight:

> **Power = Asymmetry = Transient State**  
> **No power can be permanently maintained, unless continuously creating new asymmetry**

```
Me(+)Merchant(-) → Necessarily produces "motion" (merchant's strategic choice)
→ Eventually evolves to:
  Me(-)Merchant(--) or
  Me(-)Merchant(+) or
  Me(+)Merchant(+) or
  Me(+)Merchant(-) (maintains but unstable)

Power as asymmetry is essentially transient
Once reaching new stable configuration, power changes or disappears
```

### 6.2 Strategies for Maintaining Power

To maintain power, must **continuously create new asymmetry**:

**Case: Dictator**

```
Must continuously create "enemies"
→ Maintain Me(+)People(-) state
→ "If you don't follow me, enemies will harm you"
→ Asymmetry maintained
```

**Case: Company Management**

```
Must continuously paint vision
→ Maintain Boss(+)Employees(-) state
→ "Work with me, future has equity/promotion"
→ If vision realized, or employees no longer believe
→ Power dissolves or reverses
```

**Case: Intimate Relationships**

```
If one party too easily obtained, relation becomes (--)
→ Both lose influence over each other
→ "You've got me, I've got you"
→ Passion fades, may fall into lethargy

Maintaining attraction requires:
- Keep mystery (information asymmetry)
- Keep independence (substitutability asymmetry)
- Keep growth (capability asymmetry)
```

### 6.3 Inevitability of Power Dissolution

In fake dog case, why did my power dissolve?

```
T4: Me(+)Merchant(-)
    My intention: Get money back
    Merchant's situation: Being threatened

T5: Merchant compromises, refunds
    My intention: Satisfied → Lost motivation
    Merchant's situation: Escaped threat → No longer needs defense
    I also have no new leverage → Lost means

    → Transitions to: Me(-)Merchant(--)
    Power dissolution
```

**If I Wanted to Maintain Power**:

```
Plan 1: Don't take money, continue threatening
→ But my intention is to get money, this is unreasonable

Plan 2: After getting money, find new leverage
→ Create new asymmetry
→ But no motivation to do so

Conclusion: Power dissolution inevitable
Because my intention already achieved
```

## Part Seven: Dynamic Equations

### 7.1 Symbolized Dynamic Process

Can describe power relation evolution with a simplified "equation":

```
dT/dt = f(Subject action, Object action, External factors)

Where:
T = Tension (gap between intention and reality)
t = Time

When T > T_critical → Leap necessarily occurs
Leap direction = argmin(T value in new state)

I.e.: System leaps toward state minimizing tension
```

### 7.2 Dynamics of Fake Dog Case

```
T0: T = 0.9 (I want money back but can't, tension very high)

My actions:
- Discover evidence (information update)
- Find manager (introduce new agent)
- Threaten mayor's hotline (external intervention)

→ Accumulate pressure, T approaches critical point

T4: T breaks critical → Leap occurs

Possible leap directions:
  Option A: Merchant compromises → T→0.1 (I get money, tension releases) ✓
  Option B: I give up → T→0.3 (I accept reality, lower expectations)
  Option C: Both tear faces → T→0.8 (Enter legal procedure, long consumption)

Merchant chose Option A
→ Because this option also has minimum T value for them
  (Pay minimum cost, escape threat)
```

### 7.3 Core Laws of Dynamics

```
Law 1: Asymmetric state (+-) necessarily produces "motion"
    Potential difference → Agent perception → Strategy evaluation → Action choice

Law 2: System tends to leap toward low tension state
    But not automatic, through agents' game choices

Law 3: Multiple stable states coexist
    Not unique equilibrium point
    Depends on agents' preferences and constraints

Law 4: Power as asymmetry is essentially transient
    Unless continuously creating new asymmetry
```

## Part Eight: Practical Application — How to Analyze Power Relations

### 8.1 Analysis Framework

**Step 1: Identify Potential Difference**

```
Ask yourself:
- What do I control that the other party cares about?
- What does the other party control that I care about?
- Where's the asymmetry?

Label: Me(?) × Other(?)
```

**Step 2: Evaluate Current State**

```
Me(+)Other(-): I have power
Me(-)Other(+): Other has power
Me(+)Other(+): Mutual checks
Me(-)Other(--): No power relation
```

**Step 3: Predict Other Party's Choice Space**

```
Other party might:
1. Compromise (dissolve my power)
2. Counter (gain checks)
3. Decompress to find deep power points
4. Delay until I give up
5. ...

Evaluate cost/benefit of each option for them
Predict which they'll most likely choose
```

**Step 4: Decompress to Find Deep Power Points**

```
Surface: Direct conflict point
Middle: Both parties' real needs and weaknesses
Deep: Larger structure and real power location

Ask:
- Where's the real leverage?
- Is there higher-dimensional solution?
- Can structure be changed from deep level?
```

**Step 5: Design Strategy**

```
If I want to maintain power:
→ Continuously create new asymmetry
→ Don't let other party find counter means

If I want power transformation:
→ Find other party's deep needs
→ Create win-win solution
→ Transition to new stable state

If I want to dissolve power:
→ Satisfy needs
→ Release tension
→ Transition to (--)
```

### 8.2 Case: Workplace Salary Negotiation

**Initial Analysis**:

```
My need: Raise
Boss's need: Control cost, retain me

Surface power:
- If Me(+)Boss(-): I have other offer, boss needs me
- If Me(-)Boss(+): I have no alternatives, boss doesn't care if I leave

Current: Me(?)Boss(?)
→ Need to create or discover asymmetry
```

**Decompress to Deep Level**:

```
Surface: Should they give me raise?

Middle:
- What I really care about: Income increase? Recognition? Growth?
- What boss really cares about: My value? Replacement cost? Team stability?

Deep:
- Company's compensation system
- Industry compensation level
- My market value

Deep power point:
- Not "whether boss agrees"
- But "how market evaluates my value"
```

**Strategy Design**:

```
Strategy A: Create substitutability asymmetry
→ Get external offer
→ Prove market value
→ Becomes Me(+)Boss(-)
→ Boss forced to evaluate: Raise vs lose me

Strategy B: Decompress to find deep needs
→ What does boss really care about? (not just cost)
→ Might be: Performance, project progress, team stability
→ What can I provide? (not just current work)
→ Create larger value exchange

Strategy C: Change framework
→ Not just talk "raise" (divide pie)
→ Talk "how to make pie bigger" (create value)
→ From confrontation to cooperation
→ From Me(+)Boss(-) to Me(+)Boss(+)
```

### 8.3 Case: Resolving Conflict

**Scenario**: Two teams conflict over resource allocation

**Surface Analysis**:

```
Team A: Needs more resources
Team B: Doesn't want to give up resources

Surface: Me(+)You(-) zero-sum game
```

**Decompress to Deep Level**:

```
Middle:
- What does Team A really need? (Resources? Or solving problem?)
- What does Team B really worry about? (Resources? Or influence?)

Deep:
- Why are resources limited? (Company strategy? External environment?)
- Is there possibility of creating new resources?
- Where's root cause of conflict? (System? Incentives? Culture?)

Deep power point:
- Not "who grabs resources"
- But "how to redesign resource allocation mechanism"
```

**Strategy**:

```
Plan 1: Decompress upward
→ Find higher-level decision maker
→ Redefine problem
→ From "A vs B" to "We vs external competition"

Plan 2: Decompress downward
→ Find both parties' common needs
→ Redefine resources
→ From "competing for limited resources" to "cooperating to create new resources"

Plan 3: Framework reconstruction
→ Change game rules
→ From "zero-sum" to "positive-sum"
→ From confrontation to cooperation
```

## Part Nine: Core Insights and Limitations

### 9.1 Ten Core Insights

1. **Power comes from asymmetry, not just "weak points"**
    
    - Information, resources, time, legitimacy, substitutability...
2. **Power is potential difference, embedded in relational structure**
    
    - Not "my application of force on you"
    - But "internal imbalance of relational configuration"
3. **Motion is active choice, not passive reaction**
    
    - Agents evaluate, decide, choose
    - Not mechanical inevitable motion
4. **Balance not unique, depends on game result**
    
    - Multiple stable states coexist
    - Transition through strategic choice
5. **Decompression reveals deep power points**
    
    - Surface conflict point isn't real leverage
    - Deep structure is key
6. **Power essentially transient**
    
    - Asymmetry necessarily drives evolution
    - Maintaining power requires continuously creating asymmetry
7. **Symbol system precisely expresses dynamics**
    
    - Me(+)×You(-): Power asymmetry
    - Me(-)×You(--): Power dissolution
    - Me(+)×You(+): Mutual checks
8. **Tension drives leaps**
    
    - Gap between intention and reality
    - Accumulates to critical point necessarily leaps
9. **Leap direction predictable but not deterministic**
    
    - System tends toward low tension state
    - But agents' choices have randomness
10. **Highest strategy is creating positive-sum**
    
    - Not zero-sum game
    - But redefining game

### 9.2 Theory's Applicable Boundaries

This theory **is not omnipotent**, has clear limitations:

**Limitation 1: Only applies to agents with intention**

```
Not applicable to: Natural phenomena, unconscious processes
Applicable to: Interpersonal relations, organizational games, social interactions
```

**Limitation 2: Requires certain rationality assumptions**

```
If agent completely irrational, strategy prediction fails
If agent emotionally out of control, leap unpredictable
```

**Limitation 3: External randomness cannot be eliminated**

```
Chance events may change entire structure
Cannot precisely predict leap moment
Can only identify trends and possibilities
```

**Limitation 4: Long-term dynamics complex**

```
Short-term (single game) relatively predictable
Long-term (repeated game) too many variables
Needs continuous observation and adjustment
```

**Limitation 5: Ethical constraints priority**

```
Can do doesn't mean should do
Power analysis shouldn't be used for manipulation and harm
Legitimacy, morality are hard boundaries
```

### 9.3 User's Responsibility

**Three Principles**:

1. **Honest Attitude**
    
    - Admit this isn't truth, just tool
    - Clarify applicable domain and limitations
    - Take responsibility for usage consequences
2. **Constructive Application**
    
    - Use to understand and solve problems
    - Not for manipulation and harm
    - Create positive-sum, not zero-sum
3. **Continuous Reflection**
    
    - Theory might be wrong
    - Analysis might be biased
    - Keep open and corrective

## Conclusion: Wisdom of Power

Power is not a tool for "smart people to suppress dumb people."

True power wisdom is:

> **See structure clearly** → Not deceived by surface  
> **Decompress to deep level** → Find real leverage  
> **Create positive-sum** → Rather than zero-sum confrontation  
> **Use responsibly** → Rather than manipulate and harm

In fake dog return case, I gained power, also dissolved power.

The inspiration from this process isn't "how to suppress others," but:

1. **Asymmetry can be discovered** (License loophole, manager's slip)
2. **Deep power points can be leveraged** (Regulatory agency is key)
3. **Legitimacy is fundamental boundary** (Legal claims have moral high ground)
4. **Power naturally dissolves after goal achieved** (Got money back, no need to continue)

**Ultimate Wisdom**:

```
Not "how to gain power"
But "how to use power responsibly"

Not "how to defeat others"
But "how to create win-win"

Not "how to manipulate"
But "how to cooperate"

The highest realm of power is not needing power.
```
