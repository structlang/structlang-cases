## Introduction: A Seemingly Ordinary Conversation

In Chapter 30, we systematically analyzed the dynamics of power—how power emerges from relational structures themselves, how it generates influence through asymmetry and potential difference. That was understanding power structure from a macro level.

This chapter uses an everyday case to demonstrate the theory's analytical power: the structural anatomy of showing off wealth.

"How do you like this bag? It costs 100,000 yuan. Not something ordinary people can afford. Someone earning 5,000 yuan per month would need 20 months without eating or drinking to buy it. People earning 5,000 per month are not from the same world as me."

If we examine each sentence separately:

- "Bag costs 100,000" - Stating price
- "Ordinary people can't afford" - Stating fact
- "5,000 monthly salary needs 20 months" - Mathematical calculation
- "Not from same world" - Expressing difference

Each sentence can be called an "objective statement." But the listener clearly feels provocation and contempt.

Why?

## The Chaining of Judgments Reveals Intention

The answer lies in: **individual judgments have no direction, but chained judgments have direction.**

The speaker could discuss this bag from countless angles:

- Design aesthetics: "This bag's line design is classic"
- Brand history: "This brand has 100 years of history"
- Usage experience: "This bag is very durable"
- Purchase occasion: "I bought this while traveling in Paris"

But what did they choose?

**They chose "economic capability" as the sole organizing origin.**

More critically, they didn't just say "100,000," but carefully constructed a judgment chain:

1. "100,000" - Establish number
2. "Ordinary people can't afford" - Divide class
3. "5,000 salary needs 20 months" - Quantify gap
4. "Not from same world" - Clearly separate

**Each judgment advances along the same axis, with the final sentence being the inevitable conclusion of all previous judgments.**

This isn't stating facts, this is constructing a power structure of "I'm high, you're low."

## Framework Compression Mechanism: Why It Only Works on Certain People

Interestingly, if these words were said to elementary students or Elon Musk, they'd produce no expected effect.

### Fails on Elementary Students

**Lacking common manifestation elements:**

- Elementary students have no compressed experience of "monthly salary"
- No survival experience of "20 months without eating or drinking"
- 100,000 and 1 million are both just "a lot of money," no sense of scale

**No common causal impression:**

- They haven't established the "economic capability → social status" causal chain
- Might think "having a Transformer" is more worthy of showing off

### Fails on Elon Musk

**Scale fails:**

- 100,000 produces no differentiation on Musk's economic scale
- Like showing off "I have 100 dollars" to him

**Origin not in this dimension:**

- Musk's value origin might be "capability to change the world"
- "Being able to afford 100,000 bag" isn't even his judgment dimension

### Necessary Conditions for Consensus Compression

For showing off to work, both parties need **implicit consensus** on specific elements:

1. **Common value dimension**: "Economic capability is important judgment dimension"
2. **Common scale standard**: "100,000 is a number with differentiation"
3. **Common causal impression**: "Can afford → Capable → High status"
4. **Comparable structural space**: "We're competing for same origin"

**Only when both parties are within this consensus intersection can framework compression take effect.**

A person earning 5,000 monthly is offended because they happen to be within the intersection of all consensus elements. Through carefully chosen judgment chaining, the speaker successfully manipulated their surface structure, positioning them at the "low" end.

## From StructLang Perspective: Complete Anatomy of the Attack

Analyzing with StructLang framework, this conversation involves the following structural mechanisms:

### 1. Struggle for Origin Definition Rights

Speaker tries to establish "economic capability" as the origin for judging "human value." Once this origin is accepted, all subsequent judgments unfold in this coordinate system.

### 2. Scale Establishment

Through "5,000 monthly salary needs 20 months," establishes a scale in listener's cognition: "100,000 is a big number." This scale makes "can afford" and "cannot afford" produce significant differentiation.

### 3. Judgment Chaining

Each judgment isn't isolated, but carefully organized chain. From number → class → gap → separation, advancing step by step to the conclusion "we're not from same world."

### 4. Forced Surface Structure Construction

Speaker tries to construct a two-dimensional surface structure:

- Dimension 1: Economic capability (they have, you don't)
- Dimension 2: Social class (they upper, you lower)

Through this surface structure, completes "I'm high, you're low" positioning.

### 5. Consensus Elements Delineation

The entire attack's foundation assumes the other party accepts:

- "Economic capability" is important dimension
- "100,000" is meaningful scale
- "Can afford → Capable" causal chain

Without these consensuses, attack cannot take effect.

## Defense and Counterattack: Systematized Structural Operations

Having understood the attack's structural mechanism, we can derive corresponding defense and counterattack strategies from each structural link.

### Strategy Type 1: Origin Operations

**Core property of origin:** Origin determines judgment coordinate system. Control origin, control entire judgment space.

**Strategy 1-1: Origin Preemption**

```
Other: "This bag costs 100,000"
You: "100,000? Are you in investment?"
```

Before other party establishes "economic capability" origin, force switch to "professional identity" origin through questioning. Once origin switches, 100,000 loses its showing off power.

**Strategy 1-2: Origin Voidization**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "What is 'world'?"
```

Force other party to define "world." Any definition exposes origin as arbitrary choice, thus deconstructing its authority. This is meta-level attack: your entire judgment system is subjectively constructed.

**Strategy 1-3: Origin Refusal to Acknowledge**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "Wait, your statement has a premise: we should judge human hierarchy by income. But I don't accept this premise"
```

Make implicit origin explicit, then clearly refuse. Don't argue within their coordinate system, refuse the coordinate system itself.

### Strategy Type 2: Scale Operations

**Core property of scale:** Scale determines numerical differentiation. Change scale, numerical meaning changes.

**Strategy 2-1: Scale Crushing**

```
Other: "This bag costs 100,000"
You: "Hmm, entry level. But enough for daily use"
```

Redefine scale: 100,000 = entry level. Implies your scale is higher, their showing off produces no expected effect on your scale.

**Strategy 2-2: Scale Non-linearization**

```
You: "Below 10,000 is daily necessities, 10,000-500,000 is consumer goods, above 500,000 is collectibles. Yours should be consumer goods, right?"
```

Establish new scale system, making 100,000 categorized as "mid-range." Scale redivision changes numerical meaning.

**Strategy 2-3: Scale Conversion**

```
Other: "5,000 monthly salary needs 20 months"
You: "20 months... I saw data once, average human lifespan is 29,000 days, 20 months is 600 days, or 2% of life. Thinking this way, anything costing 2% of life to exchange deserves careful consideration"
```

Switch from "money scale" to "life scale." Their showing off becomes life waste.

### Strategy Type 3: Dimension Operations

**Core property of dimension:** Objects have different judgments on different dimensions. Change dimension, change judgment result.

**Strategy 3-1: Dimension Proliferation**

```
Other: "This bag costs 100,000"
You: "Expensive is one aspect. But I'm more concerned: Does this brand's supply chain meet environmental standards? Are conflict minerals used? How are workers treated?"
```

Introduce new dimensions other party hasn't prepared for: environment, ethics. On new dimensions, they might be negative. From "I can afford expensive goods" to "I might support unethical products."

**Strategy 3-2: Dimension Degradation**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "Is income dimension interesting? I know someone who inherited tens of millions, never works. By your logic, are they in same world as Jack Ma?"
```

Question validity of dimension itself. Income ≠ Capability ≠ Value. Make "income" dimension collapse.

### Strategy Type 4: Surface Structure Operations

**Core property of surface structure:** Surface structure is combination of two dimensions. Change either dimension, entire structure reconstructs.

**Strategy 4-1: Surface Structure Reconstruction**

```
Other tries to establish: [Rich (they+ me-) × Same class (they+ me-)]

Your counterattack: "Indeed, you have purchasing power, but I observe that truly wealthy people rarely deliberately mention their consumption. This behavior makes me think of a dimension: security"
```

Accept their first dimension (rich), but introduce new second dimension: security. New surface structure: [Rich(+) × Insecure(-)]. They change from (++) to (+-).

**Strategy 4-2: Surface Structure Inversion**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000, people earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "Hmm, you're right. You have purchasing power, I have upward potential. Indeed quite different"
```

Accept surface structure but change coordinate axis. Not [rich vs poor], but [peaked vs ascending]. Static vs dynamic comparison. Implies: you are present tense, I am future tense.

### Strategy Type 5: Time Window Operations

**Core property of time window:** Structure needs time window to stabilize. Control time, control structure.

**Strategy 5-1: Time Window Premature Termination**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000, people earning 5,000..."
You: "Hmm, got it" (immediately turn and leave)
```

Physically terminate conversation's time window. Their structure construction is interrupted before completion. Simplest defense: don't give time to complete structure construction.

**Strategy 5-2: Time Window Reverse Projection**

```
You: "Hmm, quite nice. But I'm thinking, ten years from now when we meet again, will you remember this bag? Will I remember today's conversation? Probably not"
```

Introduce future time window. From ten-year perspective, this bag becomes meaningless. Deconstruct present importance from time dimension.

### Strategy Type 6: Manifestation Operations

**Core property of manifestation:** Only objects entering manifestation can be judged. Control manifestation, control judgment.

**Strategy 6-1: Manifestation Blocking**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000, people earning 5,000..."
You: (Suddenly check phone) "Sorry, I need to check a message"
```

Physically interrupt, force switch intention. Their words cannot enter your manifestation.

**Strategy 6-2: Manifestation Flooding**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000"
You: "By the way, how was that restaurant you mentioned last time? I've been wanting to try it. And have you seen that movie?..."
```

Fill manifestation space with large amounts of irrelevant information. "Bag" as object is diluted among other objects, loses weight.

### Strategy Type 7: Compression and Decompression Operations

**Core property of compression:** Compression is simplification, power comes from vagueness. Decompression exposes vagueness.

**Strategy 7-1: Decompression Attack**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "What does 'one world' mean? Physical space? Cognitive space? Values? Or purchasing power? If purchasing power, what's the threshold? Are 5,000 and 5,001 different worlds?"
```

Force decompression of compressed concept. After decompression, find concept vague, arbitrary, cannot be precisely defined.

**Strategy 7-2: Reverse Compression**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000, people earning 5,000 need 20 months, not from same world"
You: "Nouveau riche"
```

They use long judgment chain to construct "I'm higher than you" compression, you use one word to construct "you're nouveau riche" reverse compression. More concise compression = stronger memory fixation.

### Strategy Type 8: Consensus Operations

**Core property of consensus:** Consensus is foundation for structure to take effect. Destroy consensus, structure fails.

**Strategy 8-1: Explicit Consensus Refusal**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 monthly not from same world"
You: "Your statement's premise is: we should judge human hierarchy by income. I don't accept this premise"
```

Make implicit consensus explicit, then clearly refuse. Consensus foundation destroyed = entire structure fails.

**Strategy 8-2: Consensus Conditionalization**

```
You: "100,000 in which city? In Beijing might be mid-range, in small city might be high consumption. In 2025 is one number, in 1990 is another. So this 100,000 you mention, specifically in what context?"
```

Reveal consensus's conditional dependence. Consensus only holds under specific conditions, universality is deconstructed.

### Strategy Type 9: Chaining Operations

**Core property of chaining:** Judgment chain has directionality. Interrupt or reverse, change structure construction.

**Strategy 9-1: Chain Hijacking**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000, ordinary people can't afford, people earning 5,000..."
You: "Right, so I've always felt rational consumption is important. These years I invested in my own capabilities, looking back now, if I'd spent money on these things back then I'd have lost out"
```

Accept all facts, but reverse chain: expensive → irrational → I wouldn't do this → I'm more rational.

**Strategy 9-2: Chain Exposure**

```
Other: "People earning 5,000 need 20 months, not from same world"
You: "Haha, seems like you really need to draw clear boundaries with people earning 5,000 monthly"
```

Expose deep needs: you need to confirm yourself by belittling others. Transform "I'm high, you're low" to "I'm insecure."

### Strategy Type 10: Leap Operations

**Core property of leap:** Structure suddenly leaps under specific conditions. Rehearse leap, shake current structure.

**Strategy 10-1: Leap Rehearsal**

```
Other: "This bag 100,000"
You: "Suppose one day you need to sell this bag for money, how much could you get? Probably not even 50,000. How would you view today's decision to spend 100,000 buying it then?"
```

Imagining leap scenario. From "possessing" leap to "losing," makes them realize current superiority is fragile.

## General Principles of Defense and Counterattack

From StructLang perspective, all defense and counterattack follow one core principle:

**Other party tries to force you into their structure, your defense is "I'm not in your structure," your counterattack is "I use my structure to redefine situation."**

Specifically:

1. **Don't acknowledge their origin** - They cannot define your value coordinate system
2. **Don't enter their scale** - Their numbers produce no expected effect on you
3. **Don't activate dimensions they specify** - You can choose to focus on other dimensions
4. **Don't let judgment chaining take effect** - Interrupt or reverse at any link
5. **Don't participate in surface structure construction** - Refuse to be positioned at "low" end
6. **Construct new structure** - Use your origin, scale, dimension to redefine situation

This is why Musk and elementary students won't be offended—they're not in this structural space at all. While person earning 5,000 monthly is offended because they implicitly acknowledged other party's structural setup.

**Once you understand structure, you gain choice: enter or not enter, accept or reconstruct.**
