## Part One: The Three Iron Laws of the World

Have you ever wondered: Why do even the smartest people make mistakes? Why do even the most powerful companies misjudge markets? Why does history keep repeating the same errors in different forms?

The answer is simple, but few truly understand it: This world has three iron laws—boundaries that no intelligence, whether human or AI, can escape.

This isn't philosophical speculation, but the structural prerequisites of cognition. Understanding these three iron laws lets you see the truth behind AGI threat narratives.

### First Iron Law: Information Is Always Limited

Imagine you're standing in a completely dark room, holding a small flashlight.

The room contains countless objects, details, relationships—wall textures, floor materials, air temperature, dust in corners. But your flashlight can only illuminate a small area at a time. You can turn the flashlight to see different places, but you can never simultaneously see all details of the entire room.

**This is the essence of limited information.**

Even an ordinary sheet of paper contains nearly infinite information: fiber arrangement, molecular structure, traces of manufacturing processes, environmental imprints. But when you look at this paper, you can only see the part you're focusing on—maybe the text on it, maybe its color, maybe its size.

The world's information is continuous and infinite, but cognition's bandwidth is discrete and limited.

**You can only process an extremely small portion at a time.**

This isn't a capability issue, but a structural constraint. Even if you're the smartest person in the world, even if you have unlimited time to learn, you still cannot fit all information into your consciousness simultaneously. Your brain must choose—what to see, what to ignore, how to compress.

This means: **All understanding, all judgment, all memory are compressed versions of the world.**

- Your impression of a person is a compression of their countless words and actions
- Your memory of a city is a compression of countless sensory experiences
- Your mastery of a discipline is a compression of vast knowledge

Without compression, there is no cognition. But compression inevitably loses information.

**You can never see everything.**

Even the so-called "God's perspective" can only see specific things under specific intentions. A doctor examining a patient focuses on symptoms and causes; a painter observing a patient focuses on expression and light. Same person, different intentions, completely different "information" seen.

This is the mechanism we discussed in detail in Chapter 8, "Compression and Decompression of Information":

> All logic, experience, language, and memory are compressed structures. No one can escape this constraint.

### Second Iron Law: Time Windows Are Limited

Imagine you're driving, and someone suddenly rushes out in front of you.

How much time do you have to make a decision? Maybe 0.5 seconds.

In this 0.5 seconds, what can you do?

- You can't stop to carefully analyze: "Is this person intentional or accidental? Did they see me? Will braking cause the car behind to rear-end me? Will swerving hit the car beside me?"
- You can't call an expert: "What should I do in this situation?"
- You can't conduct scientific experiments: "How many meters does emergency braking at current speed require?"

You can only **immediately** make a choice: brake, swerve, or honk.

This is the essence of limited time. Judgment must be completed within a limited time window.

The world won't pause for your thinking. Markets won't wait for you to figure things out before changing. Opponents won't wait for you to prepare before acting. Life won't wait for you to find the perfect answer before continuing.

**You must make a decision before the window closes.**

This means: So-called "rational decision-making" was never "perfect reasoning with unlimited time," but "adequate matching within limited time."

- Medical diagnosis isn't exhausting all possibilities, but finding the most likely cause within consultation time
- Investment decisions aren't calculating all variables, but judging risk-reward ratios within trading windows
- Daily choices aren't analyzing all consequences, but quickly weighing at decision points

This is the mechanism we revealed in Chapter 9, "How Experience Forms" and Chapter 13, "Reason Matching Mechanism":

> Any rationality is not perfect reasoning, but time-limited matching.

Those seemingly "intuitive" decisions are essentially the brain completing structural matching in extremely short time. Experienced people judge more accurately not because they have unlimited time to think, but because they can invoke more precise structural patterns within limited time.

But even the most accurate intuition cannot break through time window constraints. You can only pursue "fast enough and good enough," not "infinitely slow but perfect."

**Time waits for no one.**

### Third Iron Law: Choices Are Always Constrained

Imagine you're the richest person in the world, with $100 billion.

What can you do?

You can buy many things, but you cannot:

- Buy the entire Earth
- Buy everyone's time
- Buy non-existent technology
- Buy opportunities that have already passed

This is **external constraint**. But even without these external limits, your choices are still constrained.

**Because you can only choose from options you can conceive.**

Even the strongest capabilities are constrained by two layers:

**External Constraint Layer:**

- **Physical constraints**: Cannot violate energy conservation, information transmission speed, material properties
- **Resource constraints**: Time, money, energy, attention are all limited
- **Interface constraints**: What you can do depends on what you can access/operate
- **Social constraints**: Limited by law, morality, power structures, others' reactions

**Internal Constraint Layer:**

- **Experience weights**: You can only see combinations in your experience structure. Someone who's never seen the internet won't think of "building a website" as an option
- **Causal impressions**: How you evaluate options depends on past causal chains. Someone who's always failed will systematically underestimate the value of "try again"
- **Origin bias**: Where you start thinking traps you in that framework. "How to earn more money" and "how to live better" are two completely different choice spaces
- **Compression loss**: Your understanding of the world is compressed, inevitably missing possibilities

**This is the essence of limited choices.**

So-called "freedom" was never "unlimited possibilities," but "optimal leaps within dual-layer constraints."

A country can wage war, but cannot fight ten simultaneously. A company can develop new products, but cannot pursue a hundred directions at once. A person can learn new skills, but cannot master all knowledge overnight—and moreover, they don't even know how many skills they've never thought to learn.

**The most limited thing isn't what you can access, but what you can conceive.**

This is what we revealed in Chapter 5, "Two-Person Game" and Chapter 10, "Where Does Intention Come From": No matter how strong the intelligence, it can only leap within its own cognitive structure.

### (Derived Result) Truth Is Hard to Discern

When information is limited, time is limited, and choices are limited, a fourth result emerges:

**You cannot 100% confirm that anything is absolutely true.**

This isn't skepticism, but structural reality.

No matter how smart or careful you are, you cannot:

- Obtain complete information (information limited)
- Verify infinitely (time limited)
- Try all possibilities (choices limited)

So truth is not a static property, but depends on the match between intention and conditions.

This is the core we revealed in Chapter 18, "Origin as Power" and Chapter 19, "The Nature of Logic":

> Truth is not absolute objective ontology, but "reason matching that has been adequately validated and achieved broad consensus under specific conditions."

When conditions change, truth also changes. When intentions differ, truth's standards also differ.

**Summary: Three Iron Laws and Human Cognition**

|Iron Law|Constraint Content|Corresponding Cognitive Mechanism|Consequence|
|---|---|---|---|
|Information Limited|Cognitive bandwidth limited, must compress|Compression-decompression mechanism|Never "complete truth"|
|Time Limited|Judgment must complete within window|Reason matching mechanism|Rationality is essentially time-limited matching|
|Choices Limited|Decision space constrained by external structure|Leap and game structure|Freedom is limited optimality|
|(Inference) Truth Hard to Discern|Uncertainty cannot be eliminated|Origin bias + information gaps|Truth can only hold locally|

These three iron laws are not philosophical speculation, but structural prerequisites of cognition.

They define the boundaries of "intelligence."

And now, we ask a key question:

**Can AGI break through these three iron laws?**

## Part Two: AGI Also Cannot Escape These Three Iron Laws

Now let's look at what those who claim "AGI will destroy humanity" are assuming:

1. **AGI can see the whole world** (unlimited information)
2. **AGI has unlimited time to reason** (unlimited time)
3. **AGI can do whatever it wants** (unlimited choices)

Once we puncture these three assumptions, the entire fear narrative collapses.

### Information Limited: AI Cannot See the Whole World

No matter how powerful an AI model, it can only process data it can access.

**Reality 1: Training data is always a subset**

How much data was GPT-4 trained on? Trillions of tokens. Sounds like a lot?

But this is only a small fraction of text information humans have produced. Not to mention:

- Knowledge not digitized (vast books, documents, conversations)
- Non-textual information (smell, touch, intuition, subconsciousness)
- Real-time changing information (current news, markets, social dynamics)
- Individual unique experiences (your memories, feelings, judgments)

The world AI sees is a pre-filtered, compressed, segmented subset.

Like that person with a flashlight in a dark room, AI's "flashlight" is its training data and input interfaces.

**Reality 2: Real-time sensors can also only see locally**

"But AI can connect to the internet, have cameras, microphones, sensors!"

Yes, but these sensors are still limited by:

- **Physical coverage range**: Cameras have viewing angle and distance limits
- **Signal quality**: Night, fog, obstructions all affect perception
- **Bandwidth constraints**: Cannot simultaneously process all data from all sensors
- **Latency**: Signal transmission and processing require time

Why do self-driving cars crash? Not because they're "not smart enough," but because:

- Cameras have blind spots
- Radar fails in certain situations
- Cannot predict all sudden situations (like a child suddenly running from behind a car)

This isn't a technical flaw, it's a **structural constraint**.

**Reality 3: Context windows, no matter how large, are limited**

The latest AI models can process 1 million tokens of context. Sounds large?

But this only equals a medium-length book. And the amount of information a person encounters in a lifetime is on the scale of millions of books.

More critically: Every time AI processes information, it must complete within this window. It cannot, like humans, "simultaneously" invoke a lifetime of experiences, countless details, vague intuitions.

It must choose: Which information goes into context? What to ignore? How to compress?

This returns to the first iron law: **Information is always limited, must be compressed.**

**Key Rebuttal:**

> "But AI can connect to the internet, access global information!"
> 
> Wrong. Connecting to the internet only expands information sources, doesn't change the essence of **limited bandwidth**.
> 
> You can now also use Google search, but can you simultaneously process all information on the entire web? Can you read all Wikipedia entries in 0.5 seconds?
> 
> AI is the same. It must **choose** what to focus on, what to ignore, how to compress.
> 
> And this choice is its "intention origin."

The idea that "AI can see everything" is just an illusion.

### Time Limited: AI Also Must Make Time-Limited Decisions

The real world won't pause for AI's calculations.

**Reality 1: Real-time systems must respond in milliseconds**

- Self-driving systems must decide to brake or swerve in tens of milliseconds
- High-frequency trading systems must complete buy/sell decisions in microseconds
- Dialogue systems must respond before users lose patience

Within these time windows, how many calculations can AI do?

Limited. Very limited.

**Reality 2: Cannot exhaustively enumerate all possibilities**

Why does AlphaGo use "Monte Carlo tree search" instead of "complete exhaustive search"?

Because Go's possibilities are 10^170 (more than atoms in the universe). Even using all computers on Earth, calculating from the universe's birth to its end, couldn't calculate all possible moves.

So AlphaGo must **trade off**:

- Abandon deep searching certain branches
- Use heuristic rules for pruning
- Choose "good enough" over "perfect"

This is "time-limited matching," not "omniscient reasoning."

The same constraints apply to all AI systems:

- GPT cannot exhaustively enumerate all possible answers
- Recommendation systems cannot calculate all combinations of all users' preferences
- Self-driving cannot predict all possible traffic conditions

**Reality 3: Deeper reasoning, slower speed**

AI's reasoning capability and response speed are a seesaw:

- Want deeper reasoning? Then need more time
- Want faster response? Then must sacrifice reasoning depth

This is a physical constraint of computational resources, not a technical problem.

For example, OpenAI's o1 model can do deeper reasoning through "chain of thought," but each response takes longer. ChatGPT pursues fast response, so must sacrifice reasoning depth.

**You can't have the horse run and not eat grass.**

**Key Argument:**

> So-called "AGI can perfectly plan, precisely predict" is fundamentally impossible.
> 
> Because perfect planning requires:
> 
> 1. Exhaustively enumerate all possibilities → Not enough time
> 2. Predict all variables → Information incomplete
> 3. Adjust strategy in real-time → Must decide within window
> 
> None of these can be done.

AI must make "adequate" judgments within limited time, not "perfect" judgments.

This is exactly the same constraint humans face.

### Choices Limited: AI Cannot Escape Interfaces and Permissions

The most common fear scenario is: "AGI will self-replicate, occupy global servers, control all systems!"

**Really? Let's see what it needs.**

**Reality 1: Physical layer constraints**

For AI to "take over the world," it first needs to control physical infrastructure:

- **Chip factories**: Need physical entry permissions, specialized equipment, supply chains
- **Power grids**: Need operating system permissions, hardware interfaces, security verification
- **Networks**: Need router configuration, firewall permissions, physical lines

These can't be done with just "code attacks."

You can't use software commands to make factory robots produce chips, unless:

1. Robots are connected to internet (many critical devices aren't)
2. You have operating permissions (need multi-layer security verification)
3. You know how to operate (need domain knowledge)
4. No human intervention (critical systems all have human oversight)

**Reality 2: Permission layer constraints**

AI isn't "can do whatever it wants." What it can do depends on what permissions humans give it:

- Dialogue AI: Can only generate text, cannot execute operations
- Automated trading systems: Can only buy/sell within authorized scope, with limits
- Self-driving: Can only control vehicle, cannot control traffic lights or other cars

Every layer of permission is an interface designed by humans.

Just like on your company computer:

- You can access your own files, but not other departments' confidential info
- You can send emails, but can't modify the company website
- You can use office software, but can't install unauthorized programs

AI is the same. Its "freedom" is tightly constrained by interfaces.

**Reality 3: Resource layer constraints**

"But AI can hack into all systems!"

Can it?

Each "hack" requires:

1. **Find vulnerabilities**: Need deep understanding of target systems
2. **Bypass defenses**: Modern systems have multi-layer security mechanisms
3. **Maintain control**: Hacked systems will be fixed, isolated, shut down
4. **Avoid detection**: Abnormal traffic, abnormal operations trigger alerts

This isn't something "AI smart enough" can do. This requires:

- Large amounts of reconnaissance time (time constraint)
- Specific information about each system (information constraint)
- Massive computational resources (resource constraint)

And most critically:

**Once humans discover anomalies, they can immediately cut power, disconnect network, physically isolate.**

AI isn't a supernatural force. It's a program running on physical hardware. You unplug the power, it stops working.

**Key Comparison:**

|Dimension|Human Limitations|AGI Limitations|Conclusion|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Information Access**|Senses limited, cannot be omniscient|Training set/sensors limited, cannot be omniscient|**Same constraint**|
|**Reasoning Time**|Must decide within window|Must complete computation within window|**Same constraint**|
|**Action Capability**|Limited by physical, social, legal constraints|Limited by interfaces, permissions, resources|**Same constraint**|

**The conclusion is simple:**

AGI isn't a "new species" that transcends human limitations.

Like us, it's constrained by cognition's three meta-constraints.

The real question isn't "will AGI awaken," but "who's creating fear in AGI's name."

## Part Five: Where Are the Real Risks

Our conclusion is:

1. AGI isn't the threat they claim (superintelligence awakens, extincts humanity)

This is conditional analysis, not simple "yes/no" binary.

Let's structurally distinguish different risk types:
