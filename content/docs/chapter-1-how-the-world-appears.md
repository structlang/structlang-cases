It's 1 PM. You walk out of the office building.

You're hungry, wanting lunch. Looking around, the street has over a dozen shops—convenience stores, cafés, noodle shops, fast food joints, Western restaurants.

Your gaze sweeps across these storefronts, and certain shop names "pop" into your field of view. Maybe it's "Spicy Hot Pot," maybe "Braised Chicken Rice." These names suddenly become clear, while other shops seem to blur, receding into the background.

**What just happened in this moment?**

The shops on the street haven't changed. They've always been there, their signs always there. What changed is: you're hungry.

"I'm hungry, I need lunch"—the moment this thought emerges, the world reorganizes itself. Things that can solve "hunger" manifest, while things that can't solve "hunger" recede into the background.

This isn't you actively searching—it happens automatically. The spicy hot pot sign "pops" out on its own, while the bookstore sign next to it, though bigger and brighter, doesn't enter your attention.

**This is the first characteristic of manifestation: Intention determines what manifests.**

---

## Intention Changes, Manifestation Changes

Now switch scenarios.

Same street, same you, but this time you're not hungry—you're looking for a quiet place to discuss business.

Looking around again—the café sign "pops" out. The spicy hot pot sign, though so clear just now, now recedes into the background, almost invisible.

**The shops on the street are still the same shops. What changed is only your intention.**

- Intention is "eat lunch" → Spicy hot pot manifests
- Intention is "find a place to talk" → Café manifests

Same world, different manifestations.

This isn't "you saw different things"—the shops were always there. Rather, **different things manifested to you**. Manifestation isn't determined by the shops, but by your intention.

Try switching intentions again.

This time you want to find a bathroom—the convenience store sign "pops" out. Both the café and spicy hot pot recede into the background.

**Three intentions, three manifestations, three different worlds.**

---

## Do Unmanifested Things Exist?

You might say: But those unmanifested shops are still there, I know they exist.

Really?

Try to recall: When you were hungry looking for food, what color was that bookstore's sign? What's the name of the bank next door? Is the pharmacy next to it having a sale?

You don't know.

Not that you didn't see them—your eyes swept across the entire street. But this information didn't enter your cognition. For the "hungry wanting food" you, they didn't manifest, therefore under that intention, they "don't exist."

Physically, of course they're there. But in your cognition, they weren't activated, weren't processed, didn't become part of your decision-making.

**This is the second characteristic of manifestation: What hasn't manifested doesn't exist in cognition.**

Someone might ask: But if I go back and look now, won't I know?

Yes. But that's a new intention ("confirm that bookstore's sign"), which will produce a new manifestation. What you're recalling now isn't the original experience, but a new experience.

## Point Structure: The Basic Unit of Cognition

Now let's give this phenomenon a name: **Point Structure**.

When an intention activates, an object manifests from the background—this forms a point structure.

Using a simple symbol to represent it:

**Intention** → **Object** = **Point**

- Intention: "I'm hungry"
- Object: "Spicy hot pot"
- Point: `Spicy hot pot*` (asterisk indicates this object is activated, focused)

Point structure is the minimal unit of cognition. You can't get simpler than this—as long as you're thinking, there must be an intention, and there must be an object manifesting.

**Several key characteristics:**

**1. Points are activated by intention** Without intention, there are no points. The object is always there, but it only becomes a point when your intention directs toward it.

**2. Points are the result of compression** The point "spicy hot pot" compresses massive information: shop location, menu, prices, flavors, waiting time... But right now you don't need these details, you only need to know "I can eat there."

**3. Points are temporary** When intention changes, the point disappears. When you're no longer hungry, the point `spicy hot pot*` recedes back into the background.

---

## Simultaneous Manifestation of Multiple Points

Usually, one intention doesn't activate just one point.

You're hungry—spicy hot pot, braised chicken rice, noodle shop might all "pop" out simultaneously. This is the simultaneous manifestation of three points:

- `Spicy hot pot*`
- `Braised chicken rice*`
- `Noodle shop*`

They all satisfy the intention "can eat," so they're all activated.

But you won't go to three places at once. You need to choose. This raises the next question: **How do you make judgments between multiple points?**

This is what we'll discuss in the next chapter: Line Structure.

## You've Always Been Doing This

Reading this far, you might think this is all very ordinary—yes, it is very ordinary.

You experience the formation and disappearance of point structures every day:

- Wake up in the morning, remember today's meeting, `meeting*` manifests
- Open your phone, want to check news, `trending topics*` manifest
- See a comment, want to refute it, `counterargument*` manifests
- Encounter a problem at work, want to find help, `Colleague A*` manifests

You don't need to learn how to form point structures—you've always been doing it. What we're doing is simply **seeing this process clearly**.

Once you see it clearly, many confusions dissolve:

- Why do different people see completely different things in the same situation
- Why do your judgments change as intentions change
- Why is communication so difficult—because you're activating different points
- Why do misunderstandings arise so easily—because points are compressed, details are lost

These aren't theories, these are facts happening every day. We've simply given it a name: Point Structure.
