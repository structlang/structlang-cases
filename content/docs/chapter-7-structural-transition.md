Looking back at the fake dog refund incident, several moments stand out:

The moment I discovered the sign vulnerability outside the shop, the entire situation's nature changed.

The moment I threatened to file a complaint after the official's perfunctory mediation, his attitude instantly flipped.

The moment the official called the seller the second time, the seller went from stonewalling to immediately refunding.

**These were all instantaneous changes**. Not gradual transitions, but "snap"—the entire situation became different.

This is **Leap**.

---

## Leap Is Not Trend

First, we need to distinguish two easily confused things.

**Trend**:

You're working out, getting stronger day by day. This is trend—gradual change, can be marked with an arrow:

```
Your strength ↑ (increasing)
```

**Leap**:

You're pulling a rubber band, tighter and tighter, suddenly—snap! It breaks.

This isn't "the rubber band slowly breaking," but instant rupture at a critical point. This is leap.

Using symbols to represent:

```
Rubber band (intact) ⇒ Rubber band (broken)
```

**Key difference**:

- Trend adjusts within the same structure (rubber band getting tighter, but still a rubber band)
- Leap means the structure itself changed (from "one rubber band" to "two pieces of rubber band")

In the fake dog refund incident:

**Example of trend**:

```
Seller's anxiety ↑ (my wife argued every day, he got more annoyed)
```

This is quantitative accumulation, structure unchanged.

**Example of leap**:

```
Official: (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)
```

From shirking to proactive—this is qualitative mutation, the entire state flipped.

## How Leaps Happen

In the fake dog refund incident, there were several key leaps. Let's examine them one by one.

### First Leap: My Judgment of the Seller

**Scene**: I discovered outside the shop that the sign didn't have "live pets" or "canines."

**Before leap**:

```
Seller = Dishonest (moral issue)
```

I positioned the seller on the negative end of the "integrity" axis. Though very negative, the nature was "moral issue."

**After leap**:

```
Seller = Illegal operation (legal issue)
```

Not just that severity deepened—more importantly, **the evaluation axis changed**:

```
Old axis: Dishonest(-) ←→ Honest(+)
New axis: Illegal(-) ←→ Legal(+)
```

From "moral axis" leaped to "legal axis."

**Why is this a leap?**

Because it's not "seller became more dishonest" (trend), but my **evaluation framework** for the seller completely changed (leap).

Originally I used business ethics standards, now I'm using legal compliance standards. These are two different structures.

**More importantly, my origin leaped**:

```
Old origin: Business integrity
New origin: Legal compliance
```

The legal origin's weight far exceeds business integrity. This means I now stood at a more powerful position.

**What triggered this leap?**

New information—the sign's vulnerability.

This information always existed (the sign was always there), but only when I noticed it did it manifest and trigger the leap.

---

### Second Leap: Official's Attitude Reversal

**Scene**: I threatened to post a complaint on the "Message to Mayor" section.

**Six-dimensional structure before leap**:

```
Me × Official: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-)
- Me: Continuously advancing, have evidence, firm attitude
- Them: Perfunctory mediation, has authority, doesn't want to really handle
```

**Six-dimensional structure after leap**:

```
Me × Official: (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)
- Me: State unchanged
- Them: Actively handles, uses authority, takes seriously
```

This is complete reversal of object's three dimensions: (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)

**Why is this a leap?**

Because it's not "official gradually became proactive," but at the trigger point of "official complaint channel," instantly flipped from one extreme to another.

Like a light switch: doesn't slowly brighten, but "snap"—it's on.

**What triggered this leap?**

External threat—a new, higher-weight authority structure entered their cognition.

In their view, "Message to Mayor" meant:

- This is formal government work procedure
- Posts almost all get responses
- Superior will hold accountable (they can't resist)
- Their previous perfunctory behavior becomes evidence

In a situation with **extremely short time window** and **incomplete information**, they could only conclude: **This threat is real.**

They had no time to verify:

- Will this person really post?
- If posted, will it really be accepted?
- If accepted, how severe are consequences?

They could only judge based on current information, then immediately change strategy.

---

### Third Leap: Seller's Compromise

**Scene**: Seller received the official's second call.

**Six-dimensional structure before leap**:

```
Official × Seller: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-)
- Official: Now serious, has authority, tough attitude
- Seller: Won't refund, has capability, tough attitude
```

**Six-dimensional structure after leap**:

```
Official × Seller: (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)
- Official: State unchanged
- Seller: Immediately refunds, has capability, cooperative
```

Again, complete reversal of object's three dimensions: (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)

**What triggered this leap?**

Chain reaction—the official's leap triggered the seller's leap.

In the seller's view:

- The official's second call had completely different tone
- The regulatory department is for real this time
- If don't refund, might face more serious consequences
- Tough attitude is useless now

The seller must immediately cut losses.

## Four Types of Trigger Factors

Now we see clearly: **Leaps don't happen randomly—there's always some factor that breaks the original balance**.

This factor is the **Trigger Factor**.

### Type 1: New Information

**Definition**: You obtained information you didn't know before, changing your judgment.

**Fake dog case**:

```
@Discovering sign vulnerability
→ New information: Seller operates without qualification
→ Leap: My origin from "business integrity" to "legal compliance"
```

**Other examples**:

- You think colleague has average capability, suddenly discover they're an expert in a field
- You think project is progressing smoothly, see report and learn it's seriously delayed
- You believe a theory, read new research that overturns it

**Why triggers leap?**

Because cognition is constructed based on manifested information. Once new information enters, old structure may not accommodate it and must reorganize.

---

### Type 2: Other Party Changes

**Definition**: The other party's action, capability, or attitude changed in ways you didn't expect.

**Fake dog case**:

```
@Official's perfunctory mediation
→ Other party exposed: Made symbolic call then said "other party doesn't agree"
→ Confirmed: They have capability to handle, just don't want to
→ This reinforced my judgment, prompted me to use stronger measures
```

Though this wasn't a leap, this type of "other party change" triggers leaps in other scenarios.

**Other examples**:

- You thought your boss supported you, suddenly they oppose your proposal in a meeting
- You thought friend was reliable, they disappeared at critical moment
- You thought opponent was weak, they suddenly showed powerful strength

**Why triggers leap?**

Because subject-object structure is six-dimensional dynamic balance. Any change in one of object's dimensions breaks original balance, causing entire relationship structure to reorganize.

---

### Type 3: External Environment

**Definition**: A new, higher-weight structure enters the situation.

**Fake dog case**:

```
@Official complaint channel threat
→ External authority: Represents superior accountability
→ Leap: Official (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)
```

**Other examples**:

- You and colleague arguing, boss intervenes
- Company has serious infighting, suddenly has external competitor
- You were casually maintaining health, medical checkup finds problems

**Why triggers leap?**

Because the original subject-object game was in a closed system. External authority's intervention breaks the closure, forcibly changes weight distribution.

**Key**: External authority doesn't necessarily really exist.

In the fake dog case, official complaint didn't physically happen yet (I didn't really post), but in the official's cognition, it manifested as real threat, producing real force.

**Manifestation is existence.**

---

### Type 4: Internal Accumulation

**Definition**: Long-term experience, intuition, or beliefs suddenly drive decision-making at a moment.

**Fake dog case**:

```
@Throughout entire incident
→ My continuous persistence
→ Based on: Legal origin anchoring + Structural intuition
→ Result: Created conditions for other trigger factors
```

This trigger factor isn't as obvious as the first three, but equally important.

**Why could I maintain (+,+,+) state throughout?**

Because I had two internal accumulations:

1. **Legal origin anchoring**: From the start, I was clear—this isn't a business dispute, but legal issue. This origin has highest weight, so I wouldn't easily give up.
    
2. **Structural intuition**: I believed deadlock will always be broken. As long as I continue pressuring, the other party will always show vulnerabilities.
    

This internal accumulation didn't suddenly form in one moment, but is long-term experience's sedimentation. But at critical moments it **triggers your judgment and action**.

**Other examples**:

- You've been hesitating about quitting, one day suddenly make up your mind
- You repeatedly struggled with a problem, suddenly figured it out
- You've long prepared a skill, suddenly burst out at a moment

## Time Window: Stable Period Between Leaps

Now we know what leaps are and how they happen.

But leaps aren't continuous. Between leaps, there are **relatively stable time periods**.

This is **Time Window**.

### Time Division in Fake Dog Case

```
[T0-T1: Day 1] - Wife hits wall
Wife(+,+,+) | Seller(-,+,-)
Seller's attitude tough, wife can't push through
Structure stable: Deadlock

@T1: I intervene and discover sign vulnerability (Leap)
My origin leaps from "business integrity" to "legal compliance"

[T1-T2: Evening Day 2 to Morning Day 3] - Finding regulatory department
Me(+,+,+) | Seller(-,+,-)
Seller still resisting, I'm preparing new strategy
Structure slightly adjusts but no leap

@T2: Find official (New role enters)

[T2-T3: Day 3 morning] - Official shirks
Me(+,+,+) | Official(-,+,-)
Official perfunctory mediation, doesn't want to really handle
Structure stable: Both sides at impasse

@T3: Official complaint channel threat (Leap)
Official: (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)

[T3-T4: Day 3 afternoon to Day 4] - Chain reaction
Official(+,+,+) | Seller(-,+,-)
Official seriously pressures, seller starts wavering
This window is very short

@T4: Official's second call (Leap)
Seller: (-,+,-) ⇒ (+,+,+)

[T4-End: Day 4] - New stability
Incident ends, seller refunds
New structure forms
```

### Characteristics of Time Windows

**1. Has boundaries**

Each window has clear start point (after last leap) and end point (before next leap).

**2. Relatively stable internally**

Within the window, structure might have small fluctuations (like seller's attitude slightly softening), but no leap occurs.

**3. Variable length**

Some windows are longer ([T0-T1] nearly one day), some windows are short ([T3-T4] might be only half day).

**4. Tension accumulating**

Though structure is stable, tension might be continuously accumulating until reaching critical point.

## Tension: The Night Before Leap

Imagine you're blowing up a balloon.

At first, the balloon inflates easily. This is **trend phase**—balloon is growing, but can still withstand.

Continue blowing, balloon gets tighter and tighter. This is **tension accumulation phase**—balloon hasn't burst yet, but getting closer to limit.

Blow one more breath—pop! Balloon explodes. This is **leap moment**—structure can't maintain, instantly collapses.

**Tension = Gap between current structure and intention**

In fake dog case:

```
My intention: Get scammed money back
Current structure: Seller refuses refund
Tension = Gap between intention and reality
```

**Tension accumulation curve**:

```
Tension
 ↑
 │              
 │            ╱ @T3-T4 (Eruption)
 │          ╱
 │        ╱ @T2-T3 (Acceleration)
 │      ╱
 │    ╱ @T1-T2 (Slow)
 │  ╱
 ├────────────→ Time
T0           T4
```

**T0-T1**: Wife hits wall, tension starts accumulating

**T1-T2**: I master legal evidence, tension slowly rises

**T2-T3**: Official shirks, tension accelerates rise

**T3-T4**: After official's attitude leap pressures, tension sharply rises

**T4**: Seller reaches critical point, instantly releases

## Why Some Leaps Can Be Predicted, Some Cannot

After seeing the fake dog case, you might ask:

**Since leaps have trigger factors, can they be predicted in advance?**

Answer: **Partially yes, partially no**.

### Predictable Situations

**When tension is obviously accumulating**:

For example, from T3 to T4, the official already changed attitude and started pressuring, the seller's resistance was obviously wavering. At this point you can feel "they're about to give in," leap is about to happen.

**When you control the trigger factor**:

For example, I held the card of "official complaint channel," I knew when playing this card would trigger leap.

### Unpredictable Situations

**Other party's unexpected behavior**:

If the official cooperated proactively at first contact, I wouldn't need to use the official complaint measure. But I couldn't know in advance whether they would cooperate.

**External environment's sudden intervention**:

Like you and colleague arguing, suddenly boss walks by and hears. This external trigger is completely coincidental.

**Internal accumulation's sudden eruption**:

Like you've been hesitating about quitting, one day suddenly make up your mind. This "one day" is hard to determine in advance.

## What You Can and Cannot Do

Understanding leap mechanisms, what can you do?

### What You Can Do

**1. Identify which time window you're currently in**

- Is it deadlock period? Tilting period? Or about to leap?
- This determines your strategy

**2. Create trigger factors**

- I proactively discovered sign vulnerability (information trigger)
- I proactively threatened official complaint (external trigger)
- I continuously persisted without giving up (internal trigger)

**3. Judge tension level**

- Is the other party close to collapse?
- Will pressuring now be counterproductive?
- Do I need to wait for timing?

**4. Prepare multiple contingencies**

- What if other party doesn't compromise?
- What if official also doesn't care?
- Each contingency corresponds to a possible leap direction

### What You Cannot Do

**1. Precisely predict leap moment**

You can only know "soon," but can't know "37 minutes left."

**2. Control all trigger factors**

Other party's sudden cooperation, external coincidental events—these aren't in your control.

**3. Force structural change**

If tension hasn't accumulated enough, no matter how you pressure it's useless. Like balloon hasn't been blown to limit, pinching it won't make it pop.

**4. Avoid all uncertainty**

Leap's essence is **making decisions in uncertainty**. You can never completely eliminate uncertainty.
