## Introduction: Starting from an Intuition

When you hear the term "meme coin," what instantly flashes through your mind?

Scam, shilling, empty promises, collapse to zero—these words emerge almost without thought.

This isn't reasoning or analysis, but some kind of "automatic activation."

**But where does this activation come from?**

In the previous chapter, we understood the nature of abstraction:

```
Abstraction is "selectively compressing information within a specific framework"
Concepts are "consensual compression points"
Not "discovery of objective essence"
```

**But this raises deeper questions:**

- Where does the compressed information go? Stored in concepts like "containers"?
- How is information "extracted" during decompression? Like taking things out of a box?
- Why do different people decompress the same concept into completely different content?

If we honestly face these questions, we'll discover:

**The traditional "container" model is wrong.**

Cognition is not information storage and retrieval. Concepts are not boxes containing attributes. Understanding is not copying files from memory.

**So what is the real mechanism of cognition?**

This chapter will reveal: **Cognition is network activation and propagation.**

- Concepts/structures = Nodes in the network
- Content = Connection patterns of nodes
- Understanding = Activation propagating along connections
- Intention = Also a node in experience
- Learning = Establishing connections + adjusting weights

When we see this network nature clearly, many confusions dissolve:

- Why the same words are understood completely differently by different people
- Why some people "just can't change"
- Why experts judge so quickly
- Why persuasion often fails

**More importantly, this is the underlying explanation for all mechanisms discussed in the previous 26 chapters.**

Compression-decompression, experience formation, intention-driven, structural leaps—these mechanisms we've discussed for 26 chapters all have network activation as their foundation.

Now, let's dive into the network nature of cognition.

## Part One: Concepts Are Not Containers, But Nodes

### Traditional Misconception

We're used to imagining concepts as containers:

```
"Dog" = {
  Attribute 1: Four legs
  Attribute 2: Barks
  Attribute 3: Bites people
  Attribute 4: Has fur
  ...
}
```

As if the concept of "dog" "contains" these attributes, which we take out when needed.

But this model can't explain:

**Why do different people "decompress" the same concept into completely different content?**

- A biologist thinks: Canidae, genes, evolutionary relationships
- A child thinks: Four legs, barks, can play with
- Some cultures think: Ingredient, cooking methods

Same "dog," why does decompression yield completely different content?

### Real Mechanism

**Concepts are not containers, but nodes in a cognitive network.**

```
Connection network of "dog" node:

Dog ──(weight 0.95)→ Four legs
Dog ──(weight 0.90)→ Barking sound
Dog ──(weight 0.40)→ Might bite (low frequency, but high emotional weight)
Dog ──(weight 0.30)→ Loyalty
Dog ──(weight 0.08)→ Food (extremely low weight in most cultures)
Dog ──(weight 0.00)→ Corrosiveness (never co-occurred)
Dog ──(weight 0.00)→ Strategic weapon (never co-occurred)
```

**The so-called "content" is not in the node itself, but in the connection pattern.**

Different people have the same "dog" node connected to different elements, with different weight distributions:

```
Biologist's "dog" node:
Dog ──(weight 0.95)→ Canidae classification
Dog ──(weight 0.90)→ Genetic characteristics
Dog ──(weight 0.70)→ Domestication history

Child's "dog" node:
Dog ──(weight 0.95)→ Four legs
Dog ──(weight 0.90)→ Barks
Dog ──(weight 0.80)→ Can play with

Certain culture's "dog" node:
Dog ──(weight 0.70)→ Ingredient
Dog ──(weight 0.60)→ Cooking methods
```

**When the "dog" node is activated, activation propagates along high-weight connections.**

This is why different people "think of" different content—not because they extracted different things from the same container, but because activation propagated along different connection networks.

### Where Connections Come From

**High-weight connections = High-frequency co-occurrence.**

```
See dog × See four legs → Co-occurred 100 times → Weight 0.95
See dog × Hear barking → Co-occurred 80 times → Weight 0.90
See dog × Being bitten experience → Co-occurred 5 times (but memorable) → Weight 0.40
See dog × Think of corrosiveness → Never co-occurred → Weight 0.00
```

**Your experience determines your connection network.**

Someone who grew up in a dog-eating culture may have "dog×ingredient" connection weight of 0.70. Someone who grew up in a pet culture may have this connection weight of 0.05.

It's not that they have different "definitions" of "dog," but that in their experience networks, the connection weight distributions differ.

### Structures Are Also Nodes

The same mechanism applies to structures.

```
Connection network of (-+-) node:

(-+-) ──(weight 0.85)→ "Deadlock" (meta-cognition)
(-+-) ──(weight 0.75)→ "Pressure breakthrough" (strategy)
(-+-) ──(weight 0.60)→ "Merchant stalling" (context)
(-+-) ──(weight 0.55)→ "Bureaucratic delay" (context)
(-+-) ──(weight 0.25)→ "Employee passive" (context)
```

When you see (-+-) in a business scenario, you instantly "know":

- This is a deadlock
- Needs pressure
- Other party is stalling

This isn't reasoning, but **node activation propagating along high-weight connections**.

And where do these connections come from? **From your high-frequency experience in business scenarios.**

```
Encounter (-+-) structure × Recognize as deadlock → High-frequency co-occurrence → Weight 0.85
Encounter (-+-) structure × Pressure effective → Medium-high frequency validation → Weight 0.75
Encounter (-+-) structure × Merchant stalling context → High-frequency co-occurrence → Weight 0.60
```

**So when you hear "meme coin," instantly thinking of scam, shilling, collapse to zero, the essence is:**

```
"Meme coin" node's high-frequency co-occurrence network:

Meme coin ──(weight 0.80)→ Scam structure
Meme coin ──(weight 0.72)→ Shilling tactics
Meme coin ──(weight 0.60)→ Collapse ending
Meme coin ──(weight 0.08)→ Serious development
```

This isn't bias, but in your experience network, these connections' weights are actually like this.

If you have 25 meme coin experiences, 20 of which encountered scams, then this connection's weight is 0.80.

## Part Two: Three Dimensions of Experience Weight

Weights in cognitive networks are not singular, but multi-dimensional.

### Dimension One: State Weight (Confidence in Static Judgment)

```
"This merchant is (-+-)"
→ Confidence in this judgment itself: 0.8

Based on:
- Observed 5 dimensions of evidence
- 4 clear, 1 ambiguous
- So overall judgment weight 0.8
```

This is the most basic weight—**confidence in a state annotation.**

### Dimension Two: Path Weight (Reliability of Causal Chain)

This dimension is most critical, yet most easily overlooked.

```
(-+-)【Pressure】→ (++-)【Partial cooperation】→ (+++)【Success】

This path's weight in different scenarios:
- Business rights protection scenario: 0.70 (15 experiences, 11 successes)
- Bureaucratic system scenario: 0.45 (8 experiences, 4 successes)
- Intimate relationship scenario: 0.20 (10 experiences, 2 successes)
```

**Path weight records the success rate of "causal transformation."**

Same starting point (-+-), same endpoint (+++), but different paths have different weights:

```
Path A: (-+-)【Forced pressure】→ (+++)
Weight: 0.60 (fast but may leave aftereffects)

Path B: (-+-)【Change incentives】→ (-++)【Attitude shift】→ (+++)
Weight: 0.40 (slow but more stable)

Path C: (-+-)【Wait for timing】→ External change → (+++)
Weight: 0.15 (passive, uncontrollable)
```

**When making decisions, the brain doesn't "calculate" the optimal path, but activates the highest-weight path.**

This is why facing the same (-+-), different people respond completely differently:

```
Person A's experience network:
(-+-)【Direct confrontation】→ (++) 
Weight: 0.80 (20 experiences, 16 successes)
→ So A habitually chooses confrontation

Person B's experience network:
(-+-)【Direct confrontation】→ (--)【Relationship breakdown】
Weight: 0.70 (10 experiences, 7 failures)

(-+-)【Indirect communication】→ (-++)→ (+++)
Weight: 0.60 (5 experiences, 3 effective)
→ So B tends toward indirect communication
```

**Same starting point, completely different path choices, stemming from path weight differences.**

### Dimension Three: Co-occurrence Weight (Associative Network)

```
Meme coin × Scam structure → Co-occurrence frequency 20/25 → Weight 0.80
Meme coin × Shilling tactics → Co-occurrence frequency 18/25 → Weight 0.72
Meme coin × Collapse ending → Co-occurrence frequency 15/25 → Weight 0.60
Meme coin × Serious development → Co-occurrence frequency 2/25 → Weight 0.08
```

**Co-occurrence weight isn't necessarily causal, but it constitutes the "associative network."**

When you hear "meme coin," your brain doesn't reason "meme coin→scam," but:

```
"Meme coin" node is activated
  ↓ (propagates along high-weight connections)
Automatically lights up "scam structure," "shilling tactics," "collapse" nodes
```

This is why it's "instantly thinking of" rather than "analyzing and concluding."

### Synergy of Three-Dimensional Weights

In real cognitive networks, three types of weights work synergistically:

```
Situation: Encounter a (-+-) merchant

State weight:
"This merchant is (-+-)" → Weight 0.8 (confidence)

Co-occurrence weight:
(-+-) × "Stalling" cognition → Weight 0.85 (high-frequency association)
(-+-) × "Merchant" context → Weight 0.90 (high-frequency co-occurrence)

Path weight:
(-+-)【Pressure】→ (++) → Weight 0.70 (business scenario success rate)

→ Three weights combined, activate "pressure on this merchant" decision
```

## Part Three: Intention Is Also a Node in Experience

Up to here, the traditional framework is:

```
Intention (given starting point)
  ↓
Drives manifestation
  ↓
Forms structure
```

**But this has a fundamental problem: Where does intention come from?**

### Intention Is Not the Starting Point, But Activated by Experience

The real mechanism is:

```
Context
  ↓ (experience network activation)
Intention
  ↓ (intention drives)
Manifestation
  ↓
Structure
```

**Intention itself is a node in the cognitive network, establishing connections with context through experience.**

### Merchant's Intention Network

```
Context: Customer complaint

Possible activated intentions:
  ├─ Intention A: "Honestly resolve"
  │   └─ Experience weight: -0.3
  │       ├─ High cost (refund + time)
  │       ├─ Hassle (need to explain, apologize)
  │       └─ Low benefit (customer won't appreciate anyway)
  │
  ├─ Intention B: "Stall and delay"
  │   └─ Experience weight: +0.9
  │       ├─ Low cost (few words to dismiss)
  │       ├─ High success rate (90% customers give up)
  │       └─ High benefit (don't need to refund)
  │
  └─ Intention C: "Negotiate compromise"
      └─ Experience weight: +0.4
          ├─ Medium cost
          ├─ Medium success rate
          └─ Medium benefit

→ Automatically activates highest-weight Intention B: "Stall and delay"
```

**Key: The merchant doesn't "decide" to stall, but the moment they see the complaint, the "stall" intention is automatically activated.**

This is because in their experience network:

```
"Customer complaint" node ──(weight 0.9)→ "Stall" intention node
```

### Recursive Formation of Intention

Intention itself is also formed from deeper-level experience:

```
Merchant's "stall" intention formation process:

Initially (first encounter with complaint):
Context: Customer complaint
↓ (no experience, try multiple intentions)
Intention A: Honestly resolve → Result: Lost money, hassle
Intention B: Stall → Result: Customer gave up
↓ (form initial weight)
"Stall" intention weight +0.3

Subsequently (multiple experience accumulation):
Complaint → Stall → Success (weight +0.1)
Complaint → Stall → Success (weight +0.1)
... (20 similar experiences)
Complaint → Stall → Success (weight accumulated to +0.9)

Now:
Complaint appears → Automatically activates "stall" intention
No need to think, no need to choose
This is "instinctive reaction"
```

**So "intention" is not innate, but cognitive shortcut shaped by experience.**

### Same Context, Different Intentions

This explains why facing the same context, different people react completely differently:

```
Context: Was scammed out of 2000 yuan

Person A's intention network:
Scammed ──(weight 0.9)→ "Forget it" intention
  └─ Experience: Rights protection too troublesome, waste of time

Person B's intention network:
Scammed ──(weight 0.8)→ "Rights protection" intention
  └─ Experience: Rights protection usually succeeds

Person C's intention network:
Scammed ──(weight 0.6)→ "Revenge" intention
  └─ Experience: Can't be bullied, must fight back
```

**Same context, activates completely different intentions, leading to completely different behaviors.**

This isn't "choice," but **automatic activation of experience network.**

### Intention Leap Mechanism

In the fake dog case, the supervisor's intention leap:

```
Initial state:
"Complaint" context ──(weight 0.85)→ "Stall" intention

I said "have something to tell the mayor":
New context signal: "Superior accountability risk"

Supervisor's another high-weight experience:
"Superior accountability" ──(weight 0.95)→ "Immediately handle" intention

→ Intention leap occurs
From "stall" intention leap to "immediately handle" intention
```

**The leap didn't change the structure itself, but activated a different intention, and different intentions drove different structures.**

## Part Four: Complete Causal Impression Chain

Now we can see the complete picture:

**What's recorded in experience is not just "structure→result," but:**

```
Complete causal impression unit:
Context → Intention → Action/Structure → Result
```

### Merchant's Complete Experience Unit

```
Experience unit 1 (high weight):
Complaint context → Stall intention → (-+-) stalling attitude → Customer gives up
→ Result evaluation: +0.9 (low cost, high benefit)
→ Weight reinforcement

Experience unit 2 (low weight):
Complaint context → Honest resolution intention → (++-)cooperate refund → Lose money
→ Result evaluation: -0.3 (high cost, low benefit)
→ Weight suppression

→ After 20 similar experiences:
"Complaint" context ──(weight 0.9)→ "Stall" intention (automatic activation)
```

**This complete chain is the true "experience."**

### My Complete Experience Unit

```
Experience unit (high weight):
Being bullied context → Rights protection intention → (+++)pressure action → Problem solved
→ Result evaluation: +0.8 (acceptable cost, good result)
→ Weight reinforcement

→ After multiple similar experiences:
"Being bullied" context ──(weight 0.8)→ "Rights protection" intention (automatic activation)
```

**So in the fake dog case:**

```
My reaction:
Scammed context → Automatically activates "rights protection" intention → (+++)full push

Merchant's reaction:
Complaint context → Automatically activates "stall" intention → (-+-)passive response

Collision:
Two different experience networks interacting
Each running along their own high-weight paths
```

### Tree-Structured Path Memory

What's recorded in experience networks is not a single path, but tree-structured possibilities:

```
(-+-) starting point

├─【Pressure】(path weight 0.7)
│   ├─→ (++-)【Partial cooperation】(0.6)
│   │   └─→ (+++)【Success】(0.8)
│   └─→ (--)【Confrontation】(0.3)
│       └─→ Deadlock escalation
│
├─【Change incentives】(path weight 0.5)
│   └─→ (-++)【Attitude improvement】(0.7)
│       └─→ (+++)【Success】(0.9)
│
└─【Wait】(path weight 0.3)
    └─→ No change or natural resolution (0.2)
```

**When making decisions, the brain activates the entire tree network, but preferentially propagates along high-weight paths.**

## Part Five: Theoretical Breakthrough Significance

### One: Rethinking "Understanding"

**Understanding is not information extraction, but network activation.**

```
Traditional notion:
Understanding = Extract information from memory

Real mechanism:
Understanding = Activate nodes, propagate along connections, form activation pattern
```

When you "understand" that meme coin is a scam, you're not "extracting" the information "meme coin=scam" from memory, but:

```
"Meme coin" node is activated
  ↓ (propagates along high-weight connections)
"Scam structure" node is activated
"Shilling tactics" node is activated
"Collapse" node is activated
  ↓
Forms an activation pattern
This pattern is your "understanding"
```

### Two: Rethinking "Intuition"

**Intuition is not mysterious inspiration, but rapid activation of high-weight paths.**

```
Novice:
See (-+-) → Need to think "What is this? What should I do?"
→ Few activation paths, low weight, slow propagation

Expert:
See (-+-) → Instantly "know" it's deadlock, "know" to apply pressure
→ Many activation paths, high weight, fast propagation
```

Expert's "intuition" is:

```
(-+-) ──(weight 0.85)→ "Deadlock"
(-+-) ──(weight 0.75)→ "Pressure" strategy
(-+-) ──(weight 0.60)→ "Business scenario"
```

These high-weight connections activate simultaneously, forming rapid judgment.

### Three: Rethinking "Learning"

**Learning is not storing knowledge, but establishing connections + adjusting weights.**

```
Hear "(-+-) requires pressure" (knowledge type)
  ↓
Just establishes a weak connection:
(-+-) ──(weight 0.1)→ 【Pressure】strategy

Try pressure, succeeded (validation 1 time)
  ↓
Weight increases:
(-+-) ──(weight 0.3)→ 【Pressure】strategy

Multiple validations (10 times, 7 successes)
  ↓
Weight stabilizes:
(-+-) ──(weight 0.7)→ 【Pressure】strategy

Now this path becomes your "intuition"
```

**So the difference between expert and novice:**

```
Expert's network:
├─ More dense connections (1000 links)
├─ More precise weights (based on 100 experiences)
└─ Richer paths (20 strategies)

Novice's network:
├─ Sparse connections (50 links)
├─ Imprecise weights (based on 1-2 experiences)
└─ Single paths (2-3 strategies)
```

### Four: Rethinking "Transfer Difficulty"

**Transfer is not copying knowledge, but rebuilding network.**

```
In business scenario:
(-+-) ──(weight 0.9)→ "Stalling" context
(-+-) ──(weight 0.8)→ "Pressure" strategy
(-+-) ──(weight 0.7)→ "70% success rate"

Want to transfer to education scenario:
(-+-) ──(weight ?)→ "Student passive" context
(-+-) ──(weight ?)→ "Motivational guidance" strategy
(-+-) ──(weight ?)→ "Success rate ?"
```

You can't directly copy business scenario weights, because:

- "Pressure" might be 0.8 in business, only 0.2 in education
- "Change incentives" might be 0.5 in business, 0.9 in education

**Transfer = Establish new connections in new domain + Re-validate weights.**

### Five: Rethinking "Changing Behavior"

**Changing behavior is not persuasion, but changing activation pattern.**

```
Ineffective method:
Tell merchant "you should be honest"
→ Try to change values
→ Usually ineffective

Why ineffective? Because:
"Complaint" context ──(weight 0.9)→ "Stall" intention
This high-weight connection still exists

Effective method:
Change context, activate different intention:
"Complaint" + "Regulatory intervention" 
  ↓
Activate "superior accountability" node
  ↓
"Superior accountability" ──(weight 0.95)→ "Immediately handle" intention
```

The fake dog case's success lay in: I changed the context, activating the supervisor's another higher-weight path.

### Six: Rethinking "Concepts and Structures"

**Concepts/structures don't just compress state, but also compress intention.**

```
"Dog" node's bound intentions:
├─ "Identify animal" intention → Activates "four legs, barking"
├─ "Assess danger" intention → Activates "might bite"
└─ "Consider pet" intention → Activates "loyal, companionship"

(-+-) node's bound intentions:
├─ "Break deadlock" intention → Activates "pressure" strategy
├─ "Understand" intention → Activates "deadlock" cognition
└─ "Predict" intention → Activates "likely delay" judgment
```

**When decompressing, different intentions activate different content.**

This explains why the same concept/structure, different people, different contexts "understand" completely differently.

## Epilogue: From Linear to Network

**Traditional cognitive theory is linear:**

```
Input → Processing → Output
Stimulus → Response
Problem → Thinking → Answer
```

**Real cognition is networked:**

```
Context activates nodes
  ↓
Propagates along high-weight connections
  ↓
Forms activation pattern
  ↓
Activation pattern generates behavior
  ↓
Behavior produces result
  ↓
Result feedback adjusts weights
  ↓
Weight adjustment changes future activation
```

Cognition is not computer-style information processing, but **dynamic activation and self-organization of biological networks.**

**Intention is not the starting point, but a node in the network.** **Concepts are not containers, but connection patterns.** **Understanding is not extraction, but activation propagation.** **Learning is not storage, but connection establishment.** **Intuition is not inspiration, but high-weight rapid activation.** **Change is not persuasion, but reactivating paths.**

## From Network to Transfer

Understanding the network nature of cognition, we can now re-examine a core question:

**How does experience transfer from one context to another?**

Chapter 26 told us: Abstraction is selective compression within specific frameworks. This chapter tells us: Cognition is network activation and propagation.

**So what is transfer?**

Transfer is not "copying knowledge," but "rebuilding network."

```
In business scenario, your (-+-) node:
  ├─ Connects "stalling" context (weight 0.9)
  ├─ Connects "pressure" strategy (weight 0.8)
  └─ Connects "success" result (weight 0.7)

Want to transfer to education scenario:
  ├─ Need to re-establish connections
  ├─ Need to re-validate weights
  └─ "Pressure" 0.8 in business, might only be 0.2 in education
```

**The difficulty of transfer is fundamentally the difficulty of network reconstruction.**

But when can transfer occur? When can't it? What kinds of experience transfer more easily? What network structures have higher transferability?

These questions require a systematic framework to answer.

**Next chapter, we will establish a complete theory of transferability:**

- From normativity boundaries (morality, legality)
- To structural isomorphism (relationship nature, resource types)
- To framework compatibility (language, value systems)
- Then to specific operational judgments

This is a complete nine-layer judgment framework.

More importantly, we will use network perspective to re-understand:

- Why _The Art of War_ can transfer from warfare to business
- Why genocide experience cannot transfer to any modern context
- Why fake dog case strategies can inspire other rights protection scenarios

**Transfer is not mechanical application, but creative network reconstruction.**

This is the theme of Chapter Twenty-Eight.
