You need to hire someone.

During the interview, this candidate performs well:

**Capability dimension:**

```
Low capability(-) ←—— 0 ——→ High capability(+)
                              ↑
                            +0.8
```

Strong capability, solid technical skills.

**Attitude dimension:**

```
Bad attitude(-) ←—— 0 ——→ Good attitude(+)
                              ↑
                            +0.9
```

Good attitude too, showing strong work motivation.

On the "capability × attitude" surface structure, they're (++), looking quite ideal.

You hire them.

---

## The Problem Three Months Later

Three months later, you notice something's wrong.

This person:

- Talks eloquently in meetings (attitude +)
- Technical knowledge is indeed solid (capability +)
- But task progress is zero (action -)

They come to work on time every day, attend all meetings, speak actively, but just don't do things. Or rather, **don't actually advance work**.

You look back at your interview judgment:

```
     High capability(+)
        ↑
        |
        |  (++)
        |  They're here
--------+--------→ Good attitude(+)
        |
```

Right, capability + attitude +, indeed (++).

**But you missed the third dimension: Action.**

---

## The Third Dimension

If we add the "action" dimension:

```
Intention: Evaluate whether this person is suitable for hiring

Three dimensions:
- Action: Whether actually advancing work
- Quality/Capability: Whether has capability to complete work
- aTtitude: Whether has willingness to work
```

Now, you re-evaluate this person:

- Action: - (not advancing work)
- Quality: + (solid technical skills)
- aTtitude: + (shows enthusiasm)

**Their true state is (-,+,+)**, not (+,+,+).

This is **three-dimensional structure**.

---

## Three Axes Intersecting

Putting three axes together forms a three-dimensional space. Corresponding to point structure, line structure, and surface structure, we can also call this "**body structure**" or "**volume structure**":

```
        Action(+)
          ↑
          |
          |
       O  +————→ Quality(+)
         /
        /
       ↙
   aTtitude(+)
```

Three axes extend from the same origin O in three directions.

**What is origin O?**

Still the same intention: "Evaluate whether this person is suitable for hiring."

The origins of the three axes **must coincide**, which means:

- All three dimensions serve the same intention
- Not three independent judgments
- But one three-dimensional combined judgment

If the origin of the third axis isn't here (say you suddenly consider "are they attractive"), that axis cannot intersect meaningfully with the first two to form a three-dimensional structure.

---

## Eight Regions

Three axes intersecting create **eight regions**.

Why eight?

Because each dimension has two states (+ or -), the combination of three dimensions is: 2×2×2 = 8.

**Eight combinations:**

1. **(+,+,+)** - Action+, Quality+, aTtitude+
2. **(+,+,-)** - Action+, Quality+, aTtitude-
3. **(+,-,+)** - Action+, Quality-, aTtitude+
4. **(+,-,-)** - Action+, Quality-, aTtitude-
5. **(-,+,+)** - Action-, Quality+, aTtitude+ ← That candidate is here
6. **(-,+,-)** - Action-, Quality+, aTtitude-
7. **(-,-,+)** - Action-, Quality-, aTtitude+
8. **(-,-,-)** - Action-, Quality-, aTtitude-

That candidate falls in the 5th region: **(-,+,+)**.

Has capability, has willingness, but no action.

---

## Why These Three Dimensions?

You might ask: Why choose "Action, Quality, aTtitude" as these three dimensions?

Because under the intention "evaluate whether someone is suitable for hiring," these three dimensions are most critical:

- **Quality**: Can they do it
- **aTtitude**: Do they want to do it
- **Action**: Are they doing it

Three dimensions answer three different questions.

But note: **Dimensions aren't limited to these three.**

If your intention is "evaluate an investment project," the three dimensions might be:

- Return: Is it profitable
- Risk: Is it safe
- Timing: Is now the right time

If your intention is "choose a restaurant," the three dimensions might be:

- Taste: Is it delicious
- Price: Is it expensive
- Distance: Is it far

**The key to three-dimensional structure isn't which specific three dimensions to use, but: Under the same intention, simultaneously consider three related judgment dimensions. Dimensions cannot completely overlap, or the structure collapses; but they must be related, or they cannot jointly serve the same intention.**

In subsequent chapters of this book, we'll extensively use these three dimensions: "Action (A), Quality (Q), aTtitude (T)."

Why choose these three?

Because they have strong universality when **evaluating subject behavior**:

- Action: Whether doing
- Quality: Whether capable of doing
- aTtitude: Whether wanting to do

These three dimensions:

- Relatively independent (don't completely overlap)
- Highly related (jointly determine subject performance)
- Broadly applicable (apply to most subject evaluation scenarios)

**But this isn't the only choice.**

Under other intentions, the three dimensions might be completely different:

- Evaluating investment projects: Return, Risk, Timing
- Choosing restaurants: Taste, Price, Distance
- Evaluating cities: Opportunity, Environment, Cost

**Remember: Dimensions are determined by intention, not fixed templates.**

A/Q/T is just a set of dimensions validated as effective in the most common scenario of "evaluating subjects."

## Formation of Judgment: From Sequential to Holistic

The formation of three-dimensional structure isn't instantaneous—it's a process.

**Judgment formation is sequential:**

When you interviewed that candidate, your attention actually focused sequentially:

1. They're answering questions (a form of action) → Observed "shows enthusiasm"
2. The content of their answers (capability manifestation) → Judged "solid technical skills"
3. Their expression, tone (attitude signals) → Felt "very motivated"

These three judgments didn't happen simultaneously, but formed sequentially during the several-minute interview.

**But after judgment completes, it gets compressed into a whole:**

At the interview's end, your brain doesn't separately store three independent judgments:

- "Action: +0.7"
- "Quality: +0.8"
- "aTtitude: +0.9"

Rather, they're compressed into a holistic impression:

- "This person is good"

This "good" is the comprehensive compression result of three dimensions.

**More critically: Which dimensions get compressed in?**

In the interview scenario, you might have noticed:

- Quality (because they're demonstrating)
- aTtitude (because they're expressing)

But **didn't fully notice "real action capability"** because:

- The interview scenario itself can't fully demonstrate action capability
- "Answering questions" as action isn't the same as "advancing work" as action
- Your attention was occupied by "quality" and "attitude"

So what you actually formed is an **incomplete three-dimensional structure**:

- Quality: + (clear)
- aTtitude: + (clear)
- Action: ? (vague or ignored)

Your brain automatically filled in the "action" dimension:

- "Strong capability + good attitude → action should be fine too"
- This is **automatic inference**, not real observation

Three months later, when you discover they don't advance work, the real judgment "action-" enters, and the entire three-dimensional structure becomes truly complete.

**At this point a leap occurs:**

```
During interview (incomplete):
- Quality: +
- aTtitude: +
- Action: + (inferred, not real)
→ Overall impression: (+,+,+) "This person is good"

Three months later (complete):
- Quality: + (verified)
- aTtitude: + (verified)
- Action: - (truly exposed)
→ Overall impression: (-,+,+) "This person has problems"
```

---

## Invoking Three-Dimensional Structure: Instantaneous Holistic Feeling

Although judgment formation is sequential, **structure invocation is instantaneous**.

Three months later, when you think of this person again:

- You won't sequentially recall "their action is insufficient, capability is okay, attitude is good"
- Rather, you instantaneously produce a holistic feeling: "This person isn't great, has capability but doesn't advance"

This holistic feeling is the result of invoking the compressed three-dimensional structure.

**Analogy:**

- Judgment formation = Taking a photo (scanning pixels one by one, with sequence)
- Structure storage = The photo (compressed into a whole)
- Structure invocation = Viewing the photo (instantly seeing the complete picture)

When viewing a photo, you don't say "I look at the left first, then the right, then the middle," but rather "I see the entire picture at a glance."

But this "at a glance" complete picture is formed from previous sequential scanning compressed together.

---

## Why Are Dimensions Easily Missed?

Precisely because judgment forms sequentially, certain dimensions are easily missed:

**Limited attention capacity:**

- You can only focus on one dimension at a time
- If a dimension isn't prominent enough, it gets skipped
- During interviews, "action capability" isn't prominent enough (because it's just answering questions)

**Scenario limitations:**

- Some dimensions can't be fully demonstrated in the current scenario
- Interview scenarios can't demonstrate "long-term work advancement" action capability

**Experiential inference:**

- The brain automatically fills in missing dimensions
- "Capability + attitude + → action should also be +"
- Such inference is often unreliable

**This is why we need "complete three-dimensional structure":**

Remind yourself:

1. Which dimensions am I using to evaluate?
2. Is each dimension actually observed, or inferred?
3. Can the current scenario fully demonstrate these dimensions?

## Different Feelings of the Eight Regions

Now looking at these eight combinations, each gives a completely different feeling:

**(+,+,+)**: Full Positive State

- Doing, can do, wants to do
- Most ideal state

**(+,+,-)**: Capable but Resistant

- Doing, can do, but doesn't want to
- Possibly under pressure

**(+,-,+)**: Enthusiastic but Incapable

- Doing, can't do well, but really wants to
- Beginner or volunteer

**(+,-,-)**: Blind Action State

- Doing, can't do well, doesn't want to do well either
- Most dangerous state

**(-,+,+)**: Potential State ← That candidate

- Not doing, but can do, and wants to
- Possibly waiting for opportunity, or lacking drive

**(-,+,-)**: Observing/Waiting State

- Not doing, can do, but doesn't want to
- Deliberately not cooperating

**(-,-,+)**: Accumulating State

- Not doing, can't do, but wants to help
- Willing but unable

**(-,-,-)**: Full Negative State

- Not doing, can't do, doesn't want to either
- Completely unsuitable or no involvement

**Each combination is a "pattern," each with its specific meaning and response approach. These combinations aren't labels, but snapshots of states. Each snapshot can leap through time—it's these leaps that bring structure to life.**

## A Single Person Isn't Enough

Three-dimensional structure lets you see clearly one person's state.

But in reality, you're not just evaluating one person—you're dealing with **your relationship with them**.

Back to that candidate:

- You: (+,+,+) - Fully advancing the project
- Them: (-,+,+) - Has capability and willingness but no action

This isn't two independent evaluations, but **a relationship structure**.

Your (+,+,+) encountering their (-,+,+), what happens?

- You feel frustrated (I'm pushing hard, they're not moving)
- They might feel pressure (your expectations and their state don't match)
- The project falls into difficulty (you can't complete everything alone)

Structure doesn't stay with one person, but extends into relationships. This "subject-object structure combination" forms a **six-dimensional relationship space**:

```
You (Action+, Quality+, aTtitude+) × Them (Action-, Quality+, aTtitude+)
= 3 dimensions + 3 dimensions = 6 dimensions
```

"You: (+,+,+) × Them: (-,+,+) = 6-dimensional relationship." This is the next level: Subject-Object Structure.
