## From 64 Patterns to Abstraction

In the previous chapter, we established the 64 Patterns: the complete map of subject-object relations.

```
8 subject states × 8 object states = 64 possible configurations

Each configuration has its specific meta-dynamics
We can identify situations, predict trends, find leap directions
```

**But we discovered an astonishing phenomenon**:

The same situational configuration (e.g., subject +++ × object -+-) exhibits similar meta-dynamics in completely different domains:

- Fake dog return: I push full force, merchant has resources but shirks
- Project advancement: Project manager pushes full force, department has data but doesn't cooperate
- Bureaucratic obstruction: Applicant has complete materials, officer has authority but obstructs

**Why is this?**

Going further:

- Why can the symbol "-+-" be recognized across multiple situations?
- Why is "has capability but doesn't cooperate" a transferable pattern?
- Why can Sun Tzu's "know yourself and know your enemy" transfer from warfare to business?

**Traditional answer**: "Because we abstract away specific details and preserve the common essence"

**But this answer hides the key questions**:

- What is "essence"? Who decides what's essential?
- What is "common"? Common to whom?
- What exactly is the process of "abstraction"?

This chapter will reveal: **Abstraction is not "discovering essence," but "selective compression"**.

Understanding this, we can understand:

- Why 64 Patterns can be applied across domains
- Why it also has limitations
- Why it's not "objective structure" but "analytical framework"

## Opening: A Seemingly Simple Question

**Question: What is a "dog"?**

This seems like a foolish question. Everyone knows what a dog is—four legs, barks, domesticated by humans.

But going deeper:

**Case: "Dog" Through Different Eyes**

```
Biologist sees "dog":
- Canidae family
- Genetic sequence characteristics
- Evolutionary relationship with wolves
- Specific breed classifications

Child sees "dog":
- Four legs
- Barks
- Can play with
- Some bite

Farmer sees "dog":
- Tool for guarding property
- Cost of feeding
- Ability to chase wild animals

Person from certain cultures sees "dog":
- Food ingredient
- Specific cooking methods
- Nutritional value
```

**Same "dog"—why do different people see it completely differently?**

Traditional answers might be:

- "Because of different focus"
- "Because of different knowledge backgrounds"
- "Because of cultural differences"

**But these answers don't touch the essence. The real question is:**

> **"What is this concept 'dog' itself? Does it exist objectively or is it constructed by us?"**

## Section One: Concepts Are Not "Discovered," They're "Compressed"

### 1.1 Collapse of Traditional Views

**Traditional view:**

```
An essence exists in reality
↓
This essence is called "dogness"
↓
We discover this essence through observation
↓
Name it "dog"
```

This view assumes:

- Essence exists independent of observers
- Concepts are "discovery" and "naming" of essence
- All people's understanding of "dog" should be the same

**But reality is:**

```
Physical reality: Countless specific animals
↓
Each unique, each moment different
↓
Contains infinite information
↓
We cannot process infinite information
↓
Must compress
```

### 1.2 "Dog" as Compression Point

**The real process:**

```
Step 1: Physical Reality Layer
├─ Dog A: Golden Retriever, 3 years, 25kg, lively, brown
├─ Dog B: Chihuahua, 1 year, 2kg, timid, white
├─ Dog C: Husky, 5 years, 20kg, mischievous, black-white
└─ ... countless specific individuals

Step 2: Compression Operation
What to preserve? What to discard?
→ This depends on the observer's framework

Step 3: Compression Result
Biologist: Preserve genetic features, discard individual differences
  → "Dog" = Canidae

Child: Preserve visible features, discard scientific classification
  → "Dog" = Four-legged barking animal

Farmer: Preserve function, discard details
  → "Dog" = Tool for guarding property
```

**Core insight:**

> **"Dog" is not a discovered essence, but the product of compression.**

### 1.3 Compression Inevitably Loses Information

```
Complete information: 100%
├─ Each dog's uniqueness
├─ State at each moment
├─ All relevant details
└─ All possible connections

Compressed to "dog": About 1-5%
├─ Only preserve "relevant" features
├─ Discard "irrelevant" details
└─ Form operational concept

But the question is:
Who decides what's "relevant"?
Who decides what's "irrelevant"?
```

**Answer: The observer's framework.**

## Section Two: Abstraction Is Selective Compression

### 2.1 Naive View of Abstraction

**Common misunderstanding:**

```
Abstraction = Remove specific details + Preserve common essence

Example:
This dog, that dog, another dog
↓ Remove specific differences
"Dog" as abstract concept
↓ Preserves "commonality of all dogs"
```

**Problem with this understanding:**

- Who decides what's "commonality"?
- Why are four legs commonality but coat color isn't?
- So-called "commonality" already presupposes judgment standards

### 2.2 Real Process of Abstraction

**Abstraction is not "removal," but "selective preservation"**

```
From specific dog to concept "dog":

Preserved features:
✓ Four legs
✓ Barks
✓ Mammal
✓ Domesticated
✓ ...

Discarded features:
✗ Coat color
✗ Size
✗ Temperament
✗ Who owns it
✗ Current location
✗ ...

Key question:
Who decides what to preserve?
By what standards?
```

**Answer: Framework.**

### 2.3 Framework Determines Content of Abstraction

**Same object, different abstractions under different frameworks:**

```
Framework 1: Biological Classification
Preserve:
- Genetic characteristics
- Physiological structure
- Evolutionary relationships
Discard:
- Social attributes
- Economic value
Result: "Dog" = Canidae with specific genetic features

Framework 2: Economic Analysis
Preserve:
- Market value
- Feeding costs
- Economic function
Discard:
- Physiological details
- Emotional value
Result: "Dog" = Commodity with specific market value

Framework 3: Poetry Creation
Preserve:
- Loyalty
- Companionship
- Emotional symbolism
Discard:
- Biological features
- Economic value
Result: "Dog" = Symbol of loyalty

Framework 4: Recipe Writing (certain cultures)
Preserve:
- Meat characteristics
- Cooking methods
- Nutritional value
Discard:
- Emotional attributes
- Social significance
Result: "Dog" = Food ingredient
```

**Shocking discovery:**

> **There's no "objective abstraction," only "abstraction within framework".**

- **Consensus**: Overlapping understanding among multiple subjects

Not because: ❌ Everyone's understanding of "dog" is completely identical

**Key insight:**

> **Concept stability doesn't come from "objective essence," but from "size of overlap region".**

- Ignores normativity boundaries

**Correct version:**

```

Within the same normativity region,
In structurally isomorphic situations,

- Different compression rules

But cannot cross normativity:

Because normativity differences:

## Section Six: Multi-Level Compression Theory

### 6.1 Hierarchy of Compression

**Returning to core insight from Chapter 7 Supplement**:

In "Chapter 7 Supplement: Volume Structure and Decompression Diversity," we discussed:

```
Volume structure = Compression point
Same volume structure can decompress to multiple six-dimensional configurations
Decompression diversity comes from information loss
```

**Now we can see the complete picture**:

Compression in cognition isn't single-level, but multi-level recursive:

```
Original layer: Reality situation (100% information)
↓ Compression (Ch. 8: Compression inevitably loses)
First layer: Six-dimensional notation Subject(A,Q,T) × Object(A,Q,T) (about 10% information)
↓ Compression (Ch. 7 Supp.: Volume structure compression)
Second layer: Surface structure (hierarchical relation) ++/+-/-+/-- (about 3% information)
↓ Compression
Third layer: Volume structure -+- (about 1% information)
↓ Compression
Fourth layer: Meta-structure "deadlock" (about 0.3% information)
↓ Compression
Fifth layer: Meta-principle "go with the flow" (about 0.1% information)
```

**Each compression level loses information**:

- From reality to six-dimensional: Lost countless specific details
- From six-dimensional to volume structure: Lost subject-object attribution (Ch. 7 Supp. details)
- From volume structure to meta-structure: Lost dimensional information
- From meta-structure to meta-principle: Lost situational features

**But each compression level increases abstraction**:

- Higher abstraction → Wider application range (within framework)
- Lower abstraction → More specific information (but poor transferability)

**Key discovery: Same volume structure can decompress to multiple six-dimensional configurations**

Why can there be multiple?
Because:
- Volume structure dropped "subject-object attribution"
- Only preserved "overall dynamic characteristics"
- When decompressing can allocate to different subject-objects

**What does this mean?**

```
Volume structure -+- represents: "Deadlock, has potential but lacks kinetic energy"

### 6.3 Why Volume Structure Can Transfer

**Volume structure removes "who is who," only preserves "system state"**

```
Six-dimensional:
- Contains subject-object attribution information
- Specific to particular persons
- Low transferability

Volume structure:
- Removes subject-object attribution
- Only describes "system dynamics"
- High transferability

But cost is:
- Information loss
- Detail blur
- Need to re-decompress to specific situation

**This is compression-transfer tradeoff:**

```
More information retained → More specific → Harder to transfer
More information lost → More abstract → Easier to transfer

But losing too much → Loses meaning
Retaining too much → Cannot compress

Optimal point:
Retain "pattern," lose "individual information"

## Section Eight: Epistemological Stance

### 8.1 From Realism to Constructivism

**Three stances:**

```
Extreme realism:
"Structure objectively exists in reality"
"We discover and describe it"
Problem: Ignores role of framework

Extreme relativism:
"Everything is subjective"
"No right or wrong, construct freely"
Problem: Denies reality's constraints

Moderate constructivism:
"Structure is constructed by us with framework"
"But construction is constrained by reality"
"Some constructions more useful than others"
```

**We adopt moderate constructivism:**

```
Acknowledge:
1. Reality exists independent of observers
2. But what we know is "phenomenon" not "thing-in-itself"
3. Phenomenon is product of framework-reality interaction
4. Different frameworks produce different phenomena
5. Objectivity possible within framework (inter-subjective consensus)
6. But no absolute objectivity across frameworks
```

### 8.2 Pragmatic Standard of Truth

**How to verify theory?**

```
Not through:
"Conforms to objective reality" (cannot verify)

But through:
"Useful in practice" (can verify)

Standards of usefulness:
├─ Helps understand complex situations
├─ Helps predict behavior
├─ Helps formulate strategies
├─ Helps solve problems
└─ Can communicate within community

If theory satisfies these:
→ It's "good theory"
→ Regardless of whether "absolutely true"
```

**Analogy to Newtonian mechanics:**

```
Newtonian mechanics not "absolute truth"
(Fails at near light speed)

But very useful in certain range:
├─ Architectural engineering
├─ Mechanical design
├─ Daily physics
└─ Aerospace (low-speed part)

→ This is good theory

Our structural language same:
Don't claim "absolute truth"
Only claim "useful in specific range"
```

### 8.3 Objectivity of Consensus

**Inter-subjective objectivity:**

```
Not:
Absolute objectivity independent of all subjects

But:
Relative objectivity within subject community

Example:
"Earth orbits sun"
├─ Not personal opinion (subjective)
├─ Is scientific community consensus (inter-subjective objective)
├─ Can be falsified and revised (fallibilism)
└─ Is "best theory" under current conditions

Our structural language:
├─ Not absolute truth
├─ Is attempting to establish "inter-subjective consensus"
├─ Among people who endorse this framework
└─ Reach relatively stable understanding
```
