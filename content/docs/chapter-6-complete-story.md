My wife bought a dog.

The seller said it was a Bichon Frisé, purebred. Not cheap, but since it was adding a family member, we gritted our teeth and bought it.

Three days after bringing the dog home, the more I looked, the more something seemed wrong.

This appearance, this coat color, this build—not a Bichon Frisé at all. I researched online, compared photos, and confirmed: we'd been scammed.

This was a mixed breed, passed off as a Bichon Frisé and sold to us.

I decided to get this money back.

---

## The Complete Process

**Day 1: Wife Hits a Wall**

My wife went to the seller first.

"This dog isn't the Bichon you claimed. I want a refund."

The shop owner's attitude was very tough: "The dog's already sold, no refunds."

"You said it was a purebred Bichon."

"I said it's a Bichon, you just don't know how to judge."

"It's obviously not, the appearance is way off."

"That's your problem. No refunds anyway."

My wife was blocked at every turn, came back and told me the seller was completely unreasonable.

**Day 2: I Go to Observe**

The second day, my wife asked me to go together.

At the shop, she argued with the seller inside. I listened to a few exchanges from the side, watched how the seller spoke and their tone, and determined they definitely wouldn't refund.

I didn't join the argument, but walked outside the shop to look at the surroundings.

That's when I noticed the sign at the shop entrance.

The sign was large, listing business scope: flowers and birds, ornamental fish, aquarium equipment, pet supplies... densely packed with items.

But I discovered a fatal problem: **No "live pets" or "canines"**.

This meant they didn't have legal qualification to operate a pet dog business. This was illegal.

I took out my phone and photographed that sign.

Then I walked into the shop and told my wife: "Let's go, we'll find the department head. No need to waste words with him."

My wife was a bit confused, skeptically followed me back.

On the way I explained to her: "They don't have qualification to sell dogs, this is illegal operation. We don't need to beg them for a refund—we'll have the regulatory department handle it."

**Day 3: Seeking External Help**

I went to the relevant regulatory department and found a person in charge.

"Hello, I want to file a complaint against a business. They're operating a pet dog business without qualification and sold me counterfeit goods."

The official looked up at me, tone very impatient: "This matter isn't easy to handle."

"Why isn't it easy? They're clearly breaking the law."

"These things are complicated. Do you have evidence?"

I showed him the photo of the sign.

He looked at it, frowned: "This... let me make a phone call to help you mediate."

He called the seller in front of me, said a few words, tone very bland, like going through motions.

After hanging up, he told me: "The other party doesn't agree to refund, you two negotiate."

"What do you mean negotiate? They're operating illegally, you're not handling it?"

The official probably felt I was being too aggressive, blurted out: "This whole industry is like this."

As soon as he said this, I immediately caught it. This wasn't just shirking responsibility—it was admitting the fact of "industry-wide violations."

I could see he just wanted to brush this off and make me compromise.

I said directly: "If you won't handle it, I'll file a complaint with the mayor."

The official froze: "What?"

"Our official website has a 'Message to Mayor' section where you can post to report problems. I know that's part of government work, posts basically all get responses. I'll post about this there, including this business's illegal operation, and your regulatory department's inaction."

The official's expression changed.

He stared at me for a few seconds, probably judging whether I'd really do this.

After a pause, he said: "Wait a moment, I'll make another call."

This time his tone was completely different—very serious, even somewhat forceful.

After hanging up, he told me: "Alright, he agrees to refund."

I didn't wait for the next day. Hearing this, I immediately went to the seller's shop.

**That Afternoon: Getting the Money Back**

I went directly to the seller's shop.

The shop owner's attitude did a 180-degree turn. Though his expression wasn't great, he still refunded the money.

The entire incident, from discovering the scam to getting the money back, took three days.

## Using This Story to View Structure

Now, let's use this real story to see how all the previously discussed concepts operate.

### Point Structure: Intention Determines Manifestation

The moment I discovered the scam, an intention crystallized in my mind: **Get the scammed money back**.

Once this intention was established, the entire world reorganized:

**Manifested points**:

- Dog* - Counterfeit product
- Seller* - Fraud perpetrator
- Money* - Loss that needs recovery
- Evidence* - Weapon that needs finding (the sign)
- Regulatory department* - Power that can be leveraged
- Official complaint channel* - Ultimate deterrent

Everything else receded into the background: whether the dog is cute, what kind of person the seller is, whether this would be troublesome—none of that mattered.

**What mattered was one thing only: Get the money back.**

If my intention had been "forget it, consider it a lesson learned," then the business scope on the sign, the official's brushoff, the official complaint channel—none of these would have entered my cognition.

**Intention determines what gets seen.**

### Line Structure: Multi-Dimensional Judgment

When I first went to the seller's shop, I formed judgments about the seller:

```
Seller = Dishonest (very negative)
Seller = Has refund capability (positive)
Seller = Tough attitude (very negative)
```

Three line structures, three dimensions of judgment. Combined, they told me: **This seller can refund but won't, and their attitude is extremely tough.**

Looking at just one line alone, I might have thought "forget it, not worth the trouble for this money."

But looking at all three lines together, what I saw was: **They have the capability to refund, they're just stonewalling.**

This changed the entire situation.

When finding the regulatory official, my judgment of them:

```
Official = Has regulatory authority (positive)
Official = Doesn't want to really handle it (negative)
Official = Brushing off responsibility (very negative)
```

Another "has capability but won't act" structure.

**I discovered a pattern: The seller's and official's strategies were actually the same—both have capability but won't cooperate.**

This wasn't coincidence. This is causal impression at work: in their past experience, they discovered that the best way to deal with "troublesome customers" is to "shirk, delay, make them give up."

This strategy works in most situations.

But I wouldn't give up.

### Surface Structure: Multi-Dimensional Combination

When deciding whether to continue pursuing, my surface structure:

```
Benefit: Can recover several thousand yuan (+0.7)
Risk: Might waste time and energy (-0.3)

Benefit × Risk = 0.7 × 0.3 = 0.21
```

Though benefit wasn't particularly high, risk was very low (at most wasting time), combined it was worth continuing.

If the risk were "might face retaliation" (-0.8), then benefit × risk would become negative, and I might have given up.

**Surface structure let me see: It's not a single dimension that determines action, but multi-dimensional combination.**

### Body Structure: Three-Dimensional State

Throughout the process:

**My state: Always (+,+,+)**

- Action: Continuously advancing
- Quality: Mastered evidence and strategy
- aTtitude: Firmly demanding refund

**Seller's state: From (-,+,-) to (+,+,+)**

- Initially: Won't refund, has capability, tough attitude
- Finally: Cooperates with refund, has capability, though reluctant but cooperative

**Official's state: From (-,+,-) to (+,+,+)**

- Initially: Doesn't want to handle, has authority, shirking responsibility
- Finally: Actively pressures, uses authority, handles seriously

**Three-dimensional state let me see: Not simply "good person" or "bad person," but specific performance under specific intention.**

### Subject-Object Structure: Six-Dimensional Game

**Me × Seller (First Phase): (+,+,+) | (-,+,-)**

```
My three dimensions: (+,+,+)  Continuously advancing, have evidence, firm
Their three dimensions: (-,+,-)  Won't refund, has capability, tough attitude
```

This was a deadlock. I couldn't push through, they wouldn't yield.

**Me × Official (First Contact): (+,+,+) | (-,+,-)**

```
My three dimensions: (+,+,+)  Complaining and pressuring, have evidence, firm attitude
Their three dimensions: (-,+,-)  Perfunctory mediation, has authority, doesn't want to really handle
```

Another deadlock.

**But the official leaped under my pressure:**

**Me × Official: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)**

```
Before leap: They(-,+,-)  Perfunctory mediation, has authority, doesn't want to really handle
After leap: They(+,+,+)  Actively pressures, uses authority, handles seriously
```

**Official × Seller (After Official Intervention): (+,+,+) | (-,+,-)**

```
Official: (+,+,+) - Actively pressures, has authority, tough attitude
Seller: (-,+,-) - Wanted to resist, has capability, but tough attitude now useless
```

The seller also leaped:

**Official × Seller: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)**

```
Before leap: Seller(-,+,-)  Won't refund, has capability, tough attitude
After leap: Seller(+,+,+)  Cooperates with refund, has capability, though reluctant but cooperative
```

**Subject-object structure let me see: This isn't three independent people, but three sets of dynamic games.**

### Leap: Sudden Structural Change

Several key leaps occurred throughout the story:

**First Leap: Discovering the Sign**

My judgment of the seller leaped:

```
Old judgment: Seller = Dishonest (moral issue)
New judgment: Seller = Illegal operation (legal issue)
```

Not just that values changed, but **the nature changed**.

More importantly, my **origin** leaped:

```
Old origin: Business integrity
New origin: Legal compliance
```

The legal origin's weight far exceeds business integrity. This means I now stood at a more powerful position.

**Second Leap: Official's Perfunctory Mediation**

The official made a symbolic phone call, then said "the other party doesn't agree, you negotiate."

This action exposed their strategy: **Go through motions, make me give up.**

But this instead let me confirm: **They have capability to handle it, just don't want to.**

This reinforced my judgment, making me decide to use stronger measures.

**Third Leap: Threat of Official Complaint Channel**

When I mentioned "Message to Mayor," the official's state leaped:

**Me × Official: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)**

```
Before leap: (-,+,-) - Perfunctory mediation, has authority, doesn't want to really handle
After leap: (+,+,+) - Actively handles, uses authority, takes seriously
```

**Why the leap?**

Because in their view, "Message to Mayor" meant:

- This is formal government work procedure
- Posts almost all get responses
- If complained about there, it's formal superior accountability
- My previous perfunctory behavior becomes evidence

In a situation with **extremely short time window** and **incomplete information**, they could only conclude: **This threat is real.**

They had no time to verify:

- Will this person really post?
- If posted, will it really be accepted?
- If accepted, how severe are the consequences?

They could only judge based on current information:

- This person seems very serious (already persisted several days, even found evidence)
- This person knows the rules (knows official complaint channel)
- This threat might come true

**So they must immediately change strategy.**

**Fourth Leap: Seller's Compromise**

After receiving the official's second call, the seller immediately leaped:

**Official × Seller: (+,+,+) | (-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)**

```
Before leap: (-,+,-) - Won't refund, has capability, tough attitude
After leap: (+,+,+) - Immediately refunds, has capability, cooperative
```

Because in their view, the official's second call had a completely different tone, which meant:

- The regulatory department is for real this time
- If don't refund, might face more serious consequences
- Tough attitude is useless now

**Leap let me see: Structure isn't linear evolution, but sudden reversal. The key is finding the factor that triggers the leap.**

### Time Window: Stable Period of Each Stage

```
[T0-T1: Day 1] - Wife hits wall stage
Wife(+,+,+) | Seller(-,+,-)
Seller's attitude tough, wife can't push through

[T1-T2: Day 2] - Discovering vulnerability
I discover sign vulnerability outside shop
Origin leaps: From business integrity to legal compliance

[T2-T3: Day 3 morning] - Seeking help
Me(+,+,+) | Official(-,+,-)
Official perfunctory mediation, slips "this whole industry is like this"

[T3-T4: Day 3 afternoon] - Official's attitude leaps
Me(+,+,+) | Official(-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)
Threat of official complaint channel takes effect

[T4-T5: Day 3 evening] - Seller compromises
Official(+,+,+) | Seller(-,+,-) → (+,+,+) | (+,+,+)
I immediately go to shop, seller refunds on the spot
```

Within each time window, structure is relatively stable.

But with the appearance of trigger factors (discovering evidence, perfunctory behavior exposed, threat escalated), structure suddenly leaps, entering the next time window.

**Time window let me see: The game doesn't advance at uniform speed, but follows a rhythm of "stable-leap-stable-leap."**

## What Does This Story Tell Us?

**First, intention determines manifestation.**

If my intention had been "forget it, consider it a lesson learned," then the seller's qualification vulnerability, the official's perfunctory behavior, the official complaint channel—none of these would have entered my cognition.

**Second, structure can be identified.**

Both the seller and official had (-,+,-) structure of "has capability but won't cooperate," both used shirking strategy. Once you identify this pattern, you can find response methods.

**Third, leaps can be guided.**

The official's attitude leap, the seller's position leap, didn't happen by chance—I actively created them through trigger factors (evidence, perfunctory behavior exposed, threat escalated).

**Fourth, time windows and incomplete information can be leveraged.**

Precisely because the other party had no time to verify and no complete information, the "virtual pressure" I created (official complaint) could produce real force.

**Fifth, choice of origin determines the game's altitude.**

By initially anchoring the "law" origin with highest weight, I established the tone of the entire game. If I'd anchored "business integrity" or "personal favor," the result might be completely different.
